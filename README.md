# SnowRaven

Self-hosted birding tools and data explorer for your eBird workflow: a standalone Mac or Windows app, or hosted on a Raspberry Pi (or any computer on your network).

SnowRaven turns your own eBird and Macaulay Library exports into a personal birding dashboard: weather for your checklists, deep per-species history, life-list analytics, media-coverage tracking, breeding-code history, and an interactive map, all running on your own device, with your own data.

**See it in action:** the [SnowRaven website](https://snowraven.dtgibson.com/) walks through every feature with screenshots.

## What it does

- **Weather Lookup**: paste an eBird checklist ID and get a formatted historical weather summary (temperature, wind, humidity, dew point, sunrise/sunset, and the moon phase on night checklists) ready to paste into your checklist comment. The same lookup also shows the historical **tide** from the nearest NOAA station (observed or predicted, with the surrounding high/low tides), and can copy weather and tide together. You can also look up weather and tide **directly**, without a checklist: **Current** for where you are right now, or **Predict** for a place and time you choose (a name search or a map pin), with forecast weather up to about eight days out, plus the predicted tide, which reaches much further ahead. A **weather backlog** at the bottom of the tab lists your most-recent checklists that still have no weather block (built offline from your backup, complete/non-incidental by default with a toggle to widen), each with links to open the checklist or its comment page and a **Copy weather & go** action that copies that checklist's weather and jumps you to its eBird edit page to paste.
- **Species Detail**: your complete history with any species: sighting stats, breeding codes, co-occurring species, field notes, top locations (public eBird hotspots link to their hotspot page, a convention shared across the app wherever a location name appears), a map of every observation, and embedded Macaulay Library media. If you prefer not to load inline players, Settings can replace them with a short disabled note while keeping dates, checklists, and direct media links.
- **Statistics**: a multi-section analytics dashboard: life-list totals and growth, top species, firsts and milestones, temporal and geographic patterns, effort and outings, data quality, highlights and records, breeding stats, deep media stats (documentation coverage, age/sex and behavior breakdowns, and time-of-day), and the playful **Frivolous Lists**, themed bird-name collections you complete from your own life list. Countable totals still exclude true interspecies hybrids, while an intergrade named inside a trailing parenthetical counts as its parent species. A second count rule, **Count escapees** (off by default), makes the Species figure follow eBird's own life-list rule: Naturalized and Provisional exotics count, Escapee does not. SnowRaven works this out by asking eBird about the smallest set of your checklists that covers every species you have recorded (73 of them on a 21,000-observation export), caching the answer for 30 days, and never removing a species until every checklist carrying it has been checked. It names every bird it leaves out, and those birds stay on your Life List. Both count rules are per-session, resetting on relaunch.
- **Calendar**: a year of your birding as twelve month grids, like a wall calendar's twelve pages, with a number on each day: the **species** you saw, the **checklists** you submitted, or the **total count** of individual birds you recorded (your toggle), each day shaded green by how busy it was relative to the year on screen so the shape of your birding year reads at a glance. Page across every year your backup covers (gap years skipped), or fold **all years** into one combined grid (species become a distinct-species union; checklists and total count are sums; February keeps its Feb 29 cell). In the all-years view the weekday columns align to the current year, matching this year's single-year layout. A searchable **Species** filter narrows the whole calendar to one species (type to find it by common or scientific name; every cell, the tiers, the legend, and the popup redraw for it; subspecies/forms fold into the parent), a **Compact | Large** view toggle swaps the big month grids (count-only, no date) for a 3×4 grid of shaded mini-month thumbnails that each carry a small day-of-month date (the number tucks away when a thumbnail is too small; clicking any day in a thumbnail opens the same day popup right there without switching views; the month cards themselves don't navigate: the toggle switches views, and both views work at every screen size, including on a phone), an optional colorblind-friendly **Use Textures** mode encodes each day's level as crosshatch density instead of color, and a low-emphasis switch (off by default) can count spuh/slash/hybrid forms toward the species and total-count numbers. Click any day for a popup with all three counts and a list of that day's checklists, each with its start time, location, and species count (following the spuh/slash/hybrid toggle), and a link straight to eBird. Entirely offline: it reads only your already-loaded backup.
- **Map Explorer**: an interactive map of your sightings (with heatmap), nearby eBird hotspots colored by whether you've visited them, media targets, an optional California Breeding Bird Atlas overlay, and optional **county lines** that draw US county boundaries over the current view (kept crisp and accurate at every zoom level by drawing them straight from the map's own tiles) and can shade each county by how many species (or checklists) you've recorded there, a county-by-county read of your own coverage drawn entirely from your loaded eBird backup, with a legend, a click-through popup per county, an optional colorblind-friendly **Use Textures** mode that encodes each county's level as crosshatch density instead of color, and a keyboard-accessible "counties in view" list (those two metrics add no network calls of their own). A third **Completeness** metric shades each county by how complete your county list is (your countable species there measured against everything ever reported to eBird for that county, on a fixed 0–100% scale), with a per-county popup showing a progress bar ("X of Y species (Z%)"), your newest county species (from your own backup, works offline), and a short chase list of targets you haven't recorded there yet; never-birded counties stay plain outlines with a one-click on-demand lookup. Unlike the other two metrics, Completeness needs a connection and your own eBird API key (it says so at the point of use); lookups are bounded to the birded counties in view and cached on-device for 30 days, so fetched counties keep shading offline. Only one of the atlas or county shadings is active at a time (their ramps competed for the same map), and while a shading ramp is on the basemap's land mutes to grey so the ramp stands out. A **Point Size** control (Normal / Small / Off) lets you shrink or hide the sighting points in Pins mode so a shaded breeding-code or county map reads through underneath. Hiding them removes their click target too, and it composes with the automatic fade. A **Nearby Lifers** section maps *where* species you've never recorded were reported recently near a chosen point: each spot is a labeled pin (the species, or "{n} species" where several were reported together) colored by how recently it was seen, and clicking it shows the lifers with dates and eBird checklist links. Nearby Lifers and Media Targets each carry a **locator dot** at every marker's exact coordinate plus a **Labels | Dots** marker-style toggle that collapses the chips to just those dots for a clean overview of where the birds are (the pins stay clickable). Pick a point with your location, a place-name search, a radius, or by dropping a pin (right-click, or long-press on touch) and dragging it to fine-tune, and filter by a **Time Range** (last day / week / 30 days). The map's bottom-right corner carries the same row of three round buttons on every view that shows a map: share, a **location button** that centers the map on where you are without opening the filters panel, and fullscreen. (My Sightings shows setup instructions rather than a map until your eBird backup is loaded.) If location is off or denied, the reason and the fix appear on the map rather than vanishing into a corner. Switchable street / satellite / topo basemaps plus a hiking-trails overlay.
- **Share a location from any map**: right-click (or long-press on touch) anywhere on a birding map to plant a share pin at that exact spot, drag it to fine-tune, and copy a share-ready block: the coordinates in decimal degrees, plus a Google Maps and an Apple Maps link that open the spot for whoever you send it to. Paste it into a text message and the links are tappable; the coordinate line on its own pastes straight into any maps app's search box. Settings has an independent switch for each of the three lines, so you can send any combination: coordinates only, one map link only, coordinates plus one link, or nothing at all, in which case the popup still shows the coordinates on screen and simply offers no copy. Every map with a pin also has a round flag button in its corner, matching the flag it plants, that drops the pin at the center of the view, so the whole flow works from the keyboard with no pointer gesture. On Map Explorer's Hotspots, Nearby Lifers, and Media Targets views the existing search-center pin is clickable and offers the same copy, and the corner carries a matching **map pin button** that opens that same popup (bringing the center back into view first if it has drifted off screen), so every view has the tool in the same place and no second pin is ever created. The pin is transient (Esc clears it, nothing is written to disk) and the whole thing is assembled locally as plain text, so it works offline and no coordinate ever leaves your device.
- **Multimedia**: your life list with photo/audio/video coverage per species, with **sex and age filters** (pull up just your juveniles, or the males of a sexually dimorphic species) and links straight to the matching Macaulay Library media, to find what you still need to capture. An **Unbounded** toggle expands the table to its full width so it scrolls with the page instead of inside a fixed-width scroll box, and a **Pin column labels** toggle keeps the row of column headings visible at the top of the screen as you scroll down the list, so you can always tell which count a number is (off by default, per-session, resetting on relaunch; pinning uses the Unbounded view). It is the same control, and the same behavior, as Pin code labels on the Breeding Codes tab.
- **Breeding Codes**: every species you've recorded a breeding code for, as a color-coded matrix with a column for each code you've recorded, filterable by tier or by individual code. An **Unbounded** toggle drops the table's horizontal scroll constraint so the full row is visible at once instead of scrolling within a fixed width, and a **Pin code labels** toggle keeps the row of code headings visible at the top of the screen as you scroll down a long species list, so you can always tell which column a circle sits in (off by default, per-session, resetting on relaunch; pinning uses the Unbounded view). On a phone, in both the normal and Unbounded views, the columns tighten to dot-width so more fit at once, with thin vertical column rules and a species-name column that stays fixed as you scroll the codes sideways in the normal view, and you can pinch to zoom. Long filter meanings wrap inside their pills, and a long bird name can wrap above its eBird and Birds of the World links. Every label remains complete, both links remain available with their full touch targets, and nothing escapes the name column. The table scrolls as part of the page, with the tier legend after the last species; in the normal view the legend's longer code meanings wrap onto a second line at phone widths so they stay inside the card, still spelled out in full.
- **Named Birds**: track individual birds you've named in your eBird species comments with a `[name:…]` tag (e.g. `[name:Winky]`): each individual's species, first/last-seen (with a small line noting how long you've followed it: the elapsed span, e.g. "2 yrs. 3 mos."), sighting count, and every checklist it appears on (with its location and a map of where that bird has been seen). If your ML export is saved too, each individual also gathers *its own* photos, audio, and video as inline Macaulay Library players, matched by `[name:…]` tag, with an asset's own caption or media note taking precedence *when it carries such a tag* and the checklist's species comment as the fallback otherwise (so tagging once in the species comment attributes that observation's media, including assets whose captions carry no tag of their own, while adding a `[name:…]` tag to an individual asset overrides it), each labeled with its date and checklist, loaded on demand and degrading to a link-out when offline or while the Cornell Lab's bot check is blocking embedded players. Sort by name, alphabetical, taxonomic, or last seen. Also surfaces per-species on Species Detail.
- **Checklists**: your checklists as whole outings: search every checklist comment and every species comment you've ever written (last 10 shown, expandable, with eBird links), and browse a filterable list of all your checklists: combine has/doesn't-have filters for comments, media (by type with the ML export), breeding codes, weather/tide blocks, complete/incomplete, protocol, county, and date range. A toggle (off by default) hides pasted SnowRaven weather/tide blocks from display *and* search.
- **List Comparer**: compare two life lists (your backup vs. another birder's) to see shared and unique species, or compare two individual eBird checklists by ID/URL with side-by-side counts (higher-count emphasis), breeding codes, photo/audio/video indicators, effort details (type, distance, duration, observers, app), and checklist + species comments (with a side-by-side comments table). Each checklist card also shows at-a-glance badges (media, breeding, and whether the comment already has a weather/tide block), and a **Weather & Tide** section can pull a fresh weather and tide reading for each checklist to compare conditions side by side (on demand, with per-side copy buttons; nothing is copied automatically).

- **Offline support**: SnowRaven keeps working on your own data when the network drops. Every analytical tab and every map opens offline once it has loaded online at least once, since it runs from your already-loaded, locally-stored eBird and Macaulay Library data. Maps mount offline and draw your own data layers (sightings, heatmap, atlas blocks, county overlay) with the vector base's labels; bird names sort in proper taxonomic order with their small site icons even on a first-ever cold start with no connection (from a bundled eBird taxonomy snapshot, instead of a plain A–Z fallback); and a checklist's weather or tide reading you loaded online once re-shows when you reopen it offline, clearly marked as the last loaded result with the time it was loaded. When a genuinely live feature can't run, you get a clear, distinct message ("you're offline" vs. "no API key configured" vs. "server error") rather than one confusing failure, and the update check no longer reports "up to date" when it couldn't actually reach the update server. *(Some features are fundamentally live and stay online-only: live weather lookups, live tide readings, place/address search, the List Comparer, live nearby-bird overlays, and downloading an app update; offline you get the last loaded result where one exists, otherwise the honest "you're offline" message. Only the vector **Map** base works offline; Satellite / Topo / Trails need a connection and are disabled while offline.)*

- **Settings**: your API keys and data files, a default location and search radius, and appearance controls: light/dark theme, text size up to 200%, date format, and an off-by-default **Disable embedded media** switch. Turning it on prevents inline Macaulay Library players from loading in Species Detail and Named Birds and shows “Embedded media is disabled in Settings.” instead; direct media links and locally derived media information remain available. A **Sharing** section chooses what a map pin copies, as three independent switches (coordinates, Google Maps link, Apple Maps link, all on by default), with a live example of the exact text and a sentence naming the lines it will contain. You can also reorder or hide tabs, and every preference persists across launches.

Full per-feature documentation lives in **[docs/HELP.md](docs/HELP.md)**, and in-app from the **Help** link in the footer of every tab. New users get a first-run welcome screen on a cold start.

A companion project, [SnowRaven Mini](https://github.com/dtgibson/snowraven-mini), is a small Chrome and Firefox extension that runs the same weather and tide lookup right on the eBird checklist page. It's separate from SnowRaven and not required by it.

## Privacy

SnowRaven is local-first and **collects nothing**: no accounts, no analytics, no telemetry, and no developer-operated server. Your eBird backup, Macaulay Library export, settings, and API keys stay on your own device (or your own self-hosted machine). Network requests go directly from your device to the services the app draws from: eBird, OpenWeather, OpenStreetMap/Nominatim, and NOAA Tides & Currents with *your* API keys where keys are needed, plus the keyless map-tile hosts and the Cornell Lab sites that bird-link icons and, while embedded media is enabled, Macaulay players load from. Turn on **Disable embedded media** to prevent those player requests; ordinary media links still open the Macaulay Library when you choose them. The full provider list is in the [Privacy Policy](PRIVACY_POLICY.md); see also the [Accessibility statement](ACCESSIBILITY.md).

## Requirements

Two free API keys, entered once in the app's Settings:

- **eBird API key**: free, from [ebird.org/api/keygen](https://ebird.org/api/keygen). Powers weather lookups, hotspot and recent-sighting data, the map's hotspot features, and taxonomy/region lookups.
- **OpenWeather API key**: free, from [openweathermap.org](https://openweathermap.org). You must subscribe to the **One Call by Call** plan (free for the first 1,000 calls/day) or weather lookups return an error. Activating that plan requires a payment card on file even though the free tier is free; you can set a usage cap in your account to avoid charges.

Most analytics tabs also use your own data files (optional, but they unlock most features):

- **eBird backup** (`MyEBirdData.csv`) from [ebird.org/downloadMyData](https://ebird.org/downloadMyData): powers Species Detail, Statistics, Calendar, Map Explorer, Breeding Codes, Multimedia, Named Birds, Checklists, and List Comparer.
- **Macaulay Library export** (optional): adds media coverage and trends.

## Installation

### Mac

Download `SnowRaven_x.x.x_universal.dmg` from the [latest release](https://github.com/dtgibson/snowraven/releases/latest); one universal build runs natively on both Apple Silicon and Intel Macs. Open the DMG, drag SnowRaven to Applications, and launch it. On first launch, right-click the app and choose **Open** to clear the macOS security prompt.

### Windows

Download `SnowRaven_x.x.x_x64-setup.exe` from the [latest release](https://github.com/dtgibson/snowraven/releases/latest) and run it. The app isn't code-signed yet, so SmartScreen may warn "unknown publisher": click **More info → Run anyway**. (In-app updates are cryptographically verified regardless.)

### Raspberry Pi / Linux

Run one command on your Pi (or any Debian/Ubuntu machine):

```bash
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dtgibson/snowraven/main/install.sh | bash
```

It handles everything: system packages, Node.js, the build, the Python environment, API keys, and an optional auto-start service. Then open the app in your browser on your local network.

**Updating:** desktop apps download and install updates in place from **Check For Updates** in the footer (then relaunch); self-hosted installs are notified in-app when a new version is available and updated by running `./update.sh`.

> **About the `npm` security notice during a Pi/Linux update.** When `./update.sh` reinstalls the frontend with `npm ci`, npm audits the full dependency tree and may print a summary like "N vulnerabilities (… moderate, … high)." Those advisories are in build-only tooling (the dev server, a lint plugin) that is never part of the app that ships. A production-scoped `npm audit --omit=dev` reports zero. The app that ships is unaffected.

## Build from source

For development or building your own bundle: clone the repo, then `cd frontend && npm install && npm run dev` (with `cd backend && uvicorn main:app --reload --port 1620` for the server), or, from the repo root, `npm install && npm run desktop:dev` for the Tauri desktop app (the desktop tooling lives in the root `package.json`, so install there too). Requires Node.js, Python 3.10+, and (for the desktop build) [Rust](https://rustup.rs/).
