# Roadmap

This is a living document. It reflects the current best thinking
on what to build next — not a contract. Things change as you learn
more about your users and your product. Update it freely.

## Shipped

129 versions shipped. Last shipped: **SnowRaven 1.0.3 -- where a named bird actually hangs out, and the app's own bird becomes the raven** -- two builds shipped as one release. On the **Named Birds** tab, expanding an individual now ranks the places that bird has actually been recorded, between its reports and its map. The counts come only from the checklists carrying that bird's `[name:…]` tag and never from the species as a whole, so a hotspot where the species floods but your bird turned up once sits exactly where it belongs, and the numbers add up to the card's own sighting count. Five places show with a "Show all N locations" control beyond that; a public hotspot name links to its page on eBird and a personal location stays plain text, as everywhere else in the app. A bird you have only ever seen in one place gets a single line naming it rather than a ranking of one, and a bird whose export carries no location names shows no list at all. Species Detail's Named Individuals section is unchanged: that tab already has its own Top Locations for the whole species. Alongside it, the last two surfaces in the app carrying a generic bird now carry the brand's raven -- the app header and the first-run welcome screen had shown a thin outlined bird from an icon library, and both now show the same raven silhouette as the app icon, at the same size, in the same accent green in both themes. Nothing moves and nothing else changes; the mark stays decorative and the wordmark still carries the name. Shipped to macOS, Windows, the website, and TestFlight; the App Store submission deliberately stays at the in-review 1.0.1, with 1.0.3 to follow as an ordinary store update once Apple's queue resolves.

Previously: **SnowRaven 1.0.2 -- tier rings: the hotspot color modes become readable without color** -- the Map Explorer's "Color pins by" modes gain an opt-in **Use Tier Rings** switch, answering the feedback that arrived the day v0.5.92 shipped: the pins look great, but they are not colorblind accessible. Turn it on and every ramp pin carries a thin white ring just inside its rim, split into five segments, with the pin's tier filling that many segments clockwise from the top -- a busy hotspot closes its ring, a quiet one shows a single arc -- so a tier reads by shape at map scale the way a clock face does, and by count up close, with no reliance on telling one blue from another. The legend's little pins carry the same ring and the popup's color square becomes a round segmented badge, all three surfaces drawn from one shared geometry so they can never drift apart. It is off by default, and the choice is remembered across launches -- a deliberate first: unlike the session-only texture toggles on the county and atlas overlays, a vision-linked reading aid should not have to be switched back on every morning by exactly the person it serves. With the switch off, every pin is the shipped build bit for bit, and the hollow, dashed, pale, and personal-star pins never change either way. Shipped to macOS, Windows, the website, and TestFlight; the App Store submission deliberately stays at the in-review 1.0.1, with 1.0.2 following as a store update once Apple approves it.

Earlier: **SnowRaven 1.0.1 -- a mobile chart tip, and the escapee count repaired for domestic-form birds** -- on a phone, Statistics and Species Detail each offer a small one-time tip above their charts: charts get more room in landscape, or in the desktop app if you have one. It is a suggestion only, dismissed with one tap, saved on your device so it never returns on that tab, and tablets and desktops never see it. Alongside it, a correctness repair: a bird recorded only as a "(Domestic type)" form (a Muscovy Duck, a Swan Goose, a Graylag Goose) had silently dropped out of the escapee check, which reported zero escapees and let the species count run high -- the species-only name lookup could not resolve the form name, so the check never asked eBird about those birds, and every installation's saved answer was one cache expiry away from going wrong. Such birds now resolve to their parent species and are checked like any other, and an installation that ran the broken check heals itself on its next Statistics visit by re-asking exactly the checklists whose answers were discarded. The App Store submission was replaced in the review queue so the store debut carries the fix; the 1.0.1 submission is waiting for review and releases on approval.

Earlier: **SnowRaven 1.0.0 -- the public iOS App Store release** -- the deliberate one-time version jump from 0.5.93, shipped to every platform at once and submitted to Apple's review queue (releasing immediately on approval). The app itself is unchanged on every platform: this release is everything between "on TestFlight every version" and "any birder can find SnowRaven on the App Store" -- the committed listing record with its verified field counts, twelve demo-data screenshots at Apple's two required sizes with their own regenerable capture tool, a dedicated first-party privacy page (`snowraven.dtgibson.com/privacy.html`, parity-tested against PRIVACY_POLICY.md) that is Apple's privacy policy URL, a hosted synthetic demo dataset any reviewer or curious birder can import, and a key-free review script built on the app's own honest no-key states. The privacy label reads "Data Not Collected," and it is true. The App Store is now a standing leg of every release: the same uploaded TestFlight build is submitted after each ship, a rejection stalls only the store leg, and the availability prose (README, website, founding brief) is staged to land the day Apple approves.

Earlier: **a new app icon, and gentler eBird manners everywhere on the map (v0.5.93)** -- the SR mark gains the raven: a white serif "SR" with the raven's head worked into the S, on the same clover green the app has always used, replacing the old icon everywhere one shows. On macOS it is drawn on Apple's icon grid, so the Dock icon finally sits at the same size as every native app; Windows, iOS, the browser tab, and the SnowRaven website all carry the same mark now, where the web and the site had shown a different bird glyph that never matched the desktop icon. Alongside it, the eBird rate-limit courtesy v0.5.92 taught the Recent activity pass now covers every eBird lookup the Map Explorer makes: the hotspot search, Nearby Lifers, Media Targets, and county Completeness share one pace with the activity pass, so when eBird asks the app to slow down, a lookup waits out the same brief pause and answers instead of failing, and cached answers never wait at all.

Earlier: **color the hotspot pins by what you want to know (v0.5.92)** -- the Map Explorer's Hotspots view gains a "Color pins by" control with three opt-in modes. **My species** and **My checklists** color each hotspot by your own coverage there, computed entirely from your loaded backup with no network at all, so the map finally answers "where is my coverage thin" at a glance. **Recent activity**, the mode the original idea flagged as the point of the feature, colors each hotspot by how many species the whole eBird community has reported there in the last week or last 30 days, asked with your own key, one call per hotspot answering both windows so flipping Week and 30 days never refetches. Fetching is bounded (four at a time, at most 200 hotspots per search, on your screen first, then nearest your search center), gently paced, and polite: if eBird asks the app to slow down, the pass pauses briefly, says so in the progress line, and resumes, and a hotspot that stays rate limited through its retries lands in the honest "not checked yet" state, where Retry re-asks only what is missing. Answers are cached for six hours across sessions, and pins color one by one as they arrive. Values land on a new five-class cyan-blue ramp whose class ranges are spelled out in a legend that reveals itself when a mode turns on, and the popup and the keyboard "Hotspots in view" list state every pin's number or state in words, so nothing depends on perceiving color. Four honest non-values can never be mistaken for low values: a hollow pin means asked and zero, a dashed gray pin means not checked yet, and a pale pin means never birded by you. The default visited / unvisited / personal coloring is untouched until you opt in, byte-identical and regression-guarded, and the mode choice is per-session, resetting on relaunch. This one came straight from the idea inbox.

Earlier: **search the part of the map you are actually looking at (v0.5.91)** -- the Map Explorer's Hotspots, Nearby Lifers and Media Targets views gain a **Search this area** button on the map itself. Pan or zoom somewhere new and it appears; one press re-runs whichever of the three you are on over what is on screen, with no trip through the Filters panel and no coordinates to type. It is one button rather than three, because those views already share one search centre and only one of them is ever on screen. The centre is the middle of your view and the radius is the smallest of the existing 5, 10, 25 or 50 mile rungs that covers it, capped at 25; the radius is measured centre-to-corner, so the circle covers the whole rectangle rather than fitting inside it. The centre is adopted into the sidebar's coordinate boxes, but your Radius setting is deliberately left alone, so the circle drawn on the map is what reports the size actually used. Because a search area is a circle and a map is a rectangle, the two can never match exactly, so the ground outside the searched circle is gently dimmed with a dashed edge marking the boundary once it comes into view -- nothing dims while the circle covers what you can see, and past the cap the circle is visibly smaller than the screen, which is the whole reason it is drawn. Panning and zooming never search on their own, nothing is sent until you press, and pressing twice without moving the map costs one lookup rather than two: a press deliberately does not re-frame the map onto its own results, which is what would otherwise invite a second, wider press. Every other route to a search -- the Find button, place-name search, Use my location, a dropped pin, a view change -- now announces its outcome on the map too, which matters most on a phone where the sidebar is not on screen. On the narrowest phones at the larger text sizes the map can end up too short to hold another row of buttons, and the button stays away rather than covering a control you still need; everything it does remains reachable through Filters and Find. The release also fixes a pre-existing defect the new button made visible: the Map Explorer panel worked from a fixed guess at how much room the app's own header, tabs and footer take, and the guess was too small at every width and text size measured, so the map's round corner buttons could sit below the edge of the window until you scrolled. It now measures the space it actually has, which also covers the two cases a fixed number could never get right -- the tab strip folding into a dropdown, and the larger text sizes -- while keeping a map minimum height, so a 320 pixel phone at the largest text size still scrolls a little by design.

Earlier: **three pieces of hardening with nothing to see, plus a build-tooling fix (v0.5.90)** -- this release changes nothing you can see or do, and three of its four changes are the app enforcing exactly what it already claimed. An eBird checklist ID is the letter S followed by digits, and SnowRaven checks that shape before an ID is used anywhere; the check had no upper limit on the digits, so an absurdly long run still counted as well formed and could reach an outbound request to eBird. The backend and all six places the app makes the same check now agree on a ceiling of 15 digits, comfortably above any ID eBird has ever issued, and are asserted against each other so they cannot drift apart. The checklist route itself now refuses a malformed ID before asking eBird, the way the weather and tide routes always have -- same check, same answer, applied first -- and no request the shipped app can make is affected, because the app already checks before calling. On the desktop app, a bird literally named "constructor" can no longer confuse the taxonomy lookups: a handful of words are special to the language the lookup tables are built in, and a name from your CSV matching one of them could read a built-in function instead of a bird; every such lookup now asks only for names really in the table, and no real bird name changes its answer. The fourth change is dev-only and not in the app at all: a security advisory in the build tooling was cleared and the shipped app proven byte-identical, so release preflights report zero vulnerabilities again.

Earlier: **what counts as a species now follows eBird's own rule (v0.5.89)** -- SnowRaven used to work out whether a bird counted toward your list by looking at the shape of its name, guessing from a "sp.", a slash, or an "x". eBird already answers that question in the taxonomy SnowRaven ships with it, so it now just reads the answer. The rule in one sentence: a form that leaves the species in doubt does not count (a spuh, a slash, a hybrid, an undescribed form), and a form that only leaves the subspecies in doubt counts as its parent species. **Most totals go up.** 88 names eBird counts were being left out, folding into 59 species, and they are everyday birds: `Canada Goose (moffitti/maxima)`, `Redpoll (Common/Hoary)`, `Dark-eyed Junco (Slate-colored/cismontanus)`, `Iceland Gull (thayeri/kumlieni)`, `Red-tailed Hawk (calurus/abieticola)`. **A few go down**, because 81 names were counting that eBird does not count: three named hybrids whose names give no hint they are hybrids (`Brewster's Warbler (hybrid)`, `Lawrence's Warbler (hybrid)`, `Bogota Sunangel (hybrid)`), 25 spuhs, and 53 undescribed or unrecognized forms. Those birds stay on your Life List; only the count changes. The same number now appears everywhere, where Statistics, the Calendar, county Completeness and the Multimedia count had been applying three slightly different rules and could disagree about the same bird. The switches are renamed to match what they now do: **Count spuh, slash & hybrids** becomes **Count all forms** (Statistics and Calendar) and **Show sp./slash** becomes **Show all forms** (Multimedia and Species Detail), and the two Show switches now cover the whole set, so the rows on screen and that tab's own "X of N species" agree instead of leaving hybrids visible that the count had excluded. It all still works with no connection, and nothing new is fetched.

Earlier: **two corrected backend input checks, with nothing to see (v0.5.88)** -- this release changes nothing you can see or do, and saying so plainly is more useful than dressing it up. SnowRaven's self-hosted backend checks the shape of a value before it trusts it: the name of a settings entry, one step before that name becomes a file on disk, and a checklist id before it goes out to eBird. Each check was letting through a narrow class of value it was written to refuse. A settings name ending in a stray line break slipped past and quietly created a second settings file beside the real one, and it skipped a second safety check meant to keep certain names reserved. A checklist id written with digits from a non-Western numbering system slipped past too, which meant the backend disagreed with the equivalent check the app itself makes before sending the request. They look like one problem and are two, and fixing either would not have fixed the other. Both now refuse exactly what they always said they refused, and every settings entry and checklist id that worked before works identically.

Earlier: **an escapee-aware species count on Statistics (v0.5.87)** -- the headline species total can now match the life-list number eBird itself shows you. A new **Count escapees** checkbox, off by default, follows eBird's own rule: birds recorded as Provisional or Naturalized count toward your list, birds recorded as escapees do not. Turn it on and you get exactly the number the tab showed before, so nothing is taken away. On the reference export the default figure reads 264 rather than 267, dropping Graylag Goose, Swan Goose and Muscovy Duck, and a panel beneath the figure names them, since a total quietly falling by three with no account of itself is the failure this repo keeps rejecting. Working out which birds those are is a bounded, cached lookup rather than a sweep of your whole history: whether a bird is an escapee is recorded per sighting, but the question is per species and one checklist answers it for every bird on that checklist at once, so the app works out offline which handful of checklists it needs and asks for only those. On the reference export that is 73 lookups in about ten seconds, cached for 30 days, and a later export re-checks only what it has not already resolved. A species it could not resolve still counts, so the number only ever converges downward and never wrongly erases a bird you have seen. With no eBird key, no connection, or a failed lookup, the tab says which and shows the uncorrected number. The Calendar still opens, counts and shades with no connection at all.
