Improve · Design pass

One register for the filter pill. Actually, two.

Forty-odd controls across fifteen files draw the same idea in seven heights, six radii and four type sizes. This pass picks one of each. Reading the actual styles turned up something the count alone hid: these are two different shapes, not one — and they need two registers.

The proposal

Both registers collapse to 30px tall and 0.75rem text. Standalone pills draw their own edge; segmented groups keep taking theirs from the shell around them.

The one genuinely open question is radius — and the design system and the codebase disagree. That is section 03, and it is your call.

02

Two shapes, not one

Fifteen of the sites declare border: 'none' and get their edge from a wrapping overflow: hidden shell — they are hand-rolled segmented controls. The rest draw their own chrome and stand alone. One register cannot serve both: a segment has no radius and no outer border of its own.

Register B — .sr-seg-btn

~15 sites

They share height and type size, and differ in edge. That is what keeps the family coherent while letting each shape be itself. The two registers are deliberately not merged and deliberately not allowed to drift apart on the two values that make them read as one family.
Scope note for the Orchestrator. Register B's sites are close cousins of the existing SegControl component, which design-system.md already specifies and which the change brief puts explicitly out of scope. I have designed B as a CSS register only — matching what those sites already do visually — and have not converted them into SegControl, which would be a component and ARIA change rather than a styling one. If you would rather these 15 sites become real SegControls, that is a different build.

The decision: radius

Why the spec recommends 15px despite being outvoted 21 to 3. design-system.md specifies it; the one class-based pill in globals.css implements it; and the three newest pill sites (Checklists' tri-state filters, its clear-filter pill, and the Calendar's year toggle) all adopted it — so 15px is the direction of travel and the 6px sites are the legacy. It also draws a real distinction: buttons are radius 6, pills are radius 15, which is exactly how the design system separates an action from a filter. At 30px tall, 15px is a true pill; 6px is a rounded rectangle wearing the name.
The honest cost: 15px is the larger visible change. Twenty-one controls change shape noticeably; three stay put. If you would rather this pass be as near-invisible as possible, 6px is the defensible choice and the spec flips with one value — but it means overruling design-system.md, which should then be amended in the same change rather than left saying something the code does not do.

06

Motion: 38 controls gain a transition

Only 6 of the 44 sites declare a transition today. The other 38 snap instantly between states. Adopting a register gives all of them one 120ms ease-out fade — a small change you will feel on every filter press.

Today — instant

38 of 44 sites

Hover these — nothing eases

On the register — 120ms ease-out

All 44

Hover these — background, border and colour ease together

No transform, no scale, no entrance. These are utility controls that toggle a filter; a state change is a colour change, and animating anything more would be motion decorating a static screen. prefers-reduced-motion is handled by the app's single global block, which collapses the transition to ~1µs — the end state is the resting state, so nothing is lost and no per-component query is added.
Six sites carry an inline transition that must be removed, not left behind. An inline transition is specificity 1,0,0 and silently beats the class rule — the v1.0.18 rule the change brief names as governing. Three of the six declare transition: 'all 0.15s', which is the worst case: it would animate layout properties too. The whole inline chrome comes off at every site, not two thirds of it.

Secondary choice: the rest border

The family's rest border is --sr-border, which measures 1.27:1 against the surface. The app already mints a 3:1 control-boundary token — --sr-border-input at 3.43:1 — and uses it for form controls.

The spec proposes A: the pill is identified by its label and its state by aria-pressed plus an AA-passing text colour and a weight change, so no contrast requirement rests on the edge. That is the same reasoning design-system.md records for the sticky band's hairline, and it is what every other button in the app already does — picking B here would make 40 pills heavier than the buttons beside them. B is the stricter reading of WCAG 1.4.11. Worth a decision rather than an inherited default.

The fix changes nothing at 200%, which is the point. Above roughly 133% the rem term overtakes the floor and the two registers already agree. The repair is entirely in the band from a lowered browser default up to ~133% — and it is worst below 100%, a case the original report did not reach.

One relationship, not four constants

Eight distinct pairings, at four label sizes and six control sizes. Each label's floor is the control floor scaled by that label's ratio to its OWN control's register — not to a fixed 0.75rem, which is what makes rows 7 and 8 come out right.

The invariant this buys: a label always crosses from floor-governed to scale-governed at exactly the same text scale as the control it sits beside. Both of a label's terms carry the same ratio, so it cancels and the crossover depends only on its control's register. There is never a band where one is pinned to a floor while its neighbour tracks rem — which is precisely the mechanism that produced this defect. The crossover is per pairing, ranging from 1.14× to 1.39×; an earlier draft of this design claimed a single global 1.333× and was wrong, because it measured every ratio against 0.75rem instead of against each control's own register. Independently chosen constants would not have this property at all, and nothing would detect its loss.

An earlier version of this design preserved each pairing's inherited label-to-control ratio — eight different values across four label sizes and six control sizes. Those ratios are drift: nobody chose 0.8462. This build collapses seven pill heights to one and six radii to one; the labels deserve the same treatment. Here is what each option actually renders.

Why not simply one ratio for everything? Because the optical compensation has a reason, and the reason is specific to uppercase: capitals fill the cap height and read larger at the same metric size, which is exactly why the 1px desktop offset exists. A sentence-case field label has no such effect, so shrinking it is a loss with no gain. Option C is therefore one number — --sr-label-optical: calc(11 / 12), declared once — which uppercase labels opt into and sentence-case labels simply do not. Eight drifted ratios become one value plus a default.

You can have a constant ratio or a constant label size — not both. The control registers themselves vary six ways, and those are designed, not drift: design-system.md names the species picker's three size registers, and the command palette's 1rem is a deliberate hero input. So a label either keeps its own size and lets the ratio vary (A), or tracks its control and lets its size vary (C). The request was that headings and controls grow and shrink together — that is a statement about the pair, so the ratio is the thing that should be constant.
Open question this raises — the unpaired labels. Under C a section label beside a control renders 14.67px on a phone, while the same register on a surface with no floored control stays at 11px. That is roughly 46 label sites across ~24 files left at the old size. A build that unifies every control and then splits a label register into two sizes is not finished. The recommendation is to give the section-label register a phone floor everywhere — at the cost of visibly enlarging small labels across those files, and it would be the app's first non-control phone-tier font floor. Flagged for a decision rather than taken unilaterally.
