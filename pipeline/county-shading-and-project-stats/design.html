County Shading & ProjectsSnowRaven · design for review

Two maps that finally show counties, and a tally you have to earn.

Everything on this page is drawn from your own export: 3,252 checklists, 21,369 observations, 36 US counties, and the 251 Common Raven records behind the Species Detail map. County names, location names, shading tiers and legend breaks are computed with the shipped computeCountyTiers quantile code, so what you see is what the app would paint.

The three switches above are the real ones: theme, a 320px phone width, and 200% in-app text scale. Use them on every specimen. The last release closed four horizontal scroll leaks, so the narrow case is the one that matters most.

01 Species Detail

The Counties overlay on Sighting Locations

One switch, in the header row the Pins and Heatmap group already lives in. It is off on mount and resets on relaunch, like every control beside it. Turning it on loads the county geometry, mutes the basemap, dims the pins, and opens a shading panel beneath the map. Nothing here needs a network or a key.

The whole surface, live

Press Counties. Then try Use Textures, click a shaded county for its popup, and switch between Pins and Heatmap to confirm the shading survives the mode change. The map drawing is a stand-in for the real Positron tiles; the county outlines, the fills and every number are real.

Where the metric went. Per species, "distinct species in this county" is always 1, so there is no metric switch here (OQ-04). The metric is fixed to checklists and the legend says whose checklists they are.

The popup, adapted to one species

The shipped county popup carries two counts and swaps its list by metric. In the one-species case there is only one count worth showing, and the caption underneath is what stops "123 checklists" being read as "all my checklists here".

02 Statistics · Geographic Stats

The same overlay, one level up

Here the overlay answers "what is the shape of my coverage", so it keeps the metric choice: Species or Checklists, the Map Explorer's first two options, same values and same labels. Completeness is deliberately absent: it is the one metric that costs an eBird call per county, and it would sit inches from county tables that count species by a different rule.

The ranked pins stay. The numbered circles and squares, their popups, the share pin and the legend row beneath the map are all untouched. County shading joins them; it does not replace them. With the overlay on, the pins dim so the fills read.

03 Statistics · Projects

A number you have to pay for, priced before you press

Your backup does not record which eBird projects your checklists went to. The only source is one request per checklist, so a complete answer is 3,252 requests and about eight minutes. That single fact shapes the whole section: it is a thing you run, not a stat that appears, and it has to be honest at every moment in between.

Two rules are visible in every state below. A section that has never run shows no number at all, not a zero, because zero is a claim the app has not earned. And every tally renders with its denominator, so a partial answer reads as a floor rather than a finished count.

Run it

This specimen is live. Press Check projects to watch a compressed pass: it runs, hits one eBird cooldown, and completes. Stop and Resume work. Reset puts it back to never run.

Demo

All eleven display states

Each one carries its own denominator in the type, so a tally without its context is not expressible. The controls are exactly what that state can actually perform: offline and at-capacity offer none, because there is nothing they could do.

A project with no published name

No public eBird endpoint resolves a project id to a name. A small bundled table covers the codes and ids the app knows; anything else renders its raw identifier verbatim, in mono so it reads as an identifier rather than a name, with one line saying why. Nothing is invented and nothing is linked.

04 Both themes at once

The colour-sensitive pieces, side by side

The switch at the top moves the whole page. These three are pinned in both themes because they are where a theme mistake would actually cost something: the ramp is map-anchored and identical in both, so the legend swatches must read against two different surfaces with one set of values.
