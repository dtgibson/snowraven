# Strategic Brief: Family Rollups and Tree

Amended 2026-11-21 for the user's directions at the Stage 4 design review (`decisions.md`, D-13 to D-24). Only what those directions change is amended; the rest stands as first written.

## What We're Building
A new **Family Tree** tab, between Breeding Codes and Hotspot Reports in the default order (D-13, D-24), that arranges the birder's own life list by taxonomy, in two views over one model and one set of filters. The **rollup** view is a table of orders and families: how many of each family's species the birder has seen, and how many of those they have photographed, recorded and filmed, each family opening to its genera and species. The **tree** view draws the same order, family, genus and species hierarchy as a diagram the birder can zoom and pan, holding only their own birds, narrowed by region, year and media.

## Why Now
The user asked for both pieces together, and named the gap exactly: Birds of the World has an excellent family tree, "but it does not allow you to see a tree of only your own species, or with filters." That is the product's founding problem (the official tools do not let you explore your own data) applied to the one axis of a life list the app has never shown. Family and order appear nowhere in SnowRaven today; every other ingredient (the species set, the counting rule, the region picker, the media marks, the species links) already ships, and the one missing input is data the annual taxonomy build already downloads and throws away.

## The User Problem
A birder knows they have 600 species but cannot say how those spread across the bird families: whether they have seen 9 of the world's rails or 2, which families they have never photographed, how thin their audio is among the wood-warblers. eBird lists a life list flat or in taxonomic order with no totals per family, and Birds of the World shows the whole class with no notion of "mine." The birder wants to see the shape of their own list on the tree of birds, and to ask that question of one county, one year, or the species still missing a photo.

## What the repo establishes (the grounds for the decisions below)
- **The app holds no family or order today.** `frontend/src/assets/ebird-taxonomy.json` carries five maps (`bySci`, `byCom`, `byOrder`, `byCode`, `reportAs`): names, codes, the taxonomic sort number and the report-as parent. A grep for family across `frontend/src` finds no taxonomic use. The eBird backup carries a common name, a scientific name and a taxonomic order number per row, and nothing about family or order either.
- **The missing fields are already fetched at build time.** `scripts/build-ebird-taxonomy.mjs` reads `api.ebird.org/v2/ref/taxonomy/ebird?fmt=json` once a year on the developer's Mac and derives the bundle plus two frontend-only assets (`ebird-countability.json`, `ebird-alpha-codes.json`) from that one response. eBird's response carries each taxon's order and family (code, common name, scientific name); the derive step drops them.
- **So the split of facts is:** which species, where, when and with what media come from the user's two files; order, family, family size and current genus come from the bundled taxonomy. No fact this feature needs has to be asked of any server from the user's device.
- **The counting rule exists and is single-sourced.** `isNonCountableForm` decides what is a species; a subspecies-level form folds into its parent; the escapee rule is a cached answer that only Statistics may start and every other tab reads passively (an import-graph guard enforces it). Life Lists (`lib/lifeLists.ts`, `lib/lifeListsRegion.ts`) already derives the life list under a region and under photo, audio and video, with **Include Escapees** read passively.
- **Species links exist; family links do not.** `<BirdName>` gives every species its Species Detail link and the eBird and Birds of the World marks (`ebird.org/species/<code>`, `birdsoftheworld.org/bow/species/<code>/cur/introduction`). Macaulay links build on `ML_CATALOG_BASE` with `taxonCode`. No link in the app targets a family, an order or a genus.
- **A new tab has a known price here.** A fourteenth entry in `TAB_LABELS` and `DEFAULT_TAB_ORDER`, the one-generation tab-order migration, a `docs/HELP.md` section, every App Store, website and F-Droid screenshot set retaken, and a one-sentence README and website proposal held for the user's approval. Life Lists, Targets and Hotspot Reports each paid it.

## Success Criteria
- A birder opens the tab with only an eBird backup saved and sees, without any request leaving the device for it, every family they have recorded, each with "seen of world species," grouped under its order, plus a headline of orders, families and species seen.
- With an ML export saved too, each family also shows how many of its seen species have a photo, an audio recording and a video, and how many items of each kind.
- **The numbers reconcile.** At Everywhere, all years and default switches, the species in the tab equal the Life Lists count at its defaults (and so the Statistics Species figure at its defaults); under a region, a media choice or Include Escapees, they equal Life Lists at the same settings. The per-family counts plus the unplaced group sum to that total, in both views, always.
- The tree shows only the birder's own species, and changing Region, Year or the media filter redraws both views to the same set.
- A birder can zoom from the whole tree down to readable species names and back, with buttons and keys as well as pinch and wheel, on iPhone, iPad, Android, Mac, Windows and the self-hosted web version.
- Every species name opens Species Detail and carries its two link marks; every family offers its Birds of the World page; nothing renders a link built from a value that fails its shape check.
- On a phone the page is the only thing that scrolls up and down until the birder opens the tree full screen: no box can be scrolled while it is partly off screen (D-17, D-20).
- A keyboard or screen-reader user gets everything the diagram offers (every level, count and link) from the rollup view, which reflows at 320 px and 200% text.
- A name the bundled taxonomy cannot place is shown in an honest "not placed" group with the reason, never dropped and never guessed into a family.
- Opening the tab on a backup at the upload cap does not freeze the app, and a life list in the thousands of species stays navigable in the tree.

## Scope
- **A new build-time taxonomy asset** derived from the same annual fetch: per family its code, common and scientific name, order and world species count, and each species' family. It also carries the names the Names control shows that eBird does not supply: a plain-English name for each order from a small authored table (one entry per order in the asset), and a common name for a genus only where one can be derived without judgment, which is when every world species of the genus shares one group word in its common name; a genus without one stays scientific only (D-21, D-23). Both are authored or derived at build time. Lazy-loaded, off the entry chunk, regenerated in the annual taxonomy runbook, with a generator that fails closed and a CI equivalence check, like `ebird-countability.json`.
- **The rollup view:** orders and families the birder has recorded, in taxonomic order by default and sortable; per family the species seen of world species, species photographed, recorded and filmed, and item totals per media kind; each family row opens to its genera and species (species through `<BirdName>` with media marks), with **Expand all** and **Collapse all**, the same pair the tree has (D-15). A headline gives orders, families and species seen against the world totals.
- **The tree view:** a zoomable, pannable diagram of order, family, genus and species for the birder's own species only; branches expand and collapse; family and order nodes carry their rollup counts; species leaves show media state; a way to find a species, family or order and jump to it (D-24).
- **A Names control shared by both views** with three states: common, scientific, or both at once. It covers order, family, genus and species, opens on Both (the style of the app's list tables), and is remembered. Find matches what is shown (D-14, D-22, D-23).
- **The phone form (D-17, D-20):** the table flows with the page, with no inner vertical scroller. The tree shows in the page as a still of the whole tree that does not scroll, with an "Open the tree" button; pressing either opens the tree full screen on the app's existing map full-screen pattern, where the tree's box is the only scroller, and leaving it keeps zoom, place, expansion and selection. The same full-screen toggle is in the tree's toolbar on every screen size. The phone tier also shows the app's existing rotate or use a larger screen note (D-16).
- **Filters shared by both views:** Region (the Life Lists picker: country, state or province, county, Everywhere), Year (one year or all years, meaning species recorded in that year), and Media (species that have, or that lack, a photo, audio or video). **Include Escapees**, off by default, read passively as in Life Lists.
- **Links:** species as above. Family: its Birds of the World account by family code, and the birder's own Macaulay media for that family if the catalog accepts a family code. Order and genus: in-app actions only (expand, focus, narrow to it) unless a stable Cornell URL shape is verified.
- An empty state without a backup, a media-less state without an ML export, the "not placed" group, and a line saying how many recorded entries are not single species and so have no place on the tree.
- `docs/HELP.md` section, tab icon, Search entry, the tab's place in the default order (between Breeding Codes and Hotspot Reports, D-13) with its one-generation tab-order migration, and the held one-sentence proposals for README and website.

## Out of Scope
- **Unseen birds as tree content.** No world tree, no ghosted unseen species, no list of families never seen. Unseen species exist only as each family's denominator and the headline totals. (Listing unseen families is the obvious follow-up; it goes to the idea inbox, not this build.)
- Regional denominators ("seen of the species on the California list"): that needs eBird region lists, which is a new request.
- Phylogenetic meaning: branch lengths, divergence dates, cladograms within a family. This is the ranked eBird/Clements classification, drawn as a tree.
- Subspecies and forms as their own leaves; spuhs, slashes and hybrids on the tree (they have no single place in it); a Count all forms mode.
- Arbitrary date ranges, protocol, breeding-code, age and sex filters; saved filter sets.
- Exporting or sharing the tree as an image or file; printing.
- A Statistics card for families, and "show in tree" links from other tabs.
- Any new third-party request, endpoint, host, embedded page or durable store; any change to the escapee check or to who may start it.
- Editing, annotating or reclassifying anything.

## Key Decisions
- **One feature, one new tab, both views in it.** The rollup is not a Statistics card and the tree is not a separate tab, because the two must share filters and totals, and because the rollup is the diagram's accessible equivalent: splitting them across tabs would break both. A view of Life Lists was weighed and set aside: it would save the tab's fixed costs, but it buries the piece the user is most excited about and loads a tab whose object is a chronological list.
- **Built rollup first, diagram second.** The asset, the tab, the rollup, the filters and the links are a complete, shippable slice with no rendering risk. The diagram is where the risk sits (thousands of nodes on a phone WebView, zoom, keyboard access), so it lands on a working model. If the diagram cannot meet the bar on some platform, that is written down as a measured decision and the rollup carries the feature there behind a named predicate in `lib/platformGates.ts`.
- **No new counting rule.** The species set is Life Lists' derivation at the same settings: `isNonCountableForm` is the only countability predicate, forms fold into their parent, escapees are read passively through the one taxonomy join (`loadEscapeeJoin()`), and the tab's modules join the passive side of `exoticProvenanceGraph.test.ts`. The reconciliation criteria above are the test of it and should be asserted structurally, not as typed-in totals.
- **Family size is world species in the bundled eBird taxonomy,** species category only, not counting species eBird marks extinct (a recorded extinct species still counts on both sides). The denominator stays the world figure under every filter, and the tab says so when a filter is on.
- **Classification comes from the current taxonomy, never from the backup's text.** Genus, family and order are looked up from the resolved species, so an older export with a since-moved genus still draws one consistent tree, and no new scan over user-file text is needed. If the build does add one, the schema declares it up front with its `security.md` glob and clamp cost.
- **The network requirement, in its durable form:** this feature makes no third-party request, adds no endpoint or host, and moves no request between components. On web and Pi the new asset is one more lazy chunk from the user's own server. Species codes for the link marks come from the taxonomy lookups every tab with bird names already makes. The order and genus common names (D-21) do not change this: they are authored or derived at build time into the bundled asset, with no new request. So `PRIVACY_POLICY.md` should not need to change; if the Architect finds otherwise, that is held copy.
- **New link shapes are verified before they are built.** The Birds of the World family URL and a family-level Macaulay `taxonCode` are believed to work but are not established in this repo; Cornell's pages refuse scripts, so the check is a look in a browser. Each new href gets a shape guard on its code, `encodeURIComponent`, `openNewTabLink` on the same expression, and a runtime row; an unverified shape ships as no link.
- **Rule files move with the code:** the new asset, its generator and the tab's `lib/` modules extend `bird-names.md` `paths` in the same change, and the annual runbook in `scripts/taxonomy-history-input/README.md` gains the regeneration step.
- **In-memory derivation only.** Nothing derived is stored, so nothing joins `clearDerived.ts`. Remembered choices (region, view, and the Names choice, D-14) go through the `storage` seam; switches are per-session, as on Life Lists.
- **The diagram's library and renderer are the Architect's,** inside these limits: off the entry chunk, no new host, labels that are real text, and not gesture-only.

## Open for the Designer
Settled at the Stage 4 design review (2026-11-21). Each item is kept as first written, with where it was settled; the approved mockup is the direction the design spec is written from (D-23).
- The tab's label and its icon. **Settled:** the label is Family Tree, between Breeding Codes and Hotspot Reports (D-13, D-24); the icon is as the approved mockup has it (D-23).
- The diagram's form, what is expanded on arrival, how counts and media state sit on nodes, and how zoom, fit and find are presented. **Settled:** the tree arrives showing birds by name at reading size from the top of the tree, with Fit one press away (D-24); the rest is as the approved mockup has it (D-23), and the phone presentation is D-20.
- Which view opens first, and whether that differs on a phone. **Settled** as the approved mockup has it (D-23); the phone form of each view is D-17 and D-20.
- The shape of the Media filter and of the Year control. **Settled** as the approved mockup has them (D-23); media figures follow Region and Year like every other figure on the tab (D-24).
- How "seen of world" is drawn in a family row and what the headline looks like. **Settled** as the approved mockup has them (D-23).
- How a genus or order node reads, given neither has an outside page. **Settled:** both now carry a common name where one is available, under the Names control (D-14, D-21, D-23), and find returns orders too (D-24).

## Companion build (not scoped here)
The user reported the same scroll-within-scroll on the map tabs on phones. That fix runs alongside as its own build in its own workspace and ships in the same release as this feature; neither ships alone (D-18). This brief does not scope it. The two builds share one phone answer (D-20), and the full-screen panel's stacking level and the phone's bottom bar are the map build's to settle and this build's to consume (D-19).

## Alignment with the product brief
Aligned on every count: the same birder, their own two files, a view eBird and Birds of the World do not offer, computed on the device, working offline, with nothing new sent anywhere. It works alongside Birds of the World (it links to it at species and family level) and does not reproduce its content: no species accounts, no illustrations, no world tree. No tension with the founding decisions or the out-of-scope list.
