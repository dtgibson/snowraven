# Held patch: privacy policy and accessibility statement

These are the changes the Help refresh found in `PRIVACY_POLICY.md`, `website/privacy.html` and
`ACCESSIBILITY.md`. **None of them has been written.** They are applied only if you say yes.

- Every privacy change is made word for word on `website/privacy.html` too, so the policy and
  its web page stay identical.
- Changing the policy also moves its effective date, on both pages. The date is set to the day
  the patch is applied: it is written as October 2, 2026 here and is restamped if the patch is
  applied later.
- Each new or changed sentence gets a test that checks it against the app's code, so it cannot
  quietly go out of date.
- The ready-to-apply version is `held-patch.diff` in this folder (`git apply` it from the repo
  root). It has been applied, tested and removed again; the files on disk are unchanged.

## Privacy policy (and its web page)

### P1. What the app saves from eBird stays on your device, except one thing you can turn on

Today this paragraph says everything saved from an answer stays on your device. Since 1.0.40, with iCloud Sync on, the Targets tab's day-by-day eBird answers also go to your own iCloud account, and the iCloud Sync section of the same policy already says so. The two contradicted each other.

**Now:**

> What you send to these services (a checklist ID, a location, a search term) is governed by each provider's own privacy policy. SnowRaven only relays the request you initiated, or the scheduled request of a widget you placed or of Alerts you turned on. It adds no tracking, and neither the request nor its answer ever reaches the developer. Anything the app saves from an answer stays on your device (the widgets' saved results are described in the iOS App section below).

**Proposed:**

> What you send to these services (a checklist ID, a location, a search term) is governed by each provider's own privacy policy. SnowRaven only relays the request you initiated, or the scheduled request of a widget you placed or of Alerts you turned on. It adds no tracking, and neither the request nor its answer ever reaches the developer. Anything the app saves from an answer stays on your device, with one exception that is yours to turn on: with iCloud Sync on, the Targets tab's day-by-day eBird answers are also copied to your own iCloud account, one copy per device, so your other devices do not ask again (see the iCloud Sync section below). The widgets' saved results are described in the iOS App section below.

### P2. The Weather tab's Plan form reads your location by itself

When you press the Plan button with no place chosen, the app asks the device for your location and fills in "Your location" in the form it opens. Nothing is sent until you press one of the form's two buttons, which send that place to OpenWeather and NOAA. The list of location controls never mentioned Plan, and the "only sent outward if" sentence did not cover those two buttons.

**Now:**

> When you use a location control (the location button on the map, "Use my location" in the map's filters or when setting a default location in Settings, "My location" when choosing where the Targets tab measures distances from, "My location" or "Use my location" in the Alerts section of Settings on iPhone and iPad, or the "Current" weather and tide lookup), SnowRaven asks your device or browser for your current coordinates, with your permission. Those coordinates stay on your device: they set the map's center, measure distances on the Targets tab, and can be saved locally as your default location or as the place Alerts checks around. They are only sent outward if you then run a search (for hotspots or nearby sightings) or use the "Current" lookup, which sends your coordinates to OpenWeather and NOAA to fetch the weather and tide where you are, the same as coordinates you type in by hand, or, on iPhone and iPad, when a home-screen widget you placed refreshes or an alert check runs, as described below. You can deny or revoke the permission at any time through your operating system or browser settings.

### P2, continued. When the iPhone and iPad location prompt first appears

The iOS App paragraph says the system location prompt appears "the first time you tap 'Use my location'". The Current lookup could already be first, and now pressing Plan with no place chosen can be too. Only the opening of this long paragraph changes; the rest is as it is now.

**Now:**

> On iPhone and iPad, the first time you tap "Use my location" iOS shows the system location permission prompt ("SnowRaven uses your location to center the map on your current position, and to measure distances in its home-screen widgets"). The app itself uses your location only while you're using it. If you place one of SnowRaven's home-screen widgets (Nearby Lifers or Media Targets, on iOS and iPadOS 17 or later), the widget also reads your location each time iOS refreshes it, about every 30 minutes and as often as iOS allows, including while SnowRaven is not open, under that same permission. It sends those coordinates to eBird with your own eBird key to find the nearest recent reports, the same kind of request the app's Nearby Lifers view makes, and nothing reaches the developer. A widget you set to measure from your Default Location reads no location and sends that saved point instead. The widget never asks for permission itself and never asks for Always access. To stop it, remove the widget from your Home Screen, or deny SnowRaven location; with location denied, a widget measures from your saved Default Location if you have set one, and reads no location. If you turn on Alerts in Settings (off until you do), SnowRaven checks eBird about hourly or about daily, as iOS allows, including while SnowRaven is not open, for species you have never recorded near a place you choose, and notifies you on your device. Each check sends that place's coordinates and your chosen radius to eBird with your own eBird key, the same kind of request the app's Nearby Lifers view makes, and nothing reaches the developer; the notifications are made on your device, with no notification server. With Fixed place chosen, no location is used at all. With My location chosen, a check measures from the most recent position the app read while you were using it, or that a widget refresh read from your device, within the past 24 hours, rounded to about a kilometer (a widget that could not read your location and used your Default Location instead does not count; without such a position, a check measures from your fixed place, if you have one); it never reads your location while the app is closed and never asks for Always access. Turning Alerts off stops every check at once. You can allow or deny location, and change your choice at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services. Denying it leaves everything else working: you can always type coordinates or search for a place by name.

### P3. Copy iCloud details

The Mac, iPhone and iPad apps have a **Copy iCloud details** button that puts a sync report on your clipboard. It is the one route by which details about your synced files can reach the developer, and only if you paste it. The policy never mentioned it. This adds one new point to the iCloud Sync section, right after "Whose account."

**Now:** (nothing; the point before it is unchanged)

**Proposed, a new point:**

> - **Copy iCloud details.** If sync is not working, **Copy iCloud details** at the foot of the iCloud Sync section in Settings puts a plain-text report on your clipboard, for you to paste into a message to the developer if you choose. It lists what iCloud reports for each synced file: the app's version, your devices' names and the random identifier the app made up for each, whether iCloud is available, county codes, file sizes and dates, sync states, and Apple's error codes and messages. It holds no file contents, no file names you chose and no API keys. SnowRaven never sends it anywhere itself: it reaches the developer only if you paste it into a message.

### A3 and A4. A count of tabs, and charts you can operate

Two changes in the Screen Reader Support paragraph. **A3:** it said "all eight tabs that read your data" and listed them, but Targets now uses the same announced error region, so the count and the list were both wrong; it now states the rule (each tab that loads one of your files as it opens) with examples. **A4:** it said a chart drawn as a graphic carries an image role, but the Weather tab's plan timeline and the Splits and Lumps chart are charts you can operate, described by their controls instead; it now states that third case.

**Now:**

> SnowRaven uses semantic structure and ARIA attributes so assistive technology can describe the interface accurately. Where the navigation is a vertical list, its destinations and their panels are linked with the correct tab/tabpanel roles and selected state; where it is the phone bottom bar, each button marks itself as the current destination instead, and every panel carries its own name either way. Filter pills announce whether they are pressed, toggles announce on/off as switches, and sortable table columns announce their sort direction. A sort offered as a row of choices rather than a menu, such as Taxonomic and A–Z on the List Comparer or Newest and Oldest above a list of comments, marks the choice that is in force on the choice itself, so its highlight is never the only thing saying which sort you are looking at, and where those choices are drawn as one segmented control that control is a named group. The species selector exposes its expanded state and current option. The in-view marker lists use real list semantics, and collapsed filter panels are made inert so their hidden controls never become stray tab stops. Information that is shown only with an icon or a color (breeding-code tiers, map recency dots) carries a hidden text label so it is never lost to a screen reader. How a chart is described depends on how it is drawn, and the rule is the same everywhere it applies. A chart drawn as a GRAPHIC, where there is nothing in it for a screen reader to read, carries an image role and a concise text summary: that covers the line, bar, area and donut charts, among them life-list growth, checklists by month and weekday, temporal and media trends, and sightings over time. A chart drawn as ROWS OF TEXT does not, and that is deliberate rather than an omission: every value is already on its own row in words and numbers, so the bars beside them are marked decorative, and wrapping those rows in an image role would replace readable text with one long label. The Weather section's distribution and per-species charts on the Statistics tab are drawn that way, as are the Data Quality bars. Purely decorative chart flourishes are hidden from assistive tech either way. Regions that update on their own (the weather result, species and result counts, loading states, inline errors, and a keyboard tab move in the Tab Layout list) are announced as they change. A region that arrives with its first message is the standard way for an announcement to be missed, so the region is mounted first and filled afterwards, each message as a fresh child node. The region holds the sentence and nothing else: the alert icon beside it is hidden from assistive technology and the "Go to Settings" button sits outside it, so what is read out is the sentence itself. The Weather tab's checklist backlog reports a saved backup it cannot load in the same words and from the same kind of region. Because that one is a section you open and close rather than a tab, its region sits outside the part that collapses, so the region is in place before any message reaches it, rather than being created along with its text: opening the section fills a region that was already there, and closing the section empties that same region rather than removing it. ## Optional extras (not in the patch)

These came up in the audit but are judgment calls. Say which, if any, you want, and they can be
added the same way.

   section say slightly different things about whether clearing the eBird backup removes the
   position Alerts read. Proposed: make the data section say it does, as the iOS App section does.
2. **OpenWeather for the Planner.** The OpenWeather point says "for a location and time you
   choose", which does not cover the Planner's whole forecast window (the NOAA point was updated
   for that). Proposed: add "or the forecast across the days ahead for a place you choose".

   Targets tab"; it also runs when you switch counties. Proposed: "when you open the Targets tab
   or choose another county".

   removing a bar-chart file also removes it from iCloud. Proposed: one clause in the "You can
   delete your stored files" point: "with iCloud Sync on, removing a bar-chart file also removes
