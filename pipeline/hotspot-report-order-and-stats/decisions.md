# Decisions: hotspot-report-order-and-stats

- **D-01 (2026-11-12, The Designer). First design direction for the Improve-lane design pass, drawn inside `pipeline/design-system.md`. Not yet approved: it is the first direction for the user's review.** The mockup is `pipeline/hotspot-report-order-and-stats/design.html` and the working spec is `design-refinement.md`. No token is minted and no new pattern is introduced. The choices worth recording, each with what would reverse it:
  - **Reading order, at every width: header, At a glance, jump row (one column only), Map, Visits and media, Weather on your visits, Checklists by protocol, Life list.** This reverses FR-31 and hotspot-reports D-01 by the user's direction ("make the life list last"). Map stays first among the compact sections because it is the shortest and says where the place is; Visits follows because the months and years continue the opening figures' account of visits; Weather describes those visits; Protocols is the most administrative and sits last before the list. Reversal: the shipped order of the four (Map, Protocols, Weather, Visits) costs nothing to restore, since they are independent cards.
  - **The opening figures are their own card, "At a glance", directly under the header, not part of the header band.** The header stays byte-for-byte as shipped, the block gets the section head every other section has, and FR-50 holds without a rule about what the header may repeat. Reversal: joining the band, which would touch `HotspotReportHeader.tsx` and its tests.
  - **Two registers inside the card, both already in the app.** Counts are tiles: the header band's figure register (a micro-caps label over a figure, `.sr-hsr-fig-label`) one step down, at the 1.25rem / 700 figure the design system's share-breakdown headline pair uses, with the denominator beneath in the note register (`.sr-hsr-note`). Dated and linked facts are the shipped fact rows (`.sr-hsr-facts`), moved up from Visits and media. Statistics' centered `StatCell` was considered and not used: its value, sub-line and label are centered, and a denominator that wraps to three lines at phone width reads ragged when centered.
  - **Deviation 1, the tile grid uses `auto-fit`, where the uniform badge set says `auto-fill`.** The badge rule exists so a short last row keeps its columns; that still holds here, because only wholly empty tracks collapse. What `auto-fit` adds is that three tiles stretch across a wide card instead of sitting in three of seven tracks at its left edge. Reversal: `auto-fill`, if the stretched tiles read too sparse in the built app.
  - **Deviation 2, the fact rows in this card carry their rule on the top edge and sit in at most two columns.** The shipped fact rows are one column with the rule beneath each. In a two-column grid a rule beneath leaves a stray line under the last row, and the last row cannot be selected in a self-collapsing grid; a rule above makes the first row's rule the divider under the tiles and leaves nothing behind. The two-column cap is `max(19rem, 34%)` as the track minimum, so no query is needed. Scoped under `.sr-hsr-glance`; the shipped fact row elsewhere is unchanged (after this change nothing else uses it, so the Engineer may fold the two).
  - **First and latest visit, time birded and the biggest checklist move up into the card. Visits and media keeps its name and holds Visits by month, Visits by year, and Photos, audio, and video.** The name is still true and its chip stays "Visits". Time birded gains the average on its note line ("over 398 of 412 checklists that recorded a duration, 1 hour 15 minutes on average"); the clause is dropped when one checklist recorded a duration, where the average is the total.
  - **What a thin place shows (the many / one / none rule, applied to figures).** Days birded and Years birded are drawn when the place has two or more dated checklists; with one, the header's "1 checklist" and the "Only visit" row already say it. Species per hour is drawn when at least one checklist has a usable duration above zero, and its note names how many it rests on; with none, the tile is absent and the Time birded row carries the shipped sentence. First species here and Newest species here are the life list's own oldest and newest rows; a list of one species shows one row, "Only species here"; a list whose oldest and newest rows share a first date shows neither, because two birds from one checklist are not a first and a newest. When no tile applies, the facts stand alone in one column.
  - **Two columns: the same two tracks, traded.** `minmax(0, 24rem) minmax(0, 1fr)`, the rail of compact sections on the left and the life list on the right, so the list is last in the DOM and last in reading order with no `order` and no grid-area placement. The opening figures span the full width above both. The list column is still the report less the 24rem rail, the 16px gap and the 2px border, so D-07's arithmetic gives the same pair, 53.25em and 28em. Both are re-measured in the built app (per grid cell, classic scrollbar, both engines) before they ship, because the columns changed sides. Reversal: one column at every width, with the compact sections in a two-up grid above a full-width list, which puts the list about a screen further down in a wide window.
  - **The jump row stays, moves to just after At a glance, takes the new order (Map, Visits, Weather, Protocols, Life list), and is still shown only while the report is one column.** Its job has turned over: it used to carry a reader past the long list to the short sections, and now it carries them past the sections to the list. Placed after the figures, the header and the figures read as one opening (on a 390px phone the first tiles are on the first screen), and the rule for the chip set stays whole: every section after the row has a chip and nothing before it does. The cost is that the row is about one screen down on a phone rather than at the top. Reversals, both drawn by the mockup's Jump row switch: directly under the header (the shipped place; one tap from the very top, 60px on a 390px phone and 104px at 320px before the figures begin, and At a glance becomes the one section below the row with no chip), or no row at all.
  - **Offered figures (the user's call at the review; the mockup draws each on request).** Recommended in: Distance traveled as a fact row beside Time birded; Recorded once and Longest gap as tiles; Most-recorded species as a ten-row section directly above the life list, whose figure it digests. Recommended out: Party size (three figures about the observer more than the place) and Highest counts (a second ten-row list, and the only figure on the page that counts individuals, so it would need its own rule). Longest gap is drawn only with three or more distinct days; Most-recorded species only with two or more checklists.
  - **Motion: nothing new.** The view's shipped 160ms entrance, the shipped distribution bars, the 120ms hover fades inside `@media (hover: hover)`. Tiles do not count up and the card does not animate in on its own. The global reduced-motion block and the tab's own reduced-motion skip cover it as they do today.
  - **Declared reads (Improve lane has no schema, so they are named here for the Engineer and the Auditor).** Days birded, years birded, longest gap, species per hour, recorded once and most-recorded species read arrays `deriveLocationFacts` and `applySpeciesRule` already build. Distance traveled and Party size read two numeric cells of the checklist the report already parses; each needs a stated plausibility bound, as FR-53 has for duration, and a clamp on any loop a value could drive. Highest counts would add a per-species maximum inside the existing species loop. None adds a scan over comment text.

- **D-02 (2026-11-12, user approval at the design review, recorded by The Designer). The first direction is approved as drawn ("Looks great"), and `design-refinement.md` is final.** The user was shown the three open questions with the Designer's recommendations and changed none, so the recommendations are the approved answers:
  - **Figures.** The recommended opening set (days birded, years birded, species per hour, first and latest visit, time birded with its average, most species on one checklist, first and newest species here), plus four of the six offered: **Distance traveled** as a fact row after Time birded, **Recorded once** as a tile, **Longest gap** as a tile (three or more distinct dates), and the ten **Most-recorded species** as its own section directly above the life list. **Party size and the ten highest counts are out** and are not built. Reversal: either can be added later as drawn in the mockup; Highest counts would first need a counting rule for individuals, which FR-36 does not give.
  - **Jump row.** Kept, after At a glance, one column only, chips in the new order. **The most-recorded section gets a chip, "Most recorded"**, because the rule the row was approved under is that every section after it has one; the chip is present exactly when the section is (it is absent at one checklist or with no countable species). The chips read Map, Visits, Weather, Protocols, Most recorded, Life list.
  - **Wide window.** Compact sections in the 24rem left rail, the life list down the right, the header and At a glance full width above.
  - **Distance bound, settled by the Designer at the Guide's direction.** A checklist's distance is usable when it is a finite number from 0 to `DISTANCE_MAX_KM = 2400` kilometers inclusive; anything else is left out of the total, the average and the count, and the note's "{n} of {C}" shows it. The bound is derived from the one FR-53 already uses: eBird caps a checklist at 24 hours (`DURATION_MAX_MIN = 1440` in `lib/hotspotReportsLocation.ts`), and 24 hours at 100 km/h is 2,400 km. It is generous on purpose: its job is to keep one corrupt cell from becoming a place's total, not to judge a long driving count, so no real checklist should fall outside it. A recorded 0 counts, as a recorded duration of 0 does. Reversal: a real export with an honest distance above 2,400 km raises the constant; a wish to follow eBird's own guidance on traveling counts would be a different, stricter rule and a decision of its own.
  - **Distance unit.** eBird exports kilometers. The report sums in kilometers, converts once at display with the shared `KM_TO_MI`, and shows miles to one decimal with the word spelled out ("312.4 miles"), because Statistics shows distance in miles and the app has no unit setting; one decimal always, so the noun is always plural and "1 miles" cannot be written; a nonzero total that rounds to 0.0 reads "less than 0.1 miles". Statistics applies no distance bound, so for a file with a corrupt distance the two totals can differ, as they already can for duration; Help says so with the other differences. Reversal: a units setting, which would be a new stored value and a feature of its own.
  - **Species per hour, the zero-minute case.** The figure rests on checklists whose usable duration is above zero, which can be fewer than the checklists Time birded counts. The note reads "averaged over {n} of {C} checklists with a duration" when the two counts are equal and "... with a duration above zero" when n is smaller (and "from the 1 checklist here with a duration", with or without "above zero", at n of 1), so the two denominators on one card never read as a disagreement.
  - **Thresholds.** The pair 53.25em and 28em stands as derived; both, and the two self-collapsing track minimums (19rem facts, 8rem tiles), are re-measured in the built app. **The classic-scrollbar leg is owed there**: the mockup's attempt did not take effect in the headless engines and is not claimed.
  - **The mockup** opens in the approved state and keeps its comparison switches, so the record shows what was chosen against what.

- **D-03 (2026-11-12, The Engineer). Species per hour follows the brief's definition: every checklist whose usable duration is above zero, a timed checklist with no species under the rule counting as zero.** The design spec flagged that the brief ("checklists with a usable nonzero duration") and Statistics' own figure (`computeEffort` also requires `speciesCount > 0`) differ only for a timed checklist with no countable species. The brief's form is built, for three reasons. (1) It keeps the approved denominator wording exact: the figure rests on fewer checklists than Time birded counts exactly when some checklist recorded a duration of 0, so "with a duration" / "with a duration above zero" is the whole story. Statistics' form would add a second, unworded cause (a timed checklist with nothing countable) and the "above zero" note would then be false in that case. (2) A timed outing that recorded only a spuh or an escapee is a real hour at the place in which nothing the rule counts was seen; leaving it out raises the rate the place reports. (3) It is FR-36 read literally: the figure is computed from the per-checklist counts `applySpeciesRule` builds (escapees subtracted), never from `ChecklistEntry.speciesCount`, and those counts are zero for such a checklist. A consequence named so it is not rediscovered: a checklist with a corrupt Date cell has no species under FR-36 (a species counts only from a dated record), so if it carries a duration it is in the mean at zero; only a hand-damaged export reaches it. The duration bound is FR-53's (`DURATION_MAX_MIN`, through one `usableDuration` predicate shared with Time birded), where `computeEffort` applies none. Tests: the decided case (`hotspotReportsLocation.test.ts`, "a timed checklist with no species under the rule is in the mean at zero; Statistics leaves it out", Statistics reading 2 where this tab reads 1 on the same rows), a generated corpus of 120 seeds held to the per-checklist counts recomputed from the raw rows, the 0 / blank / negative / over-bound durations, and the published-claims row that drives both. Help states the difference beside the others. Reversal: a user who wants the place's rate to agree with Statistics; the change is the `d === 0` guard in `applySpeciesRule` becoming `counts[ci] === 0`, plus a third note wording.

- **D-04 (2026-11-12, The Engineer). The date figures: distinct day keys, and the longest gap in integer civil days, never `Date`.** Days birded is the size of an integer `Set` of the day keys the checklist loop already computes (`dayKey`), and Years birded is the year map's size with its first and last kept in the same loop. The longest gap maps each distinct key to its civil day number once through the app's one date parser (`civilDaysFrom(dayToIso(key))`, Hinnant's days_from_civil: integer arithmetic, no `Date`, no clock), sorts the pairs by that number, and takes one subtraction per neighbouring pair, so its cost is O(D log D) in the distinct dates and does not depend on the span they cover (the linearity row "the facts' date figures do not grow with the span" reads 1.43 against a limit of 3 for consecutive days against one date a year across 9,998 years, and a day-by-day walk is the mutant that must exceed it). CLAUDE.md's rule that a count of days between two local midnights ROUNDS the millisecond difference has nothing to act on, because no millisecond difference is taken; the suite pins a span across the US daylight-saving change at exactly 2 days, plus leap days, 1900 and 2000. Two key shapes are not calendar dates and are handled, not rejected, matching the rest of the tab: a day past its month's end (`2024-02-31`, which `dayKey` admits and civil arithmetic reads as Mar 2) is ordered by its civil number, so no gap is ever negative and two keys on one civil day make a gap of 0, never drawn; a year of 0000 has no civil day and is left out of the gap only (it still counts as a dated day). The gap's line is the two dates either side through `formatDateRange`, the app's shared range formatter, with "to" for its separator, so it follows the date-format preference and collapses as that formatter does: "Jun 11 to Aug 27, 2016" and "Dec 20, 2019 to Feb 3, 2020" month-first (the spec's examples), "11 Jun to 27 Aug 2016" day-first, both dates whole in ISO, and "Jun 11 to 27, 2016" within one month. Should the formatter's separator ever change, `gapNote` writes both dates whole rather than a broken string.

- **D-05 (2026-11-12, The Engineer). Recorded once and Most-recorded species read the life list's own rows, and the top ten are selected, not sorted.** Recorded once counts the rows whose own checklist figure (FR-37) is 1, and each Most-recorded row IS its life-list row object, so the figure a reader sees there equals the one in the list by construction; both are held to a raw-row oracle and the life list over generated corpora. The ten are chosen by insertion into a list of at most eleven (`topRows`), linear in the life list, and the suite holds the result equal, row for row and object for object, to a stable sort of the whole list cut at ten, over 80 corpora full of ties, plus a tie at the tenth place cut by name. `TOP_SPECIES_MAX` (10) is the cap; the lead line's "ten" is that constant in words, and a row holds the two together. The one-species sentence ("{Bird} is the only species here, on {n} of {C} checklists.") passes no `trailing` to `BirdName`: the words that follow the name begin with a space and the only punctuation is the sentence's own full stop at the end, so there is nothing for the name's row to hold.
