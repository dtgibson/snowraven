# Strategic Brief: Hotspot Reports

## What We're Building
A new **Hotspot Reports** tab: a report for any one location in the user's own eBird backup, a public eBird hotspot or a personal location, chosen from a searchable dropdown. Before a choice the page shows general statistics about the user's locations; after one it shows that location's life list, a map of it, its checklists counted by eBird protocol, the weather on the user's visits there, and a short set of other statistics, all derived from the two files already loaded.

## Why Now
The user asked for it fresh, in these words: "reports for all public and personal hotspots, selectable with the searchable dropdown field. The page will show general hotspot stats before a hotspot is selected. The page will then show a life list for that hotspot, a map of the location, the number of checklists of each kind submitted, average weather, and other available statistics." It is not a roadmap Up Next item and not a saved idea. It is the natural next step after 1.0.70: the Life Lists Region control stopped at country, state/province and county and its brief set locations aside on purpose ("a per-location list is a different object"). This is that object, and every ingredient it needs has already shipped: per-location ids and coordinates in the parse, the public-hotspot classification, the regional first-date passes, the weather-block reader, the protocol names, the checklist link on media rows.

## The User Problem
A birder thinks in places: the home patch, the yard, the refuge visited every spring. eBird will show a hotspot's all-observer totals, and SnowRaven answers "where do I bird most" in Statistics, but nothing answers "what is my own record at this one place": which species, first seen when, on what kinds of checklists, in what weather, with how much media. For a personal location eBird's site offers almost nothing at all. The answer is already sitting in the backup, and the user should be able to read it offline.

## What the repo establishes (the grounds for the decisions below)
- **The backup carries a location id, name and coordinates on every row, and no hotspot flag.** `ObservationEntry` has `locationId` (`L` plus digits), `location`, `latitude`, `longitude`, `county`, `stateProvince`. Whether an id is a public hotspot is not in the file (`lib/hotspotSet.ts` header).
- **The app already classifies it.** `lib/hotspotSet.ts` builds one region-scoped Set of public-hotspot ids from the existing `/map/hotspot-region` lookup (one request per distinct `stateProvince` region, gated at the transport chokepoint under the eBird pacing contract), persisted per region for 30 days by `lib/hotspotRegionCache.ts`, with a three-state resolution snapshot (pending, answered, unanswered). `lib/hotspotShare.ts` is the shipped precedent for never folding an unanswered region into "personal".
- **A place-scoped life list already has a meaning here.** `lib/lifeListsRegion.ts` dates each species to its earliest observation inside the region, and dates media firsts by the asset's linked checklist (`MLExportRow.checklistId` joined to the backup). `lib/lifeLists.ts` and `lib/lifeListsProvenance.ts` hold the countable, exotic and escapee rules, the last read passively with no request.
- **"Kind of checklist" is the Protocol column.** `ChecklistEntry.protocol`, rolled up per submission by `computeChecklists` (`lib/birdingStats.ts`) and named by `protocolName()` (`lib/checklistMeta.ts`); `allObsReported` gives complete versus incomplete.
- **Weather is not stored anywhere except inside the user's own checklist comments.** The Weather lookup is on demand per checklist (`/weather/{id}`: one eBird checklist request, outside the gate today, plus one or two OpenWeather historical requests) and keeps nothing. What exists durably is the weather block the user pasted into a checklist comment, which `lib/weatherBlockParse.ts` reads back and `computeWeatherStats(checklists, filteredObs)` (`lib/weatherStats.ts`) aggregates for Statistics and Species Detail with no request, no key and no stored document. That function already takes a checklist subset. Blocks are a minority: on the reference export measured at the weather-stats build, 353 of 3,252 checklists carried one.
- **Per-location derivations already exist in two places with two species rules.** Statistics' Geographic Stats ranks locations by checklists and by species (`lib/birdingStats.ts`, normalized names), and the Map Explorer's My species coloring counts countable species per location id (`buildHotspotPersonalStats`, `lib/hotspotColorModes.ts`). Neither applies the Life Lists escapee rule.
- **The searchable dropdown is the house combobox family**: the Targets county picker and the Life Lists Region picker, each its own `aria-activedescendant` combobox with ids keyed on an index. The Region picker's open item (ROADMAP, life-list-region-filter item 1) is that it renders every choice; a location list is much longer than a region list.

## Success Criteria
- With a backup loaded and nothing chosen, the page shows the general statistics and the dropdown; no map is mounted and nothing is requested from a map-tile host.
- The dropdown offers exactly the locations in the user's backup, searchable by name. Where the hotspot classification has answered for a location's region, the location reads as a hotspot or a personal location; where it has not (no eBird key, offline before a first answer, a rate-limited region), the location is still offered and reportable and simply carries no kind. A location is never called personal because eBird has not answered.
- Two locations that share a name are distinguishable in the dropdown and report separately, because a location is its Location ID, never its name.
- Choosing a location shows its life list: exactly the countable species with an observation at that location, each dated to its earliest observation there with that observation's checklist link, never a global first date. The list's total is the location's species count.
- The report shows the location on a map when the backup carries coordinates for it, and says so plainly when it does not.
- Checklists at the location are counted by eBird protocol under the names the app already uses, and the counts sum to the location's checklist total, with a blank protocol counted in a stated bucket rather than dropped.
- Weather is reported only from the weather blocks on the user's own checklists at that location, states how many of the location's checklists it rests on, and declines to average below a floor, saying how many blocks there are instead. No lookup is made to fill a gap.
- The other statistics named in Scope each state their denominator where one applies (for example time birded over the checklists that recorded a duration).
- A file replace, clear or iCloud arrival updates the page: a chosen location no longer in the backup falls back to the general statistics silently.
- No third-party request, no new endpoint or host, and no request moved between components is added by this feature (the full statement is Key Decision 6).
- The tab appears in the navigation, the tab-layout settings, Search and Help under one label, on every platform, and a user whose saved tab order is the previous default gets the new default order while a custom order keeps its order and gains the new tab.
- The whole page works at 320px and at 200% text scale, in both themes, with every bird name through `<BirdName>`.

## Scope
- **A new tab**, id `hotspot-reports`, label **Hotspot Reports** in `TAB_LABELS`, with its icon, its Search entry, its place in the tab-layout settings, and its `docs/HELP.md` section and Help table of contents entry in the same change.
- **The location picker**: one searchable dropdown in the house combobox family, listing the backup's locations by Location ID, labeled by name with enough place context to tell namesakes apart, grouped or marked by kind where the classification has answered.
- **General statistics, shown before a choice**: the number of locations the user has birded, split into hotspots and personal locations where the classification has answered (three states, as the Statistics hotspot share has it); and two short ranked lists, most checklists and most species, whose rows choose that location. Nothing else.
- **The report for one location**:
  - a header: name, kind where known, county and state/province, the hotspot link to eBird where it is a public hotspot;
  - the life list at the location: species, first date there, that checklist's link, and the number of the location's checklists the species is on;
  - a map of the location through `<SnowMap>`;
  - checklists by protocol, and the complete-checklist share;
  - weather on the user's visits, from weather blocks: sky condition, temperature and wind, with coverage stated;
  - other statistics, this set and no more: total checklists and total species; first and latest visit with their checklist links; visits by month and by year; total time birded with its denominator; the checklist with the most species; photo, audio and video counts by the asset's linked checklist at the location.
- **Tab-layout default migration**: the new id joins `DEFAULT_TAB_ORDER`, and `PREVIOUS_DEFAULT_TAB_ORDER` becomes the default it immediately replaces (the order that shipped with Life Lists), one generation only, the hidden set preserved, nothing written back.
- **Tests** in the tab's own suites and the guards a new tab moves (tab order, Help table of contents parity, tab-stop coverage, entry-chunk bounds, linearity rows for the new passes).
- **Release consequences carried by later stages**: the version bump's six-file set; a held proposal of at most one sentence for the new tab's section on the README, the website and the App Store listing, plus the What's New and Fastlane changelog lines, none written without the user's yes; and, by the user's standing direction for a new tab, every App Store and website screenshot set regenerated and shown per device, decided per image against the committed frame, from synthetic demo data only, with a new frame for this tab.

## Out of Scope
- **Reports for hotspots the user has never birded.** Everything asked for (a life list, checklists by kind, weather, media) exists only in the user's own record; eBird's API would supply a name, a pin and community sightings, which is a different page, costs at least one request per choice plus a new retained answer, and is already the Map Explorer's Hotspots view.
- **eBird's all-observer species list or totals for a hotspot.** Same reason: a new request class and a different object from "my list here".
- **Fetching weather for checklists that carry no block, and any new background fetching.** One eBird request and up to two OpenWeather requests per checklist, per location, for a number the page could not retain without a new store.
- **Comparing two locations, multi-select, or merging nearby or duplicate locations into one report.** No single first-date meaning, and the List Comparer is the comparison surface.
- **Exporting, copying, printing or sharing a report.**
- **Editing, renaming or moving locations.** eBird owns them.
- **Remembering the chosen location across launches, and syncing it.** The page is designed to open on the general statistics the user asked for; persistence would add a stored value keyed on file content.
- **Opening a report from a location name elsewhere in the app, and locations in Search.** A good follow-up once the tab exists; location names elsewhere keep their eBird links.
- **Filters inside a report** (date range, species, Include Exotics and Include Escapees switches), **an embedded media gallery, tide statistics, breeding-code or named-bird sections, and a map of all locations** on the general page.
- **Changes to Statistics, Map Explorer, Life Lists, Targets or the escapee provenance pass.**

## Key Decisions
1. **"All public and personal hotspots" means every location in the user's own eBird backup, public hotspots and personal locations alike; never-visited hotspots are out.** Reason: the life list, the checklist kinds, the weather and the media can only come from the user's record, and the Map Explorer already covers discovery. A hotspot is told from a personal location by the existing region-scoped hotspot Set (`useHotspotSet`, `getHotspotResolution`), never by the file, with three states: hotspot, personal, not yet known. The report itself never depends on the classification.
2. **A location is its Location ID.** Reason: names are user text and can repeat; the id is the only stable key in the file, and `Map` keys rather than object keys hold it (the v1.0.22 prototype-key crash).
3. **"The number of checklists of each kind" is a count of distinct submissions by the Protocol column**, named through `protocolName()`, with the complete-checklist share beside it. Reason: it is the only "kind" the backup carries and the one Statistics already uses, so the two cannot name the same protocol differently.
4. **"Average weather" is the weather on the user's own visits, read from the weather blocks already in their checklist comments at that location, with coverage stated and a floor below which nothing is averaged.** Reason: weather is stored nowhere else, the reader and the aggregation already exist and take a checklist subset, and it costs no request. The honest limits are part of the feature: it is a record of the user's visits, not a climate figure, and only checklists carrying a block count. The request-backed alternative is declined (Out of Scope); if the user later wants it, it is its own feature and owes the eBird pacing contract with one enforcement point, a durable store registered in `lib/clearDerived.ts`, and a privacy-policy sentence.
5. **The life list follows the Life Lists tab's rules at a new, narrower tier**: earliest observation at the location, countable forms through `isNonCountableForm`, exotics and escapees as Life Lists shows them by default, the escapee classification read passively and never fetched. Reason: the report headlines a life-list count, so it must agree with the tab that owns that word. Consequence for the Planner: Statistics' top locations by species and the Map Explorer's My species figure use other rules, so the PRD states one rule for this tab (the overview's species ranking included) and says where the older figures can differ, rather than leaving three numbers for one place unexplained.
6. **Network posture, in the durable form: this feature adds no third-party request, no new endpoint or host, and no request moved between components.** What it reuses, stated so nobody reads this as "no network": the location map draws through `<SnowMap>`, whose tiles are an existing third-party request to the existing keyless tile hosts, made only once a location with coordinates is chosen, and a map of a personal location asks for the tiles around it as every map in the app does; the hotspot classification is the existing `/map/hotspot-region` lookup with the user's own eBird key, at its existing enforcement point and in its existing 30-day store, which this tab reads and may be the first surface in a session to trigger; hotspot and checklist links are outbound links the user presses. Requests a system component makes from the app's process count toward this statement, and the feature adds none: no iframe, no embedded media. On web/Pi the loaded files and lazy chunks reach the page over HTTP from the user's own server as they do today.
7. **Nothing is persisted.** No durable store, so no `clearDerived.ts` row and no privacy-policy sentence; the chosen location lives for the session. Reason: the user asked for a general page before a choice, and a stored location id is a value keyed on file content for little gain. Reversal is cheap: one settings key through the `storage` seam, as the Region control has.
8. **It is a new tab, labeled Hotspot Reports.** Reason: the user said "the page", the content is a whole page, and no existing tab is location-scoped. The label is the user's own words; in-app copy uses eBird's terms (hotspot, personal location) inside the page. It stays distinct from the Map Explorer's Hotspots view. Placement in the default order is the Designer's, with directly after Life Lists as the recommendation. The known consequences of a new tab are listed in Scope and are not optional.
9. **The general statistics are small on purpose**: a count of locations by kind and two ranked lists that double as shortcuts. Reason: Statistics already ranks top locations, so this page earns only what helps a user pick a place.
10. **The feature adds scans over user-file text, declared here for the Architect**: one pass building a per-location index over the observations, the per-keystroke filter over location names in the picker, and the weather-block parse over the chosen location's checklists. Each owes a linearity argument and a measured cost per draw and per re-draw at the 150,000-row cap, a length clamp on the location-name cell, and a bound on the rendered option list that the file cannot inflate, because a hostile file can give every row its own location (the Region picker's measured open item is the warning). The in-memory index follows the house rule for a derivation with no document: tied to the loaded file's lifetime, no module-scoped holder without a teardown.
11. **Inherited constraints, named once**: bird names through `<BirdName>`, maps through `<SnowMap>` with maplibre off the entry chunk, every color through `var(--sr-*)` tokens in both themes, every button and link through the `Button` and `Link` primitives, DOM ids keyed on an index, WCAG 2.1 AA at 320px and 200% text, the files epoch in the tab's load effect, no em dashes and American spelling in copy, properties rather than counts in any published claim. Verification is automated tests plus a live look at the built app before the deploy gate; no agent touches a physical device.

## Alignment with the product brief
No tension. The feature serves the same birder, reads only their own exports on their own device, adds no account, server or data leaving the device, and works alongside eBird: it links out to the hotspot page rather than reproducing it.
