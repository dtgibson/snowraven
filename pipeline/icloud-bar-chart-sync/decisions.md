## Security review fixes, The Engineer (2026-09-28, after The Auditor)

11. **M1 and L1 are fixed in code; schema §8.3 and §10.2 are corrected to match.** M1: the merge's dedupe (and the load path's, same shape) now records a key at first sight, before its verdict, so a key repeated in `order` is validated once. At the Auditor's shapes the merge went from 2.8 to 3.4 s (552 KB) and 11 s (1.1 MB) to 2.5 ms and 4.8 ms. L1: the day-obs pass reads the purge generation at its start, before the listing and every download, and hands it to `mergeSharedSnapshot(text, gen)` as a required argument. When the generation has moved by the push half, the pass also skips the push: the Clear owns the iCloud half (it removes every device's snapshot and resets the preference the pass captured). A snapshot the Clear could not remove goes at the next check, which finds no local document. The Auditor recommended the push skip as well, and it was taken.

12. **Held privacy text, item 38, corrected (L2). Needs the user's express approval with the rest of the held patch.** Only this one sentence changed, identically in `PRIVACY_POLICY.md` and `website/privacy.html`, inside `held-published-copy.patch`. The earlier text claimed the day copies held "only" a list of fields, and the list left out what else the snapshot stores. Each entry stores `fetchedAt` (when that device asked eBird), `complete` (whether the day was over when it asked) and `bytes` (the list's size). Each copy also has the same small record a file has: its name, upload time, size, checksum and the device it came from.
    - **Before:** "Your app settings, map preferences and cached lookups stay on each device and are never synced, with one exception: the day-by-day lists of species that eBird reported in a county, which the Targets tab fetches with your key, are written into the same container as one copy per device, each holding only what that device fetched from eBird (the county, the day, and each species eBird listed with the time and place of that report as eBird gives them), so your other devices do not repeat those requests."
    - **After:** "Your app settings, map preferences and cached lookups stay on each device and are never synced, with one exception: the day-by-day lists of species that eBird reported in a county, which the Targets tab fetches with your key, are written into the same container as one copy per device, each holding only what that device fetched from eBird (the county, the day, when that device asked and whether the day was over by then, the list's size, and each species eBird listed with the time and place of that report as eBird gives them), along with the same small record each file has, so your other devices do not repeat those requests."
    - The next sentence ("They hold none of your own sightings.") is unchanged. The patch's guard row for item 38 asserts the new text, and its fields row now also checks the entry's own fields (`fetchedAt`, `complete`, `bytes`, `species`) against the sentence. With the patch applied, the 17 test files that read the published files passed (377 tests). The patch was then reversed and all seven touched files were confirmed byte-identical by hash.
    - **Item 44 (App Store review notes) is deliberately unchanged.** Its sentence ("The one cache that is: the Targets tab's per-day lists of species eBird reported in a county, one copy per device, so the user's other devices do not repeat those eBird requests; they hold no user sightings.") makes no "only" or completeness claim, so it is not false. Reverse this if Apple review asks what the day copies contain. The fix would then mirror item 38's parenthesis in that bullet.

13. **The merge stays on the main thread in this build. The worker is the named follow-on (schema V4, security I1).** Measured by the Auditor: a realistic 9.47 MB snapshot (300 entries, 57,000 records) merged into an empty store in **49 to 55 ms** (Node/V8 on this Mac, 3 runs). That is the ~50 ms threshold the schema set for escalation. It was not moved off-thread here for four reasons. (1) The merge runs at most once per CHANGED peer snapshot, because an unchanged digest is never downloaded again (`state.peers`). (2) It runs inside a background iCloud check, not on a user action. (3) 10 MB is the day store's own payload budget, so this is the worst case for a legitimate peer. (4) After M1, a hostile snapshot costs time linear in its size too.
    - **Reverse when** any of these holds. The same merge measures over ~50 ms under JavaScriptCore (the Mac, iPhone and iPad runtime) on a measurement the user performs or a simulator provides. `DAY_OBS_MAX_BYTES` is raised. Or the merge moves onto an interactive path, such as a tab visit.
    - **The fix and its location:** move `JSON.parse` and the per-entry validation in `mergeSharedSnapshot` (`frontend/src/lib/countyDayObsCache.ts`) into a worker shaped like `parseOffThread`, with the settle contract of CLAUDE.md v1.0.14, returning validated entries to the main thread, which keeps only the union and the one eviction pass. The caller, `runDayObsPass` in `frontend/src/lib/icloud/dayObsSync.ts`, is unchanged apart from awaiting it.

## Device pass on 1.0.40.1, The Engineer (2026-09-29, fix errand, hands-off)

17. **User report on TestFlight 1.0.40.1: "The first bar chart sync and delete worked fine. However, when I attempt to upload a bar chart from another device, it appears to be stuck on the 'waiting to upload' step."** Diagnosed from the code and the faked-native harness only (this Mac is not signed into iCloud; no device was touched). Still unknown at the time of writing: which device was which, whether it was the county just deleted, and whether Check now or a relaunch clears it.
    - **What puts a county in "Waiting to upload", and what moves it on.** Four paths in `countySync.ts`: (a) the push returned `uploaded: false` (the daemon has not sent it yet, normal straight after any push); (b) a later check's `none` decision read the listing's `file.uploaded === false`, which native computes as the county file AND its record both reporting `NSURLUbiquitousItemIsUploadedKey` (`item_file_status`); (c) the push rejected with `unavailable` or `timeout`; (d) the listing rejected and the county was an unpushed local change. Only a LATER CHECK moves (a) or (b) on.
    - **Hypotheses ruled out.** (1) A tombstone does not block: a file added after the removal is newer than the cleared marker, so the table says push; a remembered clear yields to it and is dropped. (3) No record time the second device cannot produce (every Tauri manifest time is `toISOString()` output) and no view that only the first device holds (the pass sets a view for every county in the union each check). (5) The county pass has no eviction or admission. A scratch two-device run of the real controller (peer upload, pulled here, peer removal or our own, then our add of the same county) pushed once and read "Up to date" at the next check, so with a native layer that behaves, the county logic settles.
    - **Root cause 1, confirmed in code: nothing reliably runs that later check.** The native watch (`start_watch_on_main` in `icloud.rs`) observes `*.record.json` only, never `barcharts/<code>.txt`, whose upload flag also gates the row, so when the file finishes uploading after its record no event fires. The check that this device's own record write triggers is queued behind the push check and runs before the daemon has sent anything. What remains is the five-minute visible poll, returning to the app, and Check now, which is in Settings and not on the Targets tab. A user watching the Targets card after an add therefore sees "Waiting to upload" for up to five minutes. This is hypothesis (2) in a sharper form: it is not a connectivity retry that is missing but any re-read after the upload completes. It is not specific to the device that did not make the first upload: any device's add is exposed, and whether a record event rescues it depends on which of the two items finishes uploading last. The first device's row very likely did the same and was either not watched or was refreshed by leaving the app. This variant clears on Check now, on relaunch and on returning to the app.
    - **Root cause 2, confirmed in code: a refused write was shown as "Waiting to upload", forever.** `pushCounty` mapped a native `unavailable` or `timeout` to "Waiting to upload". The push writes only into the local container, so offline it SUCCEEDS with `uploaded: false`; `unavailable` is a filesystem or coordinator failure and `timeout` a write that did not land within the 8 s command budget. Each check retried the push, failed again and showed "Waiting to upload" again, and the check itself reported success. This variant survives Check now and relaunch. Hypothesis (4): no deterministic write failure specific to a second device was found in `push_item_at` (the subdirectory is created on first write whoever made it, and a cleared marker is replaced by rename like any record), so only a device can say whether one occurred; if it did, it now shows. Also found: a listing refused with anything other than `timeout` / `unavailable` put an unpushed county on "Waiting to upload" without failing the check, against schema section 5 step 2, which names only those two.
    - **The fix.** (1) `icloudSync.ts`: after every check that reached iCloud and left a county reading "Waiting to upload", one follow-up check re-reads the flag after `UPLOAD_RECHECK_MS` (15 s), at most `UPLOAD_RECHECK_MAX` (8) in a row, two minutes, after which the five-minute poll governs as before. Nothing is armed after a failed check (offline keeps the FR-05 behavior), while a check is already queued, or while the window is hidden (the foreground trigger re-reads). The count resets only when a check leaves no county waiting, or when the user adds a file, so a file iCloud never takes cannot keep it running. Any check that starts drops a pending re-read and re-arms from its own outcome, and `stopWatching` clears the timer (disable and dispose). The re-read never re-uploads: an identical record decides `none`. (2) `countySync.ts`: every push refusal reads "Could not sync" with its mapped reason ("iCloud could not be read." or "iCloud did not respond in time.") and Retry, and the next check pushes again. The listing answered in that check, so a push timeout is the NFR-04 "answered once, then out of budget" case, not FR-05's offline one. (3) The listing maps only `timeout` / `unavailable` to "Waiting to upload" for an unpushed county, every other refusal to "Could not sync", per schema section 5 step 2. No new strings: every label and reason is an approved existing one, and `docs/HELP.md` already describes both states as they now behave.
    - **Which devices.** Every Apple build with sync on, the shipped Mac desktop 1.0.40 included: the controller is the same TypeScript in every bundle, and neither defect is iOS-specific. The fix reaches each platform only with a new build. No Rust changed.
    - **Tests, in `countySync.test.ts`.** The fake's listing now reports the upload flag per county (set from `knobs.pushUploaded` on a push, true for a seeded record), and the harness takes `uploadRecheckMs` (60,000,000 by default, so no existing test arms it). Seven new rows under "device pass on 1.0.40.1": the second-device sequence (peer upload pulled, peer removal applied, our add) settles to "Up to date" with no native event and no Check now, with one push only; the re-read stops after exactly `UPLOAD_RECHECK_MAX` checks while iCloud never takes the file; a push refused with `unavailable` and with `timeout` each reads "Could not sync" with its reason, and Retry then pushes it; a listing refused with `unknown` reads "Could not sync", while `timeout` and `unavailable` keep "Waiting to upload" (the two boundary rows, green before and after). Against the unfixed source, 5 of the 7 fail, each at its own assertion. Mutating the cap out of `armUploadRecheck` fails the bound row (dozens of checks against 8). The eight icloud suites pass (297 tests), typecheck and lint are clean, and the full frontend suite passes (8,738 passed, 5 skipped).
    - **Noticed, not changed.** (a) The data-file rows share both defects: `pushLocal` maps `timeout` / `unavailable` to "Waiting to upload" (asserted by a shipped FR-05 test since 1.0.11) and nothing re-reads a slot's flag after its csv finishes. Left as out of scope for this fix; the extension is to include `getICloudState().slots` in `armUploadRecheck`'s waiting test and to give `pushLocal`'s catch the county mapping, with that test updated. (b) Widening the native watch predicate to the county files was considered and not taken: it cannot be exercised here, and it relies on NSMetadataQuery delivering upload-flag updates, which only a device can confirm; the re-read does not depend on it. (c) Retry on a county error shows "Syncing, downloading" for the moment of the check, `retryBarChart`'s existing behavior for every county error; left.
    - **What the user's answers will settle.** If Check now (in Settings) or a relaunch cleared it, root cause 1 was the whole report and the re-read closes it. If it survived both, a native write was being refused; a build with this fix shows it as "Could not sync", and the reason line says which kind.
