## 8. Your Location, the iPhone and iPad paragraph: a new passage on Alerts, inserted before the sentence on changing the permission

**Before**

> You can allow or deny location, and change your choice at any time in Settings

**After**

> If you turn on Alerts in Settings (off until you do), SnowRaven checks eBird about hourly or about daily, as iOS allows, including while SnowRaven is not open, for species you have never recorded near a place you choose, and notifies you on your device. Each check sends that place's coordinates and your chosen radius to eBird with your own eBird key, the same kind of request the app's Nearby Lifers view makes, and nothing reaches the developer; the notifications are made on your device, with no notification server. With Fixed place chosen, no location is used at all. With My location chosen, a check measures from the most recent position the app read while you were using it, or that a widget refresh read from your device, within the past 24 hours, rounded to about a kilometer (a widget that could not read your location and used your Default Location instead does not count; without such a position, a check measures from your fixed place, if you have one); it never reads your location while the app is closed and never asks for Always access. Turning Alerts off stops every check at once. You can allow or deny location, and change your choice at any time in Settings

## 9. iOS App: a new paragraph after the widgets' paragraph

**Before**

> (new paragraph)

**After**

> If you turn on Alerts, the app also keeps, in that same container: your alert settings (including the fixed place you chose, and its name if you picked it by search); when the last check ran and what it found; under My location, the most recent position the app itself read while you were using it; any alert waiting for the end of your quiet hours; and the inbox of past alerts. Each alert in the inbox keeps the species, the place eBird reported it, how far away it was, when it was reported, when you were alerted, the radius it was found in, the place's label (your fixed place's name, "near you" or "nearby"), and the point its check measured from: your fixed place, or under My location your position rounded to about a kilometer. An alert waiting for the end of your quiet hours keeps its species, the place eBird reported it, how far away it was, and its check's point, radius and place label, at most until it is delivered and then forgotten at the next check or the next time you open the app. None of it is your eBird key. The inbox holds at most 200 alerts, and an alert, with its point, is deleted 30 days after it was made, at the next check or the next time you open the app. The position the app read is deleted when you switch to Fixed place, turn Alerts off or clear your eBird backup, and otherwise at the next check or the next time you open the app once it is 24 hours old or location access for SnowRaven is off. Switching to Fixed place or turning Alerts off does not remove the points already in the inbox: each stays with its alert at most until that alert is deleted or cleared, and a later check that finds the same species within seven days replaces it with that check's point. Clear in the Alerts inbox removes its alerts and their points, and clearing your eBird backup removes the inbox, any alert waiting for the end of your quiet hours, and the position the app read. Like the widgets' documents, they are included in device backups under the iOS defaults, removed when you delete the app, and never synced through iCloud Sync.
