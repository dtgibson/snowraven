# Strategic Brief — iOS Alerts (new lifer nearby)

## What We're Building
An opt-in, off-by-default alert on iPhone and iPad that tells the user when eBird reports a bird they have never recorded near a place they choose (a fixed point, or where they are), checked about hourly or about daily with quiet hours, with an in-app inbox of past alerts. Everything the feature does is decided and stored on the device; the only outside request is the app's existing nearby-sightings request to eBird with the user's own key.

## Why Now
The app already knows what a "nearby lifer" is on three surfaces: the Map Explorer's Nearby Lifers view, the Targets tab, and the iOS home-screen widgets shipped in 1.0.36, which already refresh in the background, read the device position under the existing When In Use grant, and hand the recorded-species set and the eBird key to a second process through an App Group. What is missing is the one thing none of them can do: tell the user when something changes without the user looking. A widget shows a lifer to someone who glances at the home screen; an alert reaches the birder who is at work when the rarity is reported twenty minutes away. This is the natural next step on the widget foundation, and the user asked for it in these terms.

## The User Problem
A birder with years of eBird data has a life list the app can already subtract from eBird's recent reports, but finding out about a nearby lifer still requires opening the app, the widget, or eBird's own alert emails, which do not know the user's list. The moment that matters is the one where the report is fresh and the bird is still there. The user wants that moment delivered to them, on their terms: only if they opt in, only at a cadence they set, never during hours they set aside, and with a record they can look back at.

## Success Criteria
- A user who never opens Settings → Alerts sees no permission prompt, no notification, no background request and no location read caused by this feature, on every platform.
- A user who turns Alerts on, grants notifications, and picks a place and radius receives a notification the first time a check finds a species eBird reported in that radius in the last day that is not in their eBird backup, and the same species does not notify them again for the next seven days.
- Between the quiet hours the user set, no notification is delivered; anything found in that window is delivered once the quiet hours end, and it is in the inbox either way.
- The inbox shows each alert with the species, the place, the distance, when eBird reported it and when the app alerted, newest first, and a tap opens the Map Explorer's Nearby Lifers view centered on that sighting (the widget tap-through precedent).
- Turning Alerts off stops every background check and every pending notification immediately; the inbox stays until the user clears it, its entries age out, or the eBird backup is cleared.
- Loading a newer eBird backup that now contains an alerted species stops that species from alerting again without any other action.
- With no eBird key, no eBird backup, or no connection, a check sends nothing and the Alerts screen says which of the three is missing, in the same register as the widgets' "Add your eBird API key".
- The cadence words shown in Settings are true of what iOS actually does: "Hourly" reads as about once an hour and "Daily" as about once a day, and the screen says that iOS decides exactly when a background check runs.
- The privacy policy, the App Store What's New and the listing's privacy answers describe the feature accurately before the release that carries it ships, each approved by the user in the exact words.

## Scope
- iPhone and iPad only (the "iOS" in the user's name for it). The Mac and Windows desktop apps and the self-hosted web/Pi version get nothing in v1, not even the inbox; the Settings section is absent there rather than disabled.
- An **Alerts** section on the Settings tab, off by default, containing: the on/off switch; cadence (Hourly, Daily); quiet hours (a start and end time, off by default); location model (Fixed place, or My location); the fixed place (defaulting to the saved Default Location, with the app's existing coordinate entry, place-name search and "Use my location" controls); radius (up to 25 miles, the Search this area ceiling and the widgets' fixed radius; default 25); and the inbox.
- The notification permission is requested only when the user turns the switch on, and the location permission only when the user picks My location or presses "Use my location"; a denied permission is stated on the Alerts screen with the system-settings path, and the feature degrades rather than nags (notifications denied: checks still fill the inbox; location denied under My location: the check measures from the fixed place if one is set, otherwise it does not run and says so).
- A background check, at most once per chosen cadence, that makes the app's existing eBird nearby-sightings request (`data/obs/geo/recent`, last one day, the chosen point and radius, the user's key) and subtracts the recorded species set from the user's loaded eBird backup. A check also runs when the user opens the app with Alerts on and the cadence interval has elapsed, so the feature works reliably on the schedule iOS is least generous with.
- What alerts: a species-level eBird report (non-countable forms excluded through the app's shared countability rule: spuhs, slashes, hybrids, undescribed and domestic forms; a subspecies-level form folds to its parent species) whose species is not in the recorded set, and that has not been alerted in the last seven days. Every hit from one check goes into the inbox; one notification per check summarizes them ("3 lifers reported near Davis" naming up to three species), never one notification per species.
- The inbox, device-local, never synced, capped at 30 days and 200 entries with the oldest evicted first; a Clear control; cleared with the eBird backup through the existing clear-derived teardown, because its rows exist only relative to that file.
- In-app help in `docs/HELP.md`, written in the build without a stop.
- HELD proposals, prepared in the pipeline folder and applied only on the user's express yes: the privacy-policy change (`PRIVACY_POLICY.md` and its `website/privacy.html` mirror), the App Store What's New and any listing privacy-answer change, and at most one proposed sentence for the README and website. None is written by a build on its own.

## Out of Scope
- Any push server, developer-operated relay, or account. The founding brief forbids a developer-run server; the only way to guarantee a literal "every hour" is such a server, so exactness is not promised (see Key Decisions).
- "Always" location authorization, significant-location-change monitoring, background location updates, or any location read while the app is not running. v1 never asks for more than the When In Use grant the widgets already use.
- Mac, Windows and web/Pi notifications or inbox.
- Escapee exclusion in the alert rule (it needs a per-checklist eBird lookup the widgets and the Nearby Lifers view also do not make; a species eBird later tags an escapee may alert).
- Media-target alerts (a photo/audio/video the user lacks), hotspot alerts, rarity-only or ABA-code filtering, per-species mute lists, multiple saved places, and a cadence finer than hourly.
- Lock-screen, StandBy or Live Activity surfaces; a new top-level tab; changes to the existing widgets.
- Syncing alert settings or the inbox through iCloud Sync (settings are device-local by standing decision; the inbox follows them).
- eBird's own rare-bird alert emails: untouched and unreferenced; this is not a replacement for them.

## Key Decisions
- **Off by default and reachable only from Settings is a founding decision of this feature, for privacy.** No onboarding step, banner, tab badge or widget entry point turns it on or asks for a permission; the first system prompt of any kind appears only after the user flips the switch. A future build that wants to promote the feature argues against this line rather than moving it.
- **Cadence is stated as "about hourly" and "about daily", never as an exact interval, because iOS background refresh is opportunistic and the app runs no server.** The app asks iOS for a check no sooner than the chosen interval and runs one itself whenever the user opens the app after the interval has elapsed; the Settings copy says iOS chooses the exact time. Success criteria are written to this truth. A push server would make the interval exact and is a strategic change (a developer-operated service), not an implementation detail, and is out of scope.
- **Quiet hours defer delivery; they do not hide findings.** A check may still run inside quiet hours and its hits still enter the inbox; the notification is scheduled for the end of the quiet window (a local notification with a time trigger is reliable in a way the background fetch is not). "Quiet" is a promise about interruptions, which is the promise the user can feel.
- **Two location models ship, and "My location" means the device's most recent known position under When In Use, never a background track.** Fixed place is the primary model and defaults to the saved Default Location. My location reads a fresh position when the app is open and, for a background check, uses the most recent position the app (or a widget refresh, which already records the area it searched) has seen. The app does not request Always authorization, because the published privacy sentence "the app itself uses your location only while you're using it" is a posture the user chose, Always carries App Review and disclosure weight beyond what the widgets established, and the fixed-place model covers the case that matters most (home, work, a patch). Flagged for the user: if they want true live position while the phone sits in a pocket, that is an Always-authorization decision to make deliberately, not something a build should slide into.
- **What counts as a lifer alert is the Nearby Lifers subtraction plus the shared countability rule, and the countability step is a deliberate difference from the widget and map list.** The widgets and the Nearby Lifers view show every eBird report not in the recorded set, spuhs included (DECISIONS, v1.0.36: countability exclusion "would be a Map Explorer change first"). A list row can carry "gull sp."; a notification that interrupts the user's day cannot, so the alert rule applies `isNonCountableForm` before it alerts. The difference is stated in the help and is one line of code should the map and widgets later follow.
- **Dedupe is per species over seven days, one notification per check.** A species that alerted does not alert again until seven days after its last alert, whatever location it is next reported at; the inbox row is updated with the newer sighting rather than duplicated. A loaded backup that now contains the species removes it from the candidate set automatically because the recorded set is re-derived from the file. The seven-day figure is a product choice for v1 (a rarity that lingers is worth one weekly reminder, not an hourly one) and is a single constant.
- **The inbox is device-local, bounded and derived from the user's file.** 30 days and 200 entries, oldest first out; never synced (iCloud Sync carries the two data files and the bar-chart files only; settings, and therefore the inbox beside them, stay on the device); registered with the clear-derived teardown because a row is meaningful only relative to the eBird backup it was subtracted from. Turning Alerts off keeps the inbox, because a user who pauses alerts for a trip should not lose their record; Clear is explicit.
- **New network behavior, stated in the durable form:** this feature adds no third-party request, no new endpoint and no new host; it makes the app's existing eBird nearby-sightings request, with the user's own key, from a background check on iPhone and iPad at most once per chosen cadence, and it moves that request into a context where the webview is not running. Nothing is sent to the developer. The eBird terms already cover this request family.
- **Platform scope is iPhone and iPad, and the desktop and web versions get nothing rather than a stub.** Notifications on Mac are feasible but were not asked for, and the web/Pi build has no background process of its own; naming the omission keeps a later "Mac alerts" build a decision rather than a drift.
- **Published copy is HELD, in every run and spin.** The privacy policy (Your Location, Connections, iOS App sections), the App Store What's New, the listing's privacy answers and any README or website sentence are prepared as proposals in `pipeline/ios-alerts/` and shown to the user rendered; nothing lands without an express yes. `docs/HELP.md` and in-app text need no stop.
- **Questions for The Architect, not designed here:** (1) whether the background check runs natively (Rust or Swift, beside the widget extension's existing Swift lifer subtraction and hand-over reader) or by waking the webview, given that a BGAppRefreshTask has seconds, not the webview's boot time, and that the hand-over document already carries the key and the recorded set; (2) how a native check honors the app's eBird pacing contract when the shared JS gate is not running (one request per check bounds it, but say so); (3) whether "most recent known position" is read from the widget's stored search area, the app's saved position, or both, and what its age limit is; (4) where the inbox document lives so both the native check and the webview can write it under the single-webview and ordered-writer rules; (5) that the widget's App Group reader set, and any new native file, get the same security treatment the v1.0.36 review gave the hand-over.
- **Verification posture:** background refresh timing and real notification delivery can only be partly shown in a simulator (a BGTask can be triggered from the debugger; delivery timing and quiet-hour deferral on a locked phone cannot). This is a stated risk for the Tester and the deploy gate, not a manual sweep to add; the user performs any physical-device check, as the device boundary rule directs, and the TestFlight device pass is where cadence and quiet hours are seen for real.
