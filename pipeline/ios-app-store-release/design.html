Name and subtitle

The name is plain SnowRaven, verified for availability in App Store Connect before any other metadata work; a qualified fallback is used only if forced, and logged.

The subtitle Your birding data, explored (27 of 30 characters) says whose data it is and what the app does with it, in the site's register. Per the voice rules, neither field mentions eBird or Macaulay; those names appear only in the description and keywords, as compatibility statements.

The privacy label is the second headline

For the birder in US-02, "Data Not Collected" is the claim that decides the install. The sketch places the App Privacy card directly after the description so the label and the policy link read as part of the pitch, which for this app they genuinely are.

Category: Reference, then Weather

Primary Reference: the app is a reference companion over your own records (life list, species history, breeding codes, taxonomy). Secondary Weather: the founding feature is the weather and tide lookup. Alternatives considered: Lifestyle (too broad, says nothing) and Navigation (the map serves the data, not routing). Say the word if you want either swapped.

Version: 1.0.0 for the debut

The public debut ships as 1.0.0 on every platform at once, a deliberate one-time jump from 0.5.x (user decision at the design review). The single-version lockstep is unchanged, and after this release the regular incremental rhythm continues upward from 1.0.0; versions never go back below it, since Apple and the desktop updater both require each release's number to increase.

Description

2,706 / 4,000
SnowRaven is a birding data explorer for the records you already keep. Import your own eBird backup and Macaulay Library export, and years of checklists become maps, statistics, and histories you can wander through in the field or at home. Everything runs on your device. There is no account to create, no server in the middle, and nothing is collected about you. WHAT'S INSIDE • Weather & Tide: paste an eBird checklist ID and get a clean, ready-to-paste weather summary, with the historical tide from the nearest NOAA station. Current conditions and forecasts work too. • Statistics: life list totals and growth, firsts and milestones, temporal and geographic patterns, media documentation coverage, and a few playful lists. • Map Explorer: your sightings on an interactive map, with an optional heatmap, nearby hotspots, county shading, and a breeding atlas overlay. • Species Detail: your complete history with any species, including graphs, top locations, field notes, and your own recent photos, audio, and video. • Calendar: a year of your birding as twelve month grids, each day shaded by how busy it was. • Multimedia: your life list as a media checklist, showing photo, audio, and video coverage for every species. • Breeding Codes: every species you have recorded breeding evidence for, laid out as a color-coded matrix. • Named Birds: tag an individual bird in a species comment and SnowRaven follows it across your checklists. • Checklists and List Comparer: search every comment you have written, and compare lists or checklists side by side. Once your data has loaded, the maps and every analytical tab keep working offline. PRIVACY SnowRaven collects nothing: no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting, no ads, and no accounts. Your files, settings, and API keys stay on your device. When you ask for live data, the app talks directly to the service that has it, using your own free API keys where one is needed. The full privacy policy is at snowraven.dtgibson.com/privacy.html. WHAT YOU'LL NEED Most tools read the free data export you can download from your eBird account, plus an optional Macaulay Library export. Live lookups use two free API keys, entered once in Settings: an eBird key, and an OpenWeather key for weather. Tides, maps, and every offline analysis work without any key. SnowRaven is an independent, free, open source project. It works alongside eBird and the Macaulay Library, building on the exports they let you download, so you can look at your own observations from new angles. It is not affiliated with, or endorsed by, the Cornell Lab of Ornithology. Also available free for Mac and Windows, and as a self-hosted web app, at snowraven.dtgibson.com.

Structure: what it is, the privacy sentence early, the ten tools, offline, privacy in full, what you need (honest about keys), and the alongside-eBird closing with the explicit non-affiliation sentence. Every eBird and Macaulay mention is a compatibility statement about your own data.

Six screenshots, clean, in this order

Real captures of the real app over the synthetic demo dataset (a fictional birder at public northeast-US hotspots), light theme, via the existing capture pipeline. The sketches above stand in for the six; here is the order and why.

#SketchSurfaceWhat it must showWhy this slot
1 Map Explorer Sighting points and hotspot pins around the demo birder's New York area, one hotspot popup open (Jamaica Bay Wildlife Refuge). The first shot is most of the search result. A map of your own birding says the whole idea in one glance, and it is the required map view.
2 Statistics Life list total, checklist count, the growth chart, top species. The demo dataset's 149 species read as a believable life list. Shots 1 and 2 appear together in search results: your birding, mapped and measured. Required statistics view.
3 Weather & Tide A looked-up checklist with the formatted weather block and the NOAA tide, plus the one-press copy action. The founding feature and the one no other birding app has. Placed third so the first swipe lands on it.
4 (calendar sketch in carousel) Calendar A year as twelve shaded month grids; migration weeks visibly busy, mid-summer visibly quiet. The most instantly legible "shape of your year" surface; strong at thumbnail size.
5 Species Detail Northern Cardinal from the demo data: stats, sightings-over-time, the per-species map, media row. Depth: one species, everything you know about it. Answers "what do I get for one bird".
6 (matrix sketch in carousel) Breeding Codes The color-coded species-by-code matrix with its tier legend. Signals serious-birder depth to the reader who swipes all the way; distinct from everything before it.
iPhone set 6.9-inch, portrait, 1320 x 2868 px (Playwright viewport 440 x 956 at scale 3). All six shots, this order. At 440 CSS px the phone tier governs and the tab nav is the dropdown, which is honestly what an iPhone shows.
iPad set 13-inch, portrait, 2064 x 2752 px (viewport 1032 x 1376 at scale 2). The same six surfaces in the same order, re-captured at tablet width so layouts are genuinely iPad (US-04: no stretched phone shots).
Clean frames, no captions The shots ship as unadorned app captures: no marketing frames, no caption bands, no device bezels. Three reasons. The voice is informative, not promotional, and caption bands are where promotional voice creeps in. Each tab already opens with its own titled header, so the screen names itself. And clean captures regenerate byte-for-byte from the committed pipeline when the UI changes, with no per-release caption artwork to maintain or drift.
Capture rules Light theme (the store default). Synthetic demo data only, backend pointed at it with SR_DATA_DIR, never the real export. Config committed beside the existing website/tools/ capture tooling so QA can re-run it. Every visible place and species name must exist in the generated demo dataset.

Privacy

SnowRaven Privacy Policy

Effective date: August 4, 2026 · Updated with the iOS App Store release

App Store privacy label

Data Not Collected

That is SnowRaven's privacy label on the App Store, and this page is the long form of why it is true: no analytics, no telemetry, no accounts, no third-party SDKs, and no server the developer runs. Requests you make go directly from your device to the bird and weather services, with your own keys, and no one retains them.

  • No accountsNothing to sign up for
  • No trackingZero analytics or telemetry
  • Keys & data stay localOn your device only
  • No developer serverThere is no SnowRaven cloud

Overview

SnowRaven is a self-hosted birding tools app. It runs as a standalone desktop app (macOS and Windows), as an app on iPhone and iPad, or on your own machine such as a Raspberry Pi. This policy describes what happens to your data. The short version: it stays with you.

Your Data Stays on Your Device

SnowRaven keeps your data on your own device by choice and design. You keep it, and you control it.

  • Your API keys, app settings, and the files you upload (your eBird backup and Macaulay Library export) are stored only on your device (in the desktop app's local data directory, or in the iOS app's on-device sandbox on iPhone and iPad), or on your own machine when you self-host the web/Pi version. They are never uploaded to the developer or to any service the developer runs.
  • All processing of your eBird and media files happens locally, on your own device, while you use the app.
  • There is no SnowRaven account, no login, and no SnowRaven-operated server sitting between you and your data.
  • You can delete your stored files and keys at any time from the Settings tab, or by removing the app's data directory.

No Data Collection

SnowRaven collects nothing about you.

  • No analytics, usage tracking, or telemetry of any kind.
  • No crash-reporting services.
  • No advertising networks.
  • No accounts, profiles, or identifiers.

Design choices worth naming

The lead band reuses the homepage's dark privacy band, so the two pages read as one site and the "Data Not Collected" story is the first thing on screen, per US-02. The band is presentation only; the h2 sections below it mirror PRIVACY_POLICY.md exactly, so the recommended parity test has a clean structure to assert against.

One sequencing observation for the build, not a redesign: the schema places the iOS policy additions in Phase B (on approval), but Apple's reviewer reads this page during review. My recommendation is to ship the iOS App section and the two marked additions in Phase A with the page itself, since they describe behavior that is already true on TestFlight today. Flagged for the Engineer and the decisions record either way.
