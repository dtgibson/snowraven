02

What a pressable mark costs, and how it is paid

The shipped decoration contract cannot survive an interactive mark, so it is not patched. Four problems, four positions.

Problem 1 — the strip stops being decoration

Both strips become real listboxes. Neither is inert any more.

TAKEN: full a11y participation

A control that responds to a pointer but is invisible to assistive technology and unreachable by keyboard fails WCAG 2.1.1 outright, so there is no half measure available: the moment a mark answers a tap, the strip owes a role, a name, keyboard operation and focus. The aria-hidden plus inert wrapper goes, on both strips.

Each strip becomes a role="listbox" whose options are the marks. Each mark's accessible name is the whole payload — Jun 15, 2026, Pierce and Washington on a card, with the bird's name in front on the bottom strip — so a screen reader user never needs the visible line at all. Position in the set comes from aria-posinset and aria-setsize rather than from words in the name.

The duplication is real and is accepted. The card's strip lists dates and places that its report rows, twelve pixels below, also list — with the checklist link and the species comment the strip does not carry. So the strip is a strict subset: a fast overview first, the full record second, which is the reading order the approved layout already produces. The report rows do not change. The alternative, suppressing the strip for screen readers, is the option WCAG removes.

Problem 2 — tab stops

Two. Not two hundred, and not two per bird.

TAKEN: aria-activedescendant, one focusable per strip

The repo has two shipped keyboard patterns for a group of controls under one tab stop, and its own roster distinguishes them. Roving tabindex is the main navigation's tablist and the Settings radio groups. An aria-activedescendant listbox is SpeciesCombobox, where one focusable owns a long, data‑scaled option list and the arrow keys move an index rather than focus.

I took the second, and the population size is the reason. Roving tabindex puts a tabIndex attribute and a focus handler on every mark. At the performance target the PRD already names — 200 birds averaging 100 sightings — that is up to 20,000 marked elements, and if they were buttons they would all join tabOrderCoverage.test.ts's counted population under one blanket roster row, which is exactly the pardon that roster's docstring warns against. With activedescendant the marks are <div role="option"> with no tabIndex, no focus handler and no click handler, so they are outside that guard's population by element type — the same reason the roster gives for leaving SnowMap's Trails checkbox out.

The whole track is the control. One focusable per strip: the card's strip is one listbox, and the bottom strip is one listbox containing a role="group" per bird. So the Named Birds tab gains exactly two tab stops — one for the open card's strip, one for the bottom strip — and that number is constant. It does not move with the number of birds or the number of sightings.

Keys, on both: Left and Right step sightings, Home and End jump to the ends of the current bird, Up and Down move between birds on the bottom strip and choose the nearest sighting in date terms, and Escape clears. Escape is consumed only while something is selected, so with nothing selected it still reaches an outer layer — the same rule SpeciesCombobox already follows.

Problem 3 — overlapping marks that are now targets

The mark is not the target. The track is.

TAKEN: nearest-sighting hit model

Marks must still be allowed to overlap and coincide, and on this data they do: Bridge‑Ravens has six dates inside eight days, which on a 320px phone is six marks inside six pixels. A 3px mark is not a target and never can be, and every fix that makes it one — spacing, jitter, binning — is explicitly forbidden and would falsify the picture.

So the marks keep pointer-events: none and stay purely visual, and the track takes the press. A press anywhere selects the nearest sighting by horizontal position, which partitions the entire width: every sighting owns a slice, no slice is zero, and overlap becomes irrelevant to hit testing. The target is the full width of the track by its full height — 38px in a card, 24px per lane on the bottom strip and 30px at the phone tier, so the 24px minimum is cleared in every tier.

The honest residual limit, stated: where several sightings sit within a few pixels, a finger cannot land on each one individually. The arrow keys reach every one; the readout's second line says 4 of 9 dates so the neighbours are visible rather than hidden; and the card's strip sits directly above the complete dated report list, which is why that ordering was chosen. If you want previous and next buttons beside the readout, that is the fix, and it costs four more permanent tab stops.

Problem 4 — where the label appears

A fixed line under the strip, never a floating tooltip.

TAKEN: fixed readout, two lines, always present

A popover anchored to a 3px mark at 320px has nowhere to go: it overflows the card, it needs collision logic, and on a dense cluster it covers the marks it is describing. The readout instead sits in one fixed place under the axis labels, in normal flow, and wraps. It is rendered from the start and never unmounted, so nothing shifts when it fills — the shipped SyncLine rule — and because it is text it lives outside every fixed‑px box.

Line one carries the identity: date and place in a card, with the bird's name in front on the bottom strip. Line two is the muted position, 4 of 9 dates, which also quietly explains why a card saying 8 sightings can have 7 marks. At rest line one is Select a sighting to see its date and place. — the only thing on the surface that announces the new affordance.

It is never a link. The app opens external links only through the shared link components, and a readout that becomes a checklist link would be a control appearing and disappearing under the user's finger. The checklist is on the report row below, where it already is.

Pointer, touch and keyboard resolve to the same outcome. A mouse hover previews without committing, so a mouse user can scrub a cluster and read it without a click. A press — mouse or finger — commits and moves focus to the track, so the arrows carry on from there. There is no hover on iOS, which is exactly why the commit is on press and not on hover. Blur clears the selection; so does Escape; so does collapsing the card.

No live region, still

The readout is aria-hidden. The focused option already announces the same words, so a live region here would double‑speak every arrow press. That keeps FR‑37 intact and is worth stating in the component so a later reader does not add one back as an oversight.

What holds it together

The lane grid collapses from label | track to a stacked label over track, so a 120‑character name has the full column and nothing needs a min-width. 06

The cases that break charts

Three constructed birds, because the reference export has none of these shapes. Names are the app's own documented examples.

A · every sighting on one date — a sentence, not a strip

Nothing to select, so nothing pretends to be selectable

Two sightings, one date, range on Last sighting: the axis is zero days wide, so there is no strip, no listbox, no tab stop and no readout — one sentence, reusing the shipped ranked‑list idiom (Every sighting at {place}. becomes Every sighting on {date}.). Flip to Today and the same bird has a real axis, so the strip appears with its mark at the left edge and becomes selectable.

B · one sighting — no strip in the card, but a lane below

Deliberately inconsistent, and worth saying so

In a card a one‑mark strip is a chart of a fact already stated two lines above. In the bottom strip the same lane sits in a comparison with other lanes and carries real information, and it is now selectable like any other. Same rule about single facts, opposite outcome, because the surrounding context differs.

C · a 120‑character name, the tag limit

The label column is the constraint, and it is also an accessible name

NAME_TAG_RE bounds a tag at 120 characters. That string is now doing three jobs: the visible label, the lane group's accessible name, and the front of every mark's accessible name in that lane. It wraps, it carries no nowrap, and on a phone it stops being a column at all. Set the text size to 200% and the width to 320px and this is the row to watch.

08

Still deliberately absent

No motion inside either strip

Selection is instant. Flipping the range re‑scales the axis instantly. A transition on mark positions would animate left on up to a few hundred elements on a phone, and it is a data change rather than an entrance. The only motion this feature adds is the 120ms ease‑out colour transition on the range pills, and it rides the one global reduced‑motion block.

No gridlines, no tick labels, no year marks

A strip spanning seven years renders identically to one spanning six weeks. At 320px a full axis is under 300px, so any subdivision is noise before it is information, and text inside the plot is the one thing the reflow requirement cannot survive.

No links from a mark, and no popovers

The readout is text. Opening a checklist stays where it already is, on the report row. The strip describes; the rows act.

Two things I added that no requirement asked for

The span line — the accent segment from a bird's first sighting to its last, under its marks. One min and one max over positions the lane already computes, and it is what turns "which birds was I following when" into a readable answer.

Mouse hover preview. Genuinely useful for scrubbing a cluster, and free on desktop. It has no touch equivalent by construction, which is why commit is on press. Cut it and nothing else changes.
