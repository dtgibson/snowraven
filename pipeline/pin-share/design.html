Copy mode

Stage 4 · The Designer

Drop a pin. Send the spot.

Right-click or long-press any birding map, and the exact place you pressed becomes a message you can text to another birder. Nothing leaves the device to make it.

Everything below is live. Right-click the maps, drag the pins, press the copy buttons, and switch the theme and the copy mode from the bar above.

01

The share pin and its popup

Right-click (or press and hold on touch) anywhere on the map. The pin plants where you pressed, the popup opens with the coordinates already on screen, and one press puts the share block on the clipboard.

Map Explorer My Sightings

Why a flag, not another teardrop

Species Detail, Named Birds and the Statistics map are already full of accent-green teardrops, and Map Explorer adds green, slate, amber and violet circles. Every hue is spoken for on at least one surface, so color cannot carry the distinction. Shape can. A planted flag reads as something the user put there a moment ago, its base is a precise point rather than a fat bulb, and it is unmistakable next to a teardrop at a glance.

Popup anatomy

Coordinates
Monospace, tabular, selectable. Five decimals, latitude first. This is the datum, so it leads.
Copy button
Accent filled. Its label names the active mode, so a coordinates-only copy is never a surprise.
Mode line
One muted line under the button restating what the payload contains.
Close
The map popup's own close control. It removes the pin and the popup together, and returns focus to whatever opened it.
Drag
The pin drags to fine-tune. The popup follows and the coordinates update on release. Nothing is copied by dragging.

Copy is always an explicit press. Dropping, moving and dragging a pin never touch the clipboard. On the search views the same gesture also re-runs a search, so a silent clipboard overwrite would be doubly unwelcome.

03

The keyboard route

Right-click and long-press are both pointer gestures. Three of the five surfaces have no latitude and longitude fields to borrow a keyboard path from, so the pin needs a control of its own. That control turns out to solve a second problem: a gesture nobody can see is a feature almost nobody finds.

One control, one corner, every surface

A 36px map tool in the bottom-right corner that plants the pin at the center of the current view. On Map Explorer it joins the row that already holds Fullscreen and Filters, so no new corner is claimed and the iOS safe-area handling comes along for free. On the other four maps that corner is empty, and the same button sits there alone.

  1. Tab to the map. MapLibre's canvas is already focusable.
  2. Pan with the arrow keys, zoom with + and -, until the spot is in the middle.
  3. Tab to the pin button and press Enter. The pin plants at the center and focus moves into the popup.
  4. Tab to Copy, press Enter. The result is announced.
  5. Esc clears the pin and the popup and returns focus to the pin button.

The accent-tinted state is the app's existing "active" convention, and it doubles as a persistent signal that this map is holding a pin. The label changes with the state rather than staying vague, because "drop a pin" would be a small lie once one exists.

Not on the three search views. Hotspots, Nearby Lifers and Media Targets already have latitude and longitude fields and a single search-center pin. Adding a second way to move that center would be a competing control, so those views keep their existing route and the button does not appear.

04

Compact density

The per-individual map inside a Named Birds card is 220px tall and often half the page wide. It gets the same popup with tighter padding, a smaller coordinate line and a shorter scroll cap, at full meaning rather than an icon-only reduction. Density is a required choice at every call site, never a silent default.

05

The search views keep their pin

On Hotspots, Nearby Lifers and Media Targets, right-click and long-press are already taken: they set the search center. Rather than compete, the existing center pin becomes a real button. A drop still just re-runs the search and looks exactly as it does today. Click or focus the pin, and the copy popup opens.

Map Explorer Hotspots Click the search center pin, or drag it

Dragging is not activating. Fine-tuning the center by dragging must not pop the copy popup, and a pointer drag can end in a synthesized click. The pin's accessible name leads with its coordinates exactly as the latitude and longitude fields show them, then names the action: "38.54321, -121.98765. Copy this location".

06

What lands on the clipboard

Three lines by default, one line if that is what you asked for. Built as local strings from the numbers already on the device. Nothing is fetched, shortened, resolved or checked, so no coordinate leaves the machine and the privacy policy needs no new entry.

Example 

        

      

    


    

      
The example is the whole argument for a preview. "Copy coordinates and map links" describes a payload; the example is the payload. It reuses the pattern the Weather tab already uses, where the block you are about to copy is shown before you copy it.

      
The default is on, and that is deliberate. The app's usual off-by-default posture exists for options with a network or privacy cost. This one has neither: the links are built locally and nothing is fetched, so the richer mode is a strict superset with no downside and it matches the stated use, which is sharing by text.

    

  


  
  

    
08
Motion

    
Every movement here explains a state change and nothing decorates a static screen. All of it is under 200ms, all of it eases out, and all of it goes near-instant when the operating system asks for reduced motion.

    

      

        
Pin plant
190ms, ease-out, rises 7px into place with a fade. It says something was placed here, without a bounce.

        
Popup open
170ms, ease-out, scales from 0.94 with its transform origin at the pin tip, so it grows out of the pin rather than appearing over it.

        
Copy result
140ms color transition from filled to tinted. No pulse, no flash.

        
Failure reveal
190ms, ease-out, a 4px rise with a fade as the text appears.

        
Pin button
140ms color transition into and out of the active state.

        
Reduced motion
Everything collapses to about 1 microsecond through the rule already in globals.css. The end state is identical, so nothing is lost.
