Feasibility sketch, not a final design

Recorded names in splits and lumps

How to read this page. Anything inside a dashed frame is what the app would draw, in its own type, color and spacing. Anything outside a frame, in this typewriter face, is a note from the designer.

Report counts are illustrative. No backup was read to make this page. The species, the updates and their dates are real, from the taxonomy files the app already ships from.

What a box can say. The name eBird had for a report until an update, worked out from the report's date (and, for a lump, from the form the report still carries). Never the name that was typed: the backup holds one name per report, today's, and no entry date.

Split: Whimbrel, October 2025

Every report now under Hudsonian Whimbrel or Eurasian Whimbrel and dated on or before 31 October 2025 was under Whimbrel until that day. That much the data supports. Whether a given report was entered as plain Whimbrel or as the form Whimbrel (Hudsonian) is not in the backup.

Variant B the same, with the reports laid out by year

Changed from A: the thin bar in every counted box unfolds into one column per year. The fills keep the bar's meaning (gray is dated on or before the update, green is dated after it), every box shares one year axis and one height scale, and a caret marks the update's year. Read left to right it shows when the Whimbrel reports were made and, on the right, which years went to which new name. Hover a column for its figure; in the app each year's figure would also be in the text a screen reader receives.

Lump: Western Flycatcher, October 2023

eBird kept each lumped species as a named form, so an older report now reads Western Flycatcher (Pacific-slope) or Western Flycatcher (Cordilleran), and that maps back to the species name it had. A report with no form (before the lump it was the slash Pacific-slope/Cordilleran Flycatcher, or the form has since been removed) cannot be mapped back, and the chart gives those their own dashed box instead of guessing. Today both earlier boxes read "No reports under this name".

Rename: Thick-billed Longspur, September 2021

Same bird, one name before and one after: McCown's Longspur became Thick-billed Longspur between the 2019 and 2021 taxonomy files. Renames are not in the section today; for this species it reads only "No split or lump is recorded for this species in the covered updates."

New here: a third kind word, Rename, with a one-in, one-out glyph, and "renamed by eBird" in place of "reassigned by eBird". The section's title and its empty line would both need new words if renames join; that is copy for a later stage and is left as shipped here.

A lump the backup cannot trace: American Crow, September 2021

When eBird lumped Northwestern Crow into American Crow it kept no Northwestern form, so a report that was Northwestern Crow now reads plain American Crow and is indistinguishable from one that always was. There is nothing to count on the left, in either variant, and the section says so in its existing note register instead of leaving two silent boxes.

What neither variant can show

What was typed. Plain Whimbrel or the form Whimbrel (Hudsonian): the backup does not say, and no box claims it.

When it was typed. A report entered in 2026 for a 2019 sighting counts with the earlier name, as it already does in today's "reassigned by eBird" figure. The second paragraph under each chart now says so.

A lump that left no form. Shown above. It is the minority: a quick check of the taxonomy files finds about a quarter of the 101 covered lumps leave at least one earlier name with no form to trace.

The day a rename took effect. The yearly files show which update a new name first appears in, not the day eBird began using it. A name changed between two updates would be dated to the later one.

Anything before the 2019 update. A name that changed in 2017 is outside what the app holds, so every box is worded as eBird's name "until the update", never as the name on the day of the sighting.
