Pan the map. Search what you can see.

One labelled control on the map surface re-runs the active view's eBird search over the viewport you are looking at. And because a circle can never be a rectangle, the map says plainly which part of what you see was actually covered.

01

The four calls

Where the control lives

Its own row in the FAB cluster

I built the top-centre pill first, because that is the idiom and it is what the PRD assumed. Measured in Chromium at 320px it puts a persistent, tappable control over the layers switcher: 206 by 31px at 100% text scale and 294 by 148px at 200%, where it buries the switcher completely. That disqualifies it. The split that works is by interactivity, not by feature: the top-centre anchor keeps only transient pointer-events: none statements (the shipped loading chip, and now the outcome line), and the control goes where this map's other actions already live, as its own full-width row in the FAB cluster. It consumes none of that row's measured 4.00px of slack, the cluster grows upward so no shipped button moves, and it inherits the .sr-ios-app bottom inset a bottom-anchored control needs rather than adding a new rule. Measured overlap with the switcher: zero, at every scale.

What the searched area looks like

Dim what wasn't searched; dash the edge

A ring alone would be invisible in the ordinary case: a covering radius circumscribes the viewport, so straight after a press the circle is off screen entirely. Dimming the ground outside the circle inverts that: nothing dims when the search covers what you see, and the moment it does not, the uncovered ground greys out. The edge itself is a dashed red-orange ring over a soft halo, dash being the one line idiom this map does not already use for a real boundary.

How a failure reads in 96px

Same element, error tokens, retry never moves

Not a second card in a second place. The outcome region is one element that is a pill when the text is short and a block when it is long; a failure only changes its colour tokens and adds an icon. The 96px figure was measured against the location-failure strings, which are long instructions; the longest message this feature can produce is 73 characters and wraps to four lines at 320px and 200%. The retry is the control, which has already returned to the exact place it was before the press, because the record was not written and the cluster never moves a button. Nothing is truncated, duplicated into a hidden announcer, or sent somewhere else to be read.

Motion

One curve, 190ms, arrive only

The control arrives with the app's own shipped map-chrome entrance (190ms cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1), from sr-map-geo-in), growing upward out of the cluster it is anchored to. It has no exit: the chip appearing is the feedback, and a fade would delay it. The area indicator fades up over 220ms with the dashed edge landing 60ms behind the halo, so the ground settles and then the edge sharpens. A result line clears itself after six seconds; a failure stays until the next search.

04

Anatomy

The control

  • Visible text is exactly “Search this area” on all three views, with a Lucide Search glyph at 14px. The accessible name adds the view: …for hotspots, …for recent sightings, …for nearby lifers, each containing the visible text verbatim.
  • Colour is accent ink on --sr-accent-bg inside a --sr-accent-border-strong hairline: the app's own shipped “pressed / active” treatment, not a new one. Deliberately not an accent-filled slab, because solid accent circles on this canvas are sighting pins.
  • Shape is the loading chip's 20px pill with the same shadow, so all the floating chrome on this canvas reads as one family.
  • Touch reaches 2.75rem min-height in the ≤640 tier and scales with in-app text, matching the FAB row.
  • The retained state (focus held through a press) keeps the same box in muted tokens with aria-disabled, never the disabled attribute.

The searched area

  • Two new tokens, both map-anchored and declared identically in both themes, the same posture as --sr-share-pin and the county ramp: --sr-search-area-rgb and --sr-search-area-scrim-rgb.
  • Red-orange is the one free hue on this canvas. Green is pins, amber is personal locations, violet is targets, blue is rank, purple is the breeding ramp, green again is the county ramp, slate is every boundary line.
  • Three layers, all inert. A scrim fill outside the circle, a soft halo stroke, and the dashed edge, inserted below the marker layers and named in neither interaction list.
  • The scrim is always on, not just when capped. When the search covers the view it is entirely off screen and costs nothing; the moment you zoom out or pan, it appears exactly where it means something.
  • The centre needs no new mark. The shipped search-centre pin is already there, and an accent dot inside a red-orange ring reads as centre-and-extent without a word of explanation.
--sr-search-area #B4341F scrim at 13%

The outcome line

  • One element, two variants. Neutral surface for a result, error tokens plus an AlertCircle for a failure. Nothing about the shape changes; only its colours and how many lines the text needs.
  • It is the announced node. No duplicate .sr-only announcer, no truncation, no second location. What is read is what is on screen.
  • Every route announces, not just this control, so the sidebar Find button and a dropped centre pin get the same confirmation on a phone where the sidebar is not on screen.
  • Cleared at the top of each handler, before the fetch, so the slot is empty exactly while the loading chip occupies it. That is what makes single occupancy structural rather than remembered.
  • A result clears itself after six seconds; a failure does not. A count is a confirmation and the ring on the map is the durable answer, so the line has no reason to become permanent furniture over the layers switcher. A failure is an unresolved state and stays until the next search.

Motion

  • Control arrives — opacity and translateY(8px) scale(0.965) to rest, 190ms, cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1), transform-origin: bottom center: it grows up out of the cluster. The top-anchored outcome line does the opposite, from translateY(-6px).
  • Control leaves — instantly, no exit. The chip taking its place is the feedback; a fade would only delay it.
  • Outcome line — the same 190ms entrance, replayed for free on a repeat because the sequence-keyed child remounts. The visual cue and the announcement stay in lockstep by construction.
  • The area — a 220ms opacity rise on the scrim and halo with the dashed edge landing 60ms behind, so the ground settles and then the edge sharpens. Delivered as MapLibre paint-property transitions (line-opacity-transition), not a rAF loop.
  • Reduced motion — the DOM pieces inherit the app's global collapse for free, because every end state is the resting state. The GL transitions are not covered by CSS, so the layer must read matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)') in its effect and use a duration of 0.
  • No hover-scale, no pulse, no stagger. Hover is a 120ms background shift and nothing else.

What was measured, and what still needs measuring

Every geometric claim on this page was measured in Chromium against these frames, element box and text ink against the map area's content box, never document.scrollWidth. That is what rejected the top-centre pill: it read as fine in the markup and buried the layers switcher on screen.

Two things still need measuring against the real app rather than this reproduction. The cluster's height with the control row added, at 200% on the shortest supported phone, including a simultaneous location-failure row. And the scrim's 18% wash over satellite imagery and over an active county or atlas shading ramp, where a dark base and a tinted overlay both eat it.
