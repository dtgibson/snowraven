Stage 4 · The Designer

The weather record, on the bird’s own page

A Weather card on Species Detail, showing the skies and temperatures you have the selected bird in, each row carrying the same band’s share of all your outings beside it. No picker. Two shares of two different wholes, and nothing divides one by the other.

Every figure below is from your own export as it sits on this machine today: 3,252 checklists, 353 of them carrying a readable weather block, 168 species in the published table. The five birds in the stepper are real, and so are their arrays and their totals.

The controls at the top of this page are review instruments, not product. Use them to check both themes, 320px, and 200% in-app text scale, which is where this pattern is hardest.

1. The card, in its slot, live

The tab’s neighbouring sections are drawn head-only so the slot is legible without simulating the whole page. Step between the four birds to see the card move between its states.

OQ-05 · the slot

After Comments, above Named Individuals. This is the Planner’s default and I am taking it, for three reasons rather than by deferral. It closes the run of sections built from your own records of this bird; everything below it (Named Individuals, Recent Media) is about a different subject. It keeps the Top Locations → Sighting Locations pair intact, which a higher slot would split. And because the sentence, not the chart, is the ordinary outcome, a quiet state belongs low on the page where it costs a scroll rather than a glance.

What to look at

House Finch is the case where the confound is loudest: on 308 of your 353 blocked checklists, so its shape is almost your shape, and every reference figure says so. It is also where the group denominators earn their wording — 306 of its 308 carry a sky condition while 308 of its 308 carry a temperature, because a block can record a temperature and no sky.

Steller’s Jay is the case where the chart says something. Eleven of its eighteen are overcast against 39% of your outings; three of its eighteen are in the 32–44°F band against 2% of your outings, which is three of the six cold outings you have. The muted figure is what lets you see that without anything dividing one number by the other.

Western Bluebird is the below-floor state doing its real work. It is on five weather-block checklists, which against your 353 sounds like a coverage problem; against the 97 checklists you actually have the bird on it is simply a bird you saw many times before you started writing blocks. Same numerator, two wholes.

American White Pelican and Wood Duck are the zero case, and they are the ordinary one: 225 of your 282 species get a sentence rather than a chart. Wood Duck is also the typical shape of a zero-block bird here — the median total across those 114 species is two checklists, and 49 of them sit at exactly one.

3. The three questions, answered

OQ-05 · the exact slot

After Comments, above Named Individuals — the Planner’s default, taken on its merits

Three reasons, each of which would be enough. It closes the run of sections built from the bird’s own records, so the two sections below it (Named Individuals, Recent Media) stay a distinct group about something else. It does not split the Top Locations → Sighting Locations pair, which is a list and then the map of the same fact and reads as one thing. And a state that is a quiet sentence four birds in five belongs where a reader arrives at it rather than where they land on it.

The higher slot — immediately after Sighting Locations — was the real alternative and I rejected it: it puts the card’s ordinary state on the path of every reader on every bird, which trains the eye to skip the card entirely by the time it has something to show.

OQ-06 · one axis at a time, or both

Both groups, side by side, no toggle

The brief permits the axis toggle to travel, but there is nothing to travel: the Statistics per-species view has no toggle. Its Sky / Temperature control governs the band chart above it, not the pair. Adding one here would not be inheriting a control, it would be inventing one on the surface whose whole premise is that you should not have to operate anything to get the answer.

The pair fits. .sr-wx-pair is an auto-fit grid with a min(240px, 100%) track, so it is two columns wherever there is room for two and one column the instant there is not, with no breakpoint to get wrong. Try the width and text-scale switches on section 1: it collapses on its own at 640 and at 320, and at 200% text scale on a desktop width.

The cost, stated: eighteen rows is a tall card. That is the honest length of the answer, no band is dropped, and the alternative is a control that hides half of it.

OQ-07 · does the below-floor sentence name the floor

Yes — it is the only thing the card can actually promise

The state routes nowhere, and rightly: the backlog is a list of your blockless checklists across the whole export, and nothing on this card knows how many of them carry this bird, so “fill in the gaps” here would read as “and then this chart will appear”, which is a promise it cannot keep. What it can say exactly is what would change the answer.

So the state carries the count and the floor, and the floor is built from WEATHER_SPECIES_MIN_CHECKLISTS the way legendFloorNote() already is, never a re-spelled number. Two sentences, no button, no icon, no frame.

The addition · the bird’s own whole

The same numerator, measured against the bird’s own record

The opening block now carries two sentences with one numerator and two wholes: is on 308 of your 353 weather-block checklists, then that is 308 of the 1,307 checklists you have it on. The second is the one that answers how much of this bird’s history the card can speak to, which is the job the brief gives it.

“That is” is doing real work and is not a stylistic tic. Without it, the same figure printed twice reads as two different counts. With it, the repeated number is unmistakably one number held against two wholes — which is the card’s whole thesis, stated in the lede before the chart demonstrates it. Nothing divides them. There is no coverage percentage for the bird, and there will not be one.

The zero case takes its own wording, for the same reason the shipped lede does: you have it on 26 checklists, and none of them carry a weather block. “That is 0 of the 26” would put a bare zero exactly where the sentence is supposed to be an account. The singular is reachable and ordinary — 49 of the 114 zero-block species on your export sit at exactly one checklist — so it reads you have it on 1 checklist, and it does not carry a weather block. Step to Wood Duck to see it.

It needs no new basis clause. Both figures are export-wide, so FR-13’s single filter clause already covers both.

The addition · where the denominator comes from

It stayed inside the current seam — no payload change, no round trip

I checked rather than assumed, and the answer is better than the alternative. Species Detail already holds phase.observations, the raw unfiltered parse from loadEbirdObservations(). A distinct-submission count over that array, matched with normalizeSpeciesName on both sides, is export-wide by construction (the county and date filters only ever touch speciesObs), all-forms (filterObservations(obs, true) is a passthrough, so the raw parse is the all-forms set), and folds forms into the parent exactly as the numerator does. Same basis, no new field, nothing back to The Architect.

The alternative would have failed in the one state that needs this most. Adding totalChecklists[] to WeatherStats.species is technically easy — the second pass in computeWeatherStats already visits every row and the count is one continue away. But species.names holds only species with at least one readable-block checklist, so a payload field structurally cannot reach the 114 species at zero, which is exactly where the coordinator is right that the sentence earns its place. Widening the table to all 282 is worse still: stats.species.names feeds the Statistics picker directly, so the picker would start offering 114 birds whose chart is all zeros — a plain FR-27 regression.

The one constraint that rides with it, for the Engineer: this must not be a per-species scan. NFR-02 requires a species change to be a lookup. Build it once, in the same post-paint effect and the same observations-identity memo the weather seam already uses — one pass producing a name-to-count map through a transient pair set — and a species change stays a map read.

Not asked, but decided

The below-floor state is the full state with the chart not yet arrived

This is the design decision that matters most, because it is the one four birds in five will see. The below-floor card opens with the same block, in the same place, at the same weight as the full card: the name, the count sentence, the muted note. Only the chart is absent, and one quiet line says what would bring it.

Step between Steller’s Jay and American Kestrel in section 1 and watch the first paragraph: it does not move, does not change register, and does not acquire an apology. A birder stepping through their list reads one continuous sentence per bird, and the chart is a bonus that sometimes appears. The alternative — an empty-state box with a dashed border and a centred glyph — would render a broken chart on four birds in five, which is precisely what the brief said this card must never be.

Also decided, and worth flagging

Three smaller calls

The bird’s name is not accent-coloured here. The Statistics lede wraps it in .sr-wx-lede .sp, which forces the accent, because there the name is the answer to “which bird” that the user just typed into a picker. On Species Detail the page header already names the bird, and the design system’s rule is one accent per surface. So the name renders in BirdName’s own default, and the accent on this card belongs to the bars and, in the export-below-floor state, to the route.

No onOpenSpecies, so the name is not a self-link. BirdName links only when hasEntry && onOpenSpecies; omitting the handler renders the name as text plus its two link marks, which is right, because a link to the page you are already on is a control that looks pressable and does nothing.

The head carries the title and nothing else. Several Species Detail sections put a muted figure at the end of their head row, and I am not doing that here: the count lives one line below with its denominator and its explanation, and a bare number in the head would be the second place the same fact lives, without the words that make it readable.

4. New copy

Six new count-bearing strings. All of them belong in lib/weatherStatsCopy.ts and nowhere else, so they ride the generated corpus sweep; everything else on the card is a shipped function reused unchanged.

Proposed functionRendersWhy this wording
speciesFloorNote() The skies and temperatures appear once a bird is on 10 weather-block checklists. Built from WEATHER_SPECIES_MIN_CHECKLISTS, pluralised, never a re-spelled number. It borrows the section’s own vocabulary from pickerRestParts().lead (“the skies and temperatures”), and it names what would change rather than what is missing.
speciesZeroLedeParts() is on none of your 353 weather-block checklists. + the shipped Counts are checklists, not sightings. The zero case needs its own wording because speciesLedeParts would print “is on 0 of your 353”. Same lead-plus-note shape as the shipped function, so the component renders one branch.
speciesOwnWhole() That is 308 of the 1,307 checklists you have it on. The bird’s own whole. Same numerator as the sentence above it; “That is” binds the two so a repeated figure cannot read as two counts. Prints no percentage and divides nothing. “you have it on” is the section’s existing vocabulary, from pickerRestParts().lead.
speciesOwnWholeZero() You have it on 26 checklists, and none of them carry a weather block. / at one: You have it on 1 checklist, and it does not carry a weather block. No numerator to bind, so it leads with the bird’s record. Both the noun and the verb clause inflect, because 49 of the 114 zero-block species on this export sit at exactly one checklist — the singular is the common case here, not an edge.
filterBasisClause() These cover every checklist in your export, so this tab’s filters do not narrow them. Present only while a county or date filter is active. Nobody is made to read an explanation of a filter they did not set.
formBasisClause() Every form counts as its parent species. Present only when the selection is a form whose normalized name differs from it. The lede’s BirdName already shows which parent, so the clause does not name it twice.

Where the clauses sit

Both basis clauses join the muted run that already follows the lede sentence, after the shipped “Counts are checklists, not sightings.” One paragraph: the lead in body text, then everything that qualifies it, muted, in the order lede-note, filter, form. Each is absent when it does not apply, so the common case is exactly the shipped one sentence.

5. Two approved repairs to the shared row

Both surfaced while checking this card at 320px and at 200% in-app text scale, and both are approved and ship with this build. They live in the row primitive this feature shares rather than copies, so both change how the Statistics Weather section renders too. The mockup above shows the approved behaviour.

Repair 1 · approved · confirmed in the shipped v1.0.22 build

The row label paints on top of the count

In the stacked tier the label is given white-space: normal and overflow: visible but no wrap allowance, and its track is minmax(0, 1fr). normal only breaks at spaces, so the multi-word labels wrap and are always clean while the two single-word ones cannot break at all and, with no clipping ancestor anywhere, paint straight through the count cell.

Reproduced against the shipped dist, in Chromium and WebKit, agreeing to within 0.06px. Worst case “Thunderstorm” overshooting by 48.9px at 320px/200%, where its track is 70.3px against a min-content of 135.2px. It is glyph on glyph, not intrusion into padding: the count’s ink starts exactly at its cell edge.

It is not an edge case and it is not data-dependent. The failing pair is “Thunderstorm” over no outings, which is the row every birder who has never birded a thunderstorm sees. On your own export that is exactly the row this card renders.

And it is not monotonic in width, which is the part worth carrying into any fix. There are two disjoint failing bands at 200%: 300 to 365px and 620 to 640px. The second exists because .sr-wx-pair’s auto-fit grid flips from one column to two at ~620px, halving each column and collapsing the label track from 345px back to 69px while the ≤640px rules are still in force. A check at 320 and 900 finds the first band and misses the second; a check at 375, 414, 480 or 560 finds neither.

Repair: overflow-wrap: anywhere on the label, in that tier only. break-word renders identically and would not work — only anywhere lowers the item’s intrinsic contribution — and nothing reads this label’s min-content, so the v0.5.85 objection to anywhere does not apply here. The desktop tier needs nothing: it clips with an ellipsis by construction.

Its blast radius is wider than this card, and that is correct. (Measured at Stage 6 and corrected here.) The rule lives in the shared @media (max-width: 640px) tier on .sr-wx-row > .sr-wx-label, and DistRow renders that same element, so it reaches the Statistics distribution rows too. It repairs them as well: the shipped distribution rows carried label-over-count readings at supported widths, worst 21.88px on “Overcast” at 320px / 200%, and carry none afterwards. The change is confined to 300 to 355px at 200% text, at the cost of a taller row there. That is inside FR-27, because the design intent is stated over a row’s label with no per-species restriction.

Repair 2 · approved · measured on this card, same mechanism

At 200% text scale on a desktop width, the rails collapse to nothing

The stacked tier opens at @media (max-width: 640px), so at a 952px frame the row keeps its four inline tracks and the 1fr rail absorbs everything the glyph, the 8.5rem label track and the nowrap count leave over. In the same 398px column: rails of 193 to 240px at 100%, and 0.0 to 32.6px at 200%, with several rows painting no rail at all while still taking the height of one.

It leaks no scroll and hides no value — every figure is text on the same line, which is why WCAG 1.4.4 is not engaged — but a chart whose bars vanish for exactly the reader who enlarged their text is not doing the job the bars are there for.

Repair: move the tier’s trigger, not its layout. The stacked shape is already correct and already shipped; what is wrong is when it fires. What decides whether four inline tracks fit is the row’s own column measured against the size of its own text, and em inside a container query is exactly that: the same 398px column reads 24.9em at 100% and 12.4em at 200%. So the block opens at @container (max-width: 22em) on the group column, against measured widths of 6.9 to 16.9em everywhere that must stack and 24.9em in the one place that must not.

This is not the v1.0.22 rule being contradicted. That rule rejects a container query because “how wide is my box” cannot answer “how wide is my content” — and it was right, which is why the reference figure took its own row unconditionally. That change removed the data-dependent half of the width. What is left varies with the text scale alone, and an em container query tracks the text scale exactly.

Worth taking together. The container trigger is also what makes Finding 1’s two disjoint bands stop mattering. Those bands exist only because the tier is keyed on a viewport width while the column count underneath it changes independently at ~620px; keyed on the column itself, there is nothing left to be non-monotonic in. The failure is removed rather than relocated, which is the same move the reference figure made when it took its own row.

Two constraints the spec surfaced

Scope the container, and keep the declarations single-sourced

The container belongs on the per-species groups only. .sr-wx-rows appears in three places in WeatherStatsSection.tsx — the distribution groups, serving all four distribution axes, and the two per-species columns. Putting the container on that class wholesale would re-trigger the distribution rows, which do not have the rail defect this repair is about (they run at full card width and their rails measure healthily at 200%), and would visibly change the shipped Statistics distribution rows between roughly 480 and 640px, which FR-27 forbids. This mockup puts the container on the group wrapper, which is equivalent here and does not generalize. Name the defect and not just the rows, or the two repairs get conflated: the distribution rows did have Repair 1’s defect, and this scoping constraint is Repair 2’s alone. Verified at Stage 6, with the container scoped as specified the distribution rows show zero change anywhere between 470 and 660px at any text scale.

The stacked declarations must stay single-sourced. The distribution rows keep their viewport trigger and the per-species rows gain a container trigger, so two selectors now need the same declaration list. The repo has the pattern and the reason already: .sr-input-16 and .sr-ctl-row :is(button, select, input) differ in specificity on purpose, share one declaration, and are locked identical by a test that parses the real globals.css. Two blocks that agree today are two blocks that will disagree later, on the row FR-26 exists to keep single.

Why the gate did not catch either

verify-weather-species-rows.mjs is a good harness and neither of these is in its field of view. Run against the same build and the same fixture it reports all checks passed, worst reading 0.01px, across 27 widths and three text scales in both engines — including 320px at 200%, the exact configuration carrying 48.9px of painted overlap. It measures page scroll width and overflow past the card’s content box; a label overrunning into its sibling grid column never leaves the card, and a collapsed rail overflows nothing at all. The stylesheet guards are declaration-only and the component tests are jsdom, which has no layout engine. If these are taken, each wants an assertion of its own: label ink against the count cell’s edge, and a minimum rendered rail width, both swept at 200%.

6. What this design holds itself to

  • Scaled to the bird’s own largest band, per axis, never shared between the two. The shared rail does not come back; the muted trailing figure is what carries the population.
  • One numerator, two wholes, in the lede as well as the rows. The opening block states the bird’s weather-block count against your weather record and against the bird’s own record, and nothing divides them. There is no coverage percentage for the bird.
  • Nothing divides one share by the other. No rate, nothing ranked, nothing predicted, no “best conditions for” anywhere on the card.
  • The trailing figure takes its own row at every width — not a fifth column above 640px. Its width depends on the width of its own text, so no breakpoint could express the condition.
  • No positive min-width anywhere in the pattern. The one min-width: 3px is on the bar fill, inside an overflow: hidden track, where it cannot widen a row.
  • Three row states, three shapes. Data draws a solid track with a fill; a band with no outings draws a dashed track, mutes its label, prints no numeral and carries no reference; a band the bird missed on outings you did have prints 0 · 0% on a solid track and keeps its reference. Steller’s Jay shows all three.
  • No band is dropped, including the four sky conditions and two temperature bands you have never birded. Dropping bands makes the distribution itself lie.
  • Every colour is a var(--sr-*) token, declared in both themes. No hardcoded hex or RGB in the pattern.
  • Every button and a href carries a literal tabIndex={0}, including the route in the export-below-floor state. The full and bird-below-floor states render no control at all.
  • No em dash in any string the card renders.
  • Motion is the bar entrance only — 300ms, cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1), replayed when the species changes and not otherwise, with a prefers-reduced-motion fallback that renders final widths instantly. Nothing else on the card animates, because nothing else changes.
  • Identifiers key on an index, never on a species name, a condition glyph or a band label.

Three observations for the record

A bounded residual, deliberately not answered in copy. The tab shows a big stat labelled Checklists two sections above this card, and it is speciesObs.length — a ROW count, not a distinct-submission count — so it can read higher than the card’s figure when a checklist carries both a parent and a form. Measured on your export it is negligible: 4 species of 282 disagree at all (Mallard +7, Yellow-rumped Warbler +3, Dark-eyed Junco +1, Northern Flicker +1), 12 rows in 21,369, and all five birds in the stepper are exact. So I am not spending copy on it. Stated honestly, though: that 0.06% is a property of this birder’s subspecies-reporting habit, not of the code, and someone who logs Myrtle and Audubon’s routinely would see a wider gap on the same path.

A live defect next door, found while checking the above and not touched. SpeciesDetail.tsx’s Frequency stat divides that same ROW count by totalFilteredChecklists, which is a Set of submission ids — numerator and denominator on different bases, so Frequency is systematically overstated for those four species and can in principle exceed 100%. It cannot on this export. The correctly deduped count is already sitting in the component. Not this feature’s work; worth its own issue.

--sr-card-shadow is the one shadow token still on pure black (rgba(0,0,0,0.06)) while the design system says shadows are tinted with the app’s own ink. This mockup reproduces the shipped value rather than quietly improving it. Not this feature’s work; worth a roadmap line.
