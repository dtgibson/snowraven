# Strategic Brief - First of Year on Species Detail

## What We're Building
A small section on the Species Detail tab that lists, for the selected species, the first date it was reported in each calendar year the user saw it, each date opening its checklist on eBird, with a compact chart of those first-sighting days across the years. Everything derives from the user's own eBird export already loaded on the device; nothing is fetched.

## Why Now
Species Detail already answers "when did I first see this bird, ever" (the First seen card) and "how often do I see it" (the Sightings and Checklists graphs). The question a birder asks every spring and fall, "when do I usually get this one back", sits between those two and has no answer on the tab today: the user would have to scan the Comments archive or the monthly graph and work it out by hand. The data is complete and local, the tab's conventions for which observations count and how a date links to its checklist are settled, and the chart library and lazy-chart geometry pattern already exist on this tab, so the build is small and the answer is immediate. It also gives the deferred Calendar lifer/first-of-year overlay (ROADMAP, Calendar follow-ons) a concrete, well-defined precedent: on Species Detail the species is fixed, so "first of year" has exactly one meaning, which is the ambiguity that deferred the Calendar version.

## The User Problem
A birder with years of eBird history wants to know when a species shows up for them each year: the first Yellow-rumped Warbler of each fall, the first Osprey of each spring, whether this year's arrival was early or late against their own record. eBird's own species pages show range-wide bar charts, not the user's personal first dates, and SnowRaven today shows only the all-time first. The per-year first date is a personal record, and the pattern across years (steady, drifting earlier, a gap year) is the thing a birder actually wants to see at a glance.

## Success Criteria
- A birder opens Species Detail, selects a species with sightings in several years, and sees one row per calendar year with that year's first reported date, in the app's chosen date format, newest or oldest first in a fixed, stated order.
- Each per-year date opens the checklist it came from on eBird, the way First seen already does; a row whose checklist id fails the existing `S\d+` guard renders as plain text, never as a dead link.
- Beside or below the rows, a compact chart plots each year's first sighting by day of year, so a steady arrival reads as a flat line of points and a gap year reads as a missing point.
- The dates and the count of rows match what the birder could compute by hand from their export: for each year, the smallest Date among that species' rows, under the same Show subspecies setting and the same county and date filters the rest of the tab is showing.
- A species seen in only one year shows that one row and no chart; a species with no observations in scope shows no section at all; the current year in progress appears as an ordinary row with its first date so far.
- Changing Show subspecies, the county filter or the date range updates the rows and the chart together with every other section on the tab.
- The section is readable and operable at 320px width and 200% in-app text scale in both themes, and the rows remain the complete, keyboard-reachable record when the chart is not consulted.
- Opening a species with tens of thousands of observations does not visibly slow the tab: the derivation is one pass over the species' own observations.

## Scope
- A pure derivation in a `lib/` module: `ObservationEntry[]` in, an ordered list of `{ year, date, submissionId }` (one per calendar year present) out, with unit tests covering one year, many years, gap years, same-date ties, a leap day and the current year.
- A new section on Species Detail showing the per-year first dates as linked rows, placed by The Designer in the register of the existing Sightings and Graph cards.
- A compact Recharts chart of first-sighting day of year across years, lazy-loaded like the tab's other charts, with its geometry in a `lib/` module the host can size the fallback from (the CLAUDE.md lazy-chart rule), colors through the existing `--sr-graph-*` tokens, and the rows serving as the accessible form of the data.
- The empty, one-year, gap-year and current-year states.
- `docs/HELP.md` updated in the same change (in-app help is written without a stop).
- CHANGELOG entry and the standard four-file version bump, handled by the pipeline's deploy stage.

## Out of Scope
- Any Calendar change, including the deferred lifer/first-of-year overlay (ROADMAP, Calendar follow-ons). Related, distinct surface; this brief does not decide it.
- Any Statistics tab change, including a cross-species "earliest arrivals this year" view.
- Any network call: no eBird, taxonomy or other request. The feature reads only the export already loaded.
- Any new stored document, cache, iCloud Sync change, or `clearDerived.ts` row: the derivation lives in a render-time memo over the species' observations and is discarded with them.
- Widget, Alerts, Targets or native changes.
- A new toolbar toggle or persisted preference. The section follows the tab's existing switches and filters.
- Last-of-year dates, arrival/departure spans, or comparison against eBird's regional bar charts.
- Website, README, App Store listing and privacy policy copy. If a sentence is wanted for the Species Detail section of the website or README, the build prepares it as a HELD proposal in this pipeline folder and never writes it.

## Key Decisions
- **"First of year" means the earliest observation date of the species within each calendar year, by the export's own Date column.** The eBird export's Date is the checklist's local date as the user recorded it (`YYYY-MM-DD`), and every other date on this tab is read the same way, so the year is the first four characters of that string and the ordering is the string's own lexicographic order. No `Date` object, no timezone, no local-midnight arithmetic: CLAUDE.md records `Date` silently rolling impossible calendars into real instants, and `computeSightingsStats` already sorts First seen with `localeCompare` on the same field. Day of year for the chart is computed from the string's month and day with a leap-year-aware table, so February 29 is day 60 of its year and March 1 is day 61 in a leap year and 60 otherwise; the one-day leap shift is immaterial at chart scale and the axis is labeled by month.
- **The observations that count are exactly `speciesObs`, the set the tab already uses for First seen, Last seen and the graphs.** That set is the selected species' rows after the Show subspecies merge and the county and date-range filters, with no further exclusion. Reasons: a first-of-year date is a personal record of a sighting that happened, so a non-countable form or an escapee sighting still counts once the user has chosen to look at that species; the Show all forms and Show escapees switches decide which species are *offered* in the selector, not which of a chosen species' rows are real, and the tab never drops a selected species' rows on those switches; and consistency with the First seen card means the oldest row in this section always equals First seen. The Planner can make this testable as: the earliest date in the section for the earliest year equals `sightingsStats.firstObs.date`, under every switch and filter.
- **Show subspecies (the merge toggle) governs which forms fold into a year's first.** Off (the default), all subspecies and forms of the species fold together, so a "(Myrtle)" in March and an "(Audubon's)" in April give March. On, the selected form's own rows alone count. No new toggle. Species Detail has no "Count all forms" control; that is a Calendar control and does not apply here.
- **Filters apply, including the date range.** A date range starting in March makes a year's "first" the first within the range, which is how First seen already behaves and is what the filter strip above the sections already explains. Consistency across the tab wins over a special case; the section's copy must not claim "of the year" in a way that is false under a date filter, which is a Designer and copy decision to carry forward.
- **A year with no sightings is not a row and is a visible gap in the chart.** The chart's year axis spans the first year with data to the last year with data (continuous integers), and a year with no point stays empty rather than being interpolated or drawn as zero. The row list shows only years present.
- **Ties on the same date resolve to the first row in file order**, matching `computeSightingsStats` (a stable sort, so the first encountered row wins). The date is the fact; the linked checklist is one of the checklists carrying it.
- **The chart requires at least two years with data; the rows do not.** This mirrors the tab's existing rule that a graph is absent below two distinct periods. One year of data is one row and no chart. Zero rows in scope means the whole section is absent, never an empty-state box, consistent with the tab's "ranked list renders a sentence at one and nothing at zero" posture.
- **The current year is an ordinary row.** Its first date so far is a real first-of-year; the derivation does not read the clock. If The Designer wants to mark the in-progress year visually, that is a pure render decision over the year value and a `SESSION_NOW_MS`-style constant, never a render-time clock read.
- **The rows are the accessible, complete record; the chart reinforces them.** Every date is a link through the shared checklist-link primitive with its existing id guard and accessible-name formula. The chart is compact, in the register of Sightings Over Time, colored through existing graph tokens, and follows the tab's current chart accessibility pattern (decorative recharts wrapped `inert`, or the scroller-as-tab-stop pattern if it scrolls), so no information exists only in the chart.
- **Derivation is a pure, tested `lib/` function; geometry is a pure `lib/` module; the chart component is lazy.** `lib/sightingsGraph.ts` plus `speciesDetail/SightingsGraph.tsx` and `lib/planChartGeometry.ts` are the shapes to copy. Recharts stays off the entry chunk (`entryChunk.test.ts`).
- **No persistence, no cache, no sync.** The derivation is a `useMemo` over `speciesObs`, discarded with it. It therefore takes no `clearDerived.ts` row by design (CLAUDE.md: an in-memory derived value keyed on a user file is outside the registry), and adds no scan over user file text beyond reading fields the parser already validated, so no new schema-declared scan.
- **Scope is Species Detail only; the Calendar overlay stays deferred.** This feature does not settle "first of year" for a whole-year, all-species view and should not be read as doing so. The brief names the roadmap item so the Planner records the relationship, not a dependency.
