# Strategic Brief - Taxonomic Splits and Lumps

## What We're Building
On the Species Detail tab, beside the existing "Subspecies and forms" control, a "Splits and lumps" view: a list of every species in the user's eBird backup that eBird has split or lumped in one of its annual taxonomy updates, and for any such species a simple, dated lineage chart (what it was, what it became, which update did it) with the user's own record counts on each branch.

## Why Now
eBird's 2025 update split Warbling Vireo, Yellow Warbler, Whimbrel and others and lumped more species than any recent year, and the 2026 update is due in October to finish aligning with AviList, so the question "what happened to my Warbling Vireos" is live for exactly the birder SnowRaven serves. eBird answers it once, with an emailed PDF at update time; the backup CSV answers it never, because "Download My Data" rewrites every record under the current taxonomy. The app already carries a bundled taxonomy snapshot refreshed each release, a sciName-first name bridge that survives a stale export, and a Subspecies Explorer whose two-part shape (list control plus per-species section) this feature can mirror, so the cost is one small bundled history asset and one section, not new plumbing.

## The User Problem
A birder looking at their own history sees "Western Warbling Vireo" on checklists from 2011 and cannot tell from the file that eBird, not they, put that name there, nor that some of those records became an Eastern/Western slash, nor when. The same is true in the other direction after a lump: a species they ticked for years simply disappears from their list with no trace in the export. The history exists in eBird's published update records, but nothing joins it to the user's own species and dates, and eBird's own personalized report covers one update, arrives once by email, and is not something you can open next to a species.

## Success Criteria
- On Species Detail, beside the "Subspecies and forms" control, the user can open a list of every species in their loaded backup that was split or lumped in a covered eBird update, each with the update year; the count in the control matches the list; picking one selects that species.
- For a selected affected species, a lineage chart shows the before name(s), the after name(s), the kind of change (split or lump) and the update it happened in (month and year), with the user's species visibly marked in the lineage and every other name rendered through the standard bird-name component.
- On each branch the user holds records for, the chart shows how many of their rows carry that name, split into rows dated before the update (assigned by eBird during the update) and rows dated after it (recorded under the current name), and states plainly that the pre-update rows were reassigned by eBird.
- Where a split produced a slash entry (for example "Eastern/Western Warbling Vireo") and the user's backup contains rows under it, that branch appears on the chart with its count, so the user can see where the records eBird could not assign went.
- A backup exported before an update (still carrying the pre-change name) matches the "before" side of the same chart and is labelled as an export that predates that update; nothing is silently mismatched.
- A species with no split or lump in the covered record shows an honest, short empty state, and the list omits it; the view states the range of updates it covers (earliest and latest) so silence about an older change is never read as "nothing happened".
- Everything works offline and without an eBird key: no third-party request, no new endpoint or host, no request moved between components.
- The chart has a text equivalent a screen reader can follow, carries no meaning by color alone, and holds WCAG 2.1 AA at 320px and 200% in-app text scale on Mac, Windows, iPhone, iPad and web/Pi.
- Every existing Species Detail control, the Subspecies Explorer, life-list totals and every other tab behave exactly as before.

## Scope
- A bundled, build-time history asset of species-level splits and lumps from eBird's annual taxonomy updates, keyed on eBird species codes with scientific and current common names on both the before and after sides, one dated event per change, chained where one lineage changed more than once.
- A generator script (developer-run, like the existing taxonomy snapshot build) that derives that asset from the annual eBird/Clements checklist spreadsheets' per-row change records, fails closed on anything it cannot resolve to a complete event, and a CI check that holds the committed asset to the bundled taxonomy snapshot (every current-side code resolves, every event is complete and dated) plus a size bound; the asset stays off the entry chunk.
- The "Splits and lumps" list control on Species Detail, sited beside the "Subspecies and forms" control, and a per-selected-species lineage section near the subspecies breakdown, both in the default merged mode.
- The lineage chart itself, hand-built with the app's existing means (inline SVG or CSS layout, existing tokens), with a text-equivalent rendering; no new dependency.
- Matching the user's species to events through the existing name resolution (scientific name first, common name fallback), so stale and current exports both match.
- Per-branch record counts from the already-parsed backup rows, partitioned by observation date against the update's published date.
- `docs/HELP.md` and in-app copy for the new section (no approval stop). A PROPOSED single sentence for the Species Detail section of `README.md` and `website/`, held as a patch for the user's express approval, never written by the build; the App Store "What's New" line likewise held.
- Version bump, `CHANGELOG.md` entry, per the repo's release rules.

## Out of Scope
- Subspecies-group, ISSF and form-level rearrangements, and pure renames of common or scientific names without a split or lump (the existing rename bridge already covers renamed names for lookups).
- Changes to taxonomic order, family placement, or newly described species.
- Editing, reassigning or second-guessing the user's records: the app reports what eBird did and when; it never suggests which daughter a pre-split record "should" be.
- Any eBird API use for this feature: no fetching prior taxonomy versions, no version listing, no personalized report; the existing taxonomy refresh is untouched.
- A browse of the whole world's splits and lumps: only species present in the user's backup appear.
- Splits and lumps on other tabs (Statistics, Multimedia, Breeding Codes, Calendar); Species Detail only for v1, matching the Subspecies Explorer's precedent.
- Region-limited or hand-curated coverage (for example North America only): the record is global and machine-derived, or an update is absent and the coverage statement says so.
- Any change to `PRIVACY_POLICY.md`: nothing new leaves the device.

## Key Decisions
- **Source of history: a bundled build-time asset, not a runtime fetch.** eBird's exports present every record under the taxonomy current at export time, so the backup alone cannot reveal a split or lump; the history has to come from eBird's own published update records. Each annual eBird/Clements checklist release ships as a spreadsheet whose rows carry a per-year change column ("Clements vYYYY change", with a fuller "Text for website vYYYY"), eBird species codes and an Avibase Taxon Concept Id, and Cornell states the full documentation of all changes is in the spreadsheet itself. That is a machine-readable change list, one file per year. A runtime alternative exists (the eBird API's `ref/taxonomy/ebird` accepts a `version` year and `ref/taxonomy/versions` lists them) but it returns whole taxonomies of ~3 MB each with no parent-to-daughter relationships, needs the user's key, and would re-download static historical facts that change once a year. The asset is the shape this repo already uses for the taxonomy snapshot and the countability list: generated by a developer-run script at release time, committed, refreshed on the annual rollover. The Cornell download pages refuse automated clients (403 / bot check), which confirms this is a developer step, not a CI step; the CI test holds the committed output, not the download.
- **The user's direction ("splits and lumps happen once per year, so that info can probably be downloaded once and cached") was weighed, and the once-per-year cadence is met by the release-time refresh rather than a download, because nothing fetchable exists to download.** The direction is right about the cadence and the design honors it; what it cannot do today is fetch, for two measured reasons. (1) The only machine-readable change list is Cornell's annual spreadsheet, served from `birds.cornell.edu` behind a bot challenge that refused every automated fetch in this investigation (403 / Anubis browser check), so an in-app download of a 10 MB spreadsheet on Mac, iPhone, iPad, Windows or Pi is not a route the app can rely on (and, measured or not, a fetch from the webview would additionally need that host to permit cross-origin reads, which was not established); it would also be a NEW outbound host, which is a `PRIVACY_POLICY.md` change needing the user's approval. (2) The eBird API (`api.ebird.org`, an existing disclosed host) can return any year's whole taxonomy with the user's key, but it expresses no relationships: diffing two years by species code yields added and retired codes with no parent-to-daughter linkage and no Taxon Concept Id, so a split's parent or a lump's target would be guessed, which is exactly what a fails-closed generator exists to prevent; it would also cost ~3 MB per year per device and require a key for a feature that otherwise needs none. A bundled asset refreshed once a year at release time therefore delivers the same once-per-year freshness, offline and keyless, and is the same rhythm the taxonomy snapshot already follows. **Reversal condition, recorded so the Architect can plan for it:** if eBird ever publishes a fetchable change list (for example a JSON endpoint on `api.ebird.org`), a once-per-taxonomy-year download becomes the better design; it would then be stated in the durable form (host, whether it is a new third-party request, what is cached, where, for how long, and what the app shows offline or before the first download), held in a long-lived persistent cache of the `lib/countyCompletenessCache.ts` shape (storage-seam document, per-entry validation on load and at the write chokepoint, bounded), with the bundled asset as the offline floor. Until then the bundled asset is the whole design.
- **Trust boundary is build-time, per CLAUDE.md.** The generator fails closed (a split with no resolvable parent, a lump with no resolvable target, a code absent from the current snapshot, an undated year), a CI test re-checks the committed asset against the bundled taxonomy snapshot and bounds its size, and `entryChunk.test.ts` keeps it lazy. No runtime per-entry validation is owed because a user cannot alter a Vite-inlined asset without altering the code that reads it.
- **Coverage is stated, not assumed.** The asset carries the earliest and latest update it covers and the UI shows both. Target: every annual update for which the machine-readable change record can be obtained (the checklist spreadsheets carry change columns in recent years; earlier updates back to 2013 are documented on eBird's update pages and may or may not parse), with the floor decided by what the generator can actually produce, never by hand-typing. As of this brief eBird's latest update is v2025 (31 October 2025) and v2026 is expected in October 2026; whichever the asset holds at build time is what the UI claims, and the annual refresh joins the existing release-time snapshot re-run.
- **Species-level events only.** A "split" or "lump" in this feature is a change in the species category of the eBird taxonomy; group and subspecies rearrangements are excluded, and a rename that is not a split or lump is excluded. Slash taxa that a split created are carried on the event so the "could not be assigned" branch can be shown when the user's backup holds rows under it.
- **"In the user's backup" means matched by species code on either side of an event.** A species in the loaded backup is affected when its resolved code is a product of a split, the result of a lump, or a pre-change name (an export older than the update). Resolution goes through the existing scientific-name-first, common-name-fallback path in the taxonomy service, so nothing new is keyed on raw common names.
- **The user's dates matter and are shown, not interpreted.** Rows dated on or before the update's published release date are counted as reassigned by eBird; rows after it as recorded under the current name. The published release date stands in for eBird's multi-week conversion window, stated as the update's month and year. The app never proposes which daughter an old record belongs to.
- **Placement mirrors the Subspecies Explorer.** A list control beside "Subspecies and forms" (with a count) and a per-species section near the subspecies breakdown, both in merged mode, so the two taxonomy tools read as one family. The Designer owns the chart's form; the constraint is a hand-built lineage of before, event, after with a text equivalent and no new library (Recharts is a data-chart tool, and a diagram library is not justified for a one-to-many tree).
- **Network posture, in the durable form:** no third-party request, no new endpoint or host, no request moved between components. Works without a key. This holds on all five targets, including web/Pi where the storage seam is HTTP to the user's own server.
- **Published copy is held.** The build may prepare one proposed sentence for the Species Detail section of `README.md` and `website/` and the "What's New" line, as a held patch shown to the user; none of it is written without the user's yes. `docs/HELP.md` and in-app text ship without a stop.
- **Alignment verdict: aligned.** Own-data exploration over the user's export, local-first, no new provider, works alongside eBird (its personalized report is a one-time PDF; this is the persistent, per-species view) and replaces nothing. Nothing in the founding brief's Out of Scope list is touched. This is not a roadmap Up Next item; the current Up Next entries are release-process and guard work, so this is a fresh feature request and is recorded as such.

## Evidence
- Repo: `frontend/src/assets/ebird-taxonomy.json` (~1.7 MB, twin at `backend/staticdata/ebird_taxonomy.json`) is a five-map reduction of `GET /v2/ref/taxonomy/ebird` built by `scripts/build-ebird-taxonomy.mjs`; its version is a hand label (`VERSION = '2027'`, generated 2026-06-20) paired with `CACHE_KEY` in `frontend/src/lib/tauri/taxonomyService.ts`, and `ref/taxonomy/versions` is never queried, so the app has no machine-read taxonomy version today. The history asset must therefore state its own coverage rather than derive it from the snapshot label.
- Repo: `frontend/src/lib/tauri/taxonomyService.ts` `getTaxonomyCodes()` and `backend/routers/taxonomy.py` `get_species_codes` resolve sciName first, then common name (the v1.0.4 bridge, DECISIONS.md); it handles renames only, nothing for splits or lumps.
- Repo: `frontend/src/lib/parseEbirdObservations.ts` stores `commonName`, `scientificName` and `date` per row; `Taxonomic Order` is not read; the export carries no taxonomy version.
- Repo: `frontend/src/components/speciesDetail/SubspeciesExplorer.tsx` (`SubspeciesExplorerControl` below the species combobox, `SubspeciesBreakdownSection` further down) and `frontend/src/lib/subspeciesExplorer.ts`, all derived from parsed rows; `pipeline/subspecies-explorer/strategic-brief.md` set the "Species Detail only, fully offline" precedent. `TAB_LABELS` names the tab "Species Detail".
- Repo: no diagram library in `frontend/package.json` (Recharts, lucide, maplibre only); inline SVG is used for glyphs and markers.
- eBird, "2025 Taxonomy Update" (science.ebird.org and ebird.org/news): released 31 October 2025; 40 species gained by splits, 18 lost to lumps; eBird revisits all records and "wherever possible, and in almost all cases, we update your records for you"; records it cannot assign at a split become a slash or spuh; a Personalized eBird Taxonomy Update Report (PDF, by email) is offered via "Download My Data"; full AviList alignment expected by October 2026.
- Cornell Lab, Clements Checklist "October 2025" and "Updates and Corrections - October 2025": the eBird/Clements Checklist v2025 spreadsheet (xlsx and csv) includes documentation of all revisions; recent releases record changes in a "Clements vYYYY change" column with a "Text for website vYYYY" companion; rows carry species codes and an Avibase Taxon Concept Id described as the identifier for comparing taxa across versions. Download pages are bot-protected (403 to automated fetches).
- eBird API 2.0 (per the auk, rebird and ebird-api client documentation): `ref/taxonomy/ebird` takes an integer `version` year and `ref/taxonomy/versions` lists `authorityVer` entries with a `latest` flag; neither expresses split or lump relationships.
