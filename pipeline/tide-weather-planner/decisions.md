# Decisions — Weather/tide Planner

## Stage 4 -- The Designer (2026-09-12)

Design system status: `pipeline/design-system.md` exists and applies in full. No visual evolution; the feature extends the Weather tab's Predict form and result region. Deviations and settlements below.

- **D4-01 (OQ-01) Action label and weight.** "Plan sunrises and sunsets", a full-width secondary outline button beneath the primary "Get forecast", with a muted caption saying the plan runs from now through the forecast and that the date and time apply only to Get forecast. Reason: the label names what you get in the form's own register; the single-moment lookup keeps primary weight, and stacking keeps the form fitting at 320px.
- **D4-02 (OQ-02) Chart density.** A uniform real-elapsed-time axis at one constant, 16px per hour (a day is 384px; an eight-day plan about 2,870px), one curve point per 30 minutes, one strip cell per hourly entry (16px, emoji) then per daily entry (with text). Reason: an hourly glyph needs about 16px to read; a uniform axis keeps morning-to-morning comparison honest; the schema's "about 900px" estimate assumed daily cells not at 24x an hourly cell's width and is superseded.
- **D4-03 (OQ-03) The chart scrolls sideways on every width**, inside its own box, with snap points at each local midnight, a right-edge fade, a scroll hint in the legend, and the scroll container itself carrying `role="img"`, the accessible name and `tabIndex={0}` so arrow keys scroll it and there is one tab stop. The page never scrolls sideways; the list never scrolls sideways.
- **D4-04 (OQ-04) `ChartViewTip` does not mount.** The chart scrolls; it never asks for a rotation.
- **D4-05 (OQ-07) List shape.** Grouped by day under a day heading, each event item still carrying its date (sr-only) so a screen reader hears the day with the event. Every weather-strip cell reaches the list as text through a per-day sky line: a collapsed "Sky hour by hour" disclosure on hourly days, a one-line daily summary on daily days.
- **D4-06 (OQ-08) Words.** Closing note: "The plan stops where the weather forecast stops. Tide predictions reach further ahead: for any later moment, use Get forecast with a date and time." Loading: "Building the plan for {place}…". Ready: "Plan ready for {place}." One-sided brackets, polar-day notes, chart names and resolution labels are in `design-spec.md` → Content Notes.
- **D4-07 New tokens.** `--sr-plan-day`, `--sr-plan-night`, `--sr-plan-tide`, `--sr-plan-sun`, `--sr-plan-halo`, `--sr-plan-grid-rgb`, in both themes, with measured ratios in the spec. On dark the DAY is the lit band and night is the deep ground (the inverse of light), and both bands are always drawn and both are in the legend, so which one is shaded carries no meaning. Markers are haloed so no marker hue is load-bearing over the night band; shape and label distinguish them.
- **D4-08 Doctrine deviation, deliberate: the display face stays Inter / system-ui.** The doctrine asks for a distinctive display face; the design system's type is Inter, and the plan must read as the Weather tab it lives in. The design system wins on specifics. No new font.
- **D4-09 Chart text is px, list text is rem.** The chart is a fixed-px track (16px/hour), so its labels and the strip's text are px on purpose and do not follow the in-app text scale; every figure they carry is repeated in the list at rem sizes, which does. This is the design system's "text-sized element is a sibling of a track" rule applied to a chart: the list is the sibling.
- **D4-10 Resolution labels.** "FORECAST · DAILY" (Predict's pill words, extracted to `lib/forecastLabels.ts` and shared per the schema) and a new parallel "FORECAST · HOURLY" for the plan, as muted uppercase micro-labels rather than Predict's amber pill, so twelve daily events do not read as twelve warnings; the amber register is kept for the strip's single "daily from here" tag.
- **D4-11 The mockup draws the chart as inline SVG; the build uses Recharts** in the lazy `PlanChart` chunk with the same geometry (spec → The chart, mockup note).

## Stage 5 -- The Engineer (2026-09-12)

- **D5-01 PLAN_CURVE_MAX is 448, not the schema's 432.** Nine 24-hour days at 30 minutes is 432 samples, and the nine-day tide span can hold a fall-back day, which makes it 434 (the `dst-fall` parity family measures exactly that). The schema's caps are meant to sit at least above the largest conforming shape, so the cap moves rather than the shape. The bound test still measures the maximal fixture an order of magnitude under 300,000 code units.
- **D5-02 A boundary day's partial daily cell carries the clock it begins at in `local`, not the day's noon.** The schema gave every daily cell `'{date} 12:00'`; the list's "from {time} {description} (daily)" wording needs the cell's start clock, and the UI is forbidden from computing a local time for itself (schema section 13, item 1). A full-day cell keeps the noon form; only the boundary cell (startTs > day.startTs) changes. Both twins and the fixture carry it.
- **D5-03 The halo contrast invariant is stated per band.** The spec's ratios say "halo vs night 3.21 dark", but on dark the halo (`#05070D`) IS the night ground, so that ratio is 1:1; the 3.21 figure is the halo against the day band. The guard (`planContrast.test.ts`) asserts what the haloed design actually provides: over each band, every mark's fill or its halo clears 3:1, and over each band the label ink or its halo clears 4.5:1. Every token value in the spec is unchanged.
- **D5-04 The chart's hour ticks on a DST day are placed by the day's own length.** A 6 AM / Noon / 6 PM tick on a 25- or 23-hour day sits (length - 86400) seconds further along the axis than on a plain day, because every US zone changes its clock at 02:00. This is arithmetic on the document's `startTs`/`endTs`, never a timezone conversion, and it only ever affects the three tick labels on the one changed day.
- **D5-05 The chart's Recharts plot is placed by the same x(t) the HTML strip uses.** The XAxis domain is widened by the 40px gutter (`axisStart - 40 * 225 s`) with zero left margin, so Recharts' linear scale and the strip's `x(t) = 40 + (t - axisStart) / 225` agree pixel for pixel, and the day ground extends under the sticky gutter as in the mockup. Hidden axes reserve no space in Recharts 3 (verified in `selectChartOffsetInternal`), which is what makes the plot area exactly the track.
- **D5-06 The scroll container is the tab stop; the canvas child is `aria-hidden` and `inert`, with `accessibilityLayer={false}` on the chart.** The house rule for decorative recharts (its root svg is otherwise focusable). A pointer event on an inert subtree hit-tests to the nearest non-inert ancestor, which is the scroller, so wheel, touch and drag scrolling reach it; that is the one claim here only a browser settles, flagged for the Tester.
- **D5-07 The plan's request token lives in the panel, bumped by every phase-changing action.** `openPredict`, `onCurrent`, `runLookup` and `runPlan` each increment `reqRef`; a plan response compares its captured token before touching the phase. The override keeps Predict's coord identity guard.
- **D5-08 The tide slot reuses Predict's slot copy.** When the tide half fails at the transport (or an override fails), the slot shows `OFFLINE_MESSAGE` / `NO_KEY_MESSAGE` by kind, exactly as the single-moment tide slot does; the web/Pi server-down variant is used only where the weather half blocks the whole plan, which is where the panel holds the classified message.
- **D5-09 Every planner string lives in `lib/planCopy.ts`**, swept by `planCopy.test.ts` over a corpus built by calling every builder per render state (the count-bearing copy rule), with the daily label imported from `lib/forecastLabels.ts`, where Predict's wording was extracted (not copied) so the single-moment result renders the same bytes.
- **D5-10 The parity fixture is generated, not hand-written.** `weatherTidePlan.fixtureGen.test.ts` runs only with `SR_GEN_PLAN_FIXTURE=1` and writes the fixture from the SHIPPED TS builders over synthetic One Call and NOAA bodies shaped exactly as the providers ship them; the Python twin reproducing it byte for byte from the same raw bodies is the parity claim (canonical JSON compared with integral floats normalized to ints, since JSON carries no int/float distinction and JS prints `2.0` as `2`). Continuous bodies are six-minute for the families whose claim is about the grid (reference, gap, dst-fall, maximal) and thirty-minute elsewhere, which keeps the tracked fixture near 1 MB.
- **D5-11 `gmt_epoch` writes explicit ASCII digit classes.** Python's `\d` matches every Unicode decimal digit and `int()` parses one, while the TS twin's `\d` is ASCII-only; the new regex is `[0-9]` throughout, with a non-ASCII-digit row in its test. Both twins are prefix-anchored like the shipped `epochMin`, and the linearity guard measures a hostile near-miss string as a same-run quotient.
- **D5-12 The two plan routes live beside `/at` with `Query(ge=, le=)` bounds; `/at` keeps its bare floats (schema D13).** `GET /tide/plan` refuses without an OpenWeather key before anything else (schema D6), so QA-38 is structural on both transports.
- **D5-13 A planner event whose height cannot be read (no turning point on either side and no continuous sample) shows the bracket wording after "Tide:" and no height.** The spec fixes the wording for one-sided and no-sided brackets but not for a missing height; the height is omitted rather than invented. It cannot occur on a conforming NOAA response and is exercised only by the merge test.
- **D5-14 `ACCESSIBILITY.md` is unchanged.** Re-read against the plan: the image-role rule for charts drawn as graphics covers the chart, the announced-regions sentence covers the plan's status and ready announcement, and the reflow sentence names tables and maps as things that may scroll sideways without claiming nothing else does; the chart scrolls inside its own box while the page never does. Nothing published became false.
- **D5-15 (QA-26 fix) Every day in the list carries a "Tides" line naming every high and low the chart draws on that day.** The brackets on each event name only the two turning points around it, so a high or low that falls between two consecutive events with another turning point on each side reached no list item; QA measured it on six fixture families (polar: 15 of 29 drawn points absent), and a real winter night holds about 14 hours with three semi-diurnal turning points, so it is routine rather than rare, and it left the chart's markers as the only place those points existed (US-03). The line sits under each day heading in the spec's bracket vocabulary ("Tides: high 4.7 ft (12:06 AM) · low −1.2 ft (6:31 AM) · ..."), in the location's clock, and is omitted when tide is absent or the day holds no turning point. `PlanResult.test.tsx` ("every drawn turning point is in the list, and no listed one is undrawn (QA-26)") asserts over every fixture family with tide that the multiset of listed points equals the multiset the chart draws, ties "drawn" to the rendered chart's circles on one family, and names the repeated-hour high in `dst-fall`. `design-spec.md` Content Notes and the list description carry the new string.
- **D5-16 (QA-28 fix) Within a sky reading only the glyph and its clock are unbreakable.** The first build wrapped each whole reading ("☁️ 10 PM Scattered clouds") in `white-space: nowrap`, which defeated the paragraph's `overflow-wrap: anywhere`; with a disclosure open at 320px and 200% text scale the runs overhung the region's content box by 57.73px (Chromium) / 60.73px (WebKit) and the page scrolled sideways (scrollWidth 321 / 324 on a 320 viewport). The nowrap span now holds the glyph and the clock only ("☁️ 10 PM", "⛅ from 2:30 PM") and the description is ordinary text. Measured against the built bundle behind a stub backend serving the parity fixture's reference family, with a disclosure open, text scale set as an inline `--sr-text-scale` on the root and its application verified from the computed root font size: the pre-fix dist reproduced QA's numbers exactly and the fixed dist reads scrollWidth equal to the viewport with every text run inside the content box at 320px and 375px at 200% in both engines (figures in the hand-back). The harness is a scratch script; the unit suite pins only the DOM shape the measurement rests on.
- **D5-17 (Auditor Low, closed) A builder over a provider body runs inside the route's `try`, on both transports.** A JSON-valid but semantically malformed One Call body (an absurd `dt` or `sunrise`, a non-numeric `temp`, an hourly entry carrying only `dt`) or NOAA body (a `predictions` list holding non-objects) raised out of `build_weather_plan` / `build_tide_plan` as a plain-text 500, and out of the desktop twins as a status-less RangeError/TypeError that `isOfflineError` read as connection-level, so the panel said "offline" while online. The builder calls now sit inside the same `try` as the fetch: the weather route maps the throw to the existing 502 and detail (desktop: the `{ status: 502 }` shape), and the tide route and its twin read it as `{ status: 'unavailable' }`, the same honest state an unreadable body already has. The key is never in a body either way. Measured before writing the tests: Python raises on nine of twelve probed shapes, TS on five (TS tolerates a non-numeric temp as NaN rather than throwing; stated, not fixed, since the ask was the mapping). Tests: `test_weather_plan.py::test_a_semantically_malformed_body_is_a_502_never_a_500` (seven shapes), `test_tide_plan.py::test_a_predictions_list_of_non_objects_is_unavailable_never_a_500`, and in `weatherTidePlanServices.test.ts` the describe "a semantically malformed provider body is a provider error, never offline" (three weather shapes asserting status 502 and `isOfflineError` false, two tide shapes asserting `unavailable`).
- **D5-18 (Auditor Low, closed) `composePlan` type-checks every string and number the UI prints.** The merge guarded the numeric spine but passed `local`, `date` and every `weather` field through, so a corrupted replayed half (a non-string `local`, an object in `weather.emoji` or `description`) reached React as a non-string child and threw into `RootErrorBoundary`'s whole-app "Something went wrong" until a live fetch overwrote the entry. `asWeather`, `asEvents`, `asCells`, `asDays` and `asMoment` now drop a malformed entry (or read a malformed sunrise/sunset as absent, and a malformed window string as empty), the schema section 6 posture stated as "an empty list rather than a throw". Verified by RENDERING, not by reading: `PlanResult.test.tsx` → "a corrupted replayed half never throws in the real components" feeds twenty corrupted shapes through the real `composePlan` into the real `PlanResult` and `PlanChart` under jsdom and asserts no throw plus the honest state (the plan minus the bad row, an absent moment, or no tide); `plan.test.ts` pins the merge's per-field verdicts.
- **D5-19 (Auditor informational) The bundled station ids are pinned at CI time.** `tideStations.test.ts` asserts every id in `noaa-tide-stations.json` matches `^[A-Za-z0-9]{7}$` (3,241 ids; the numeric references and the `TEC...` subordinates both present, no duplicates), so the shape the NOAA URL builders rely on is guarded where a build-time asset is guarded, under the v0.5.89 trust-boundary rule, rather than validated at runtime.
- **G-01 (Guide, Case 3 re-entry to Stage 4 after the user's live look) The design is re-entered for two pieces of feedback from the built app.** After the Tester's pass and the Auditor's pass-with-notes, the user tried the built Planner over the tailnet preview and reported that dragging the chart with the mouse "jumps around and does not smoothly scroll", and asked for a more prominent and detailed desktop view with an option to see more days at a time. Both change the design rather than the build, so the run goes back to The Designer (drag scrolling that tracks the pointer; a desktop tier where the chart is the focal point with a days-in-view control), the user reviews the revised mockup at the Designer gate, and the Engineer, Tester and Auditor run again over the revised spec before the deploy gate. The approved parts (the action, the list, the honest states, the phone layout) are not re-litigated. The two Low findings from the security review (D5-17, D5-18) were closed in parallel and stand.

## Stage 4 re-entry -- The Designer (2026-09-12, after the built app's review)

- **D4-12 No scroll snapping anywhere; a mouse drag follows the pointer one to one.** The built chart's `scroll-snap-type: x proximity` re-snapped after every programmatic `scrollLeft` write during a drag, which is the jumping the user saw. The revision removes snapping, the snap markers and `scroll-padding` on both tiers, and specifies the drag exactly: pointer capture on `pointerdown` (mouse, primary button), `scrollLeft = startLeft − (clientX − startX)` on every move with no threshold or easing, release on `pointerup` / `pointercancel` / `lostpointercapture` with no post-release motion. Post-release snapping was considered and rejected: even a proximity snap moves the chart after the user has let go, which is the complaint restated. Touch, wheel and arrow keys stay native. Day alignment moves to two explicit **Earlier day / Later day** buttons in the legend row on both tiers (one day's width, smooth, instant under reduced motion, disabled at the ends and when the track fits).
- **D4-13 A wide tier (641px and up, the app's existing phone boundary) makes the chart the focal point; the phone tier is byte-identical to the approved design.** Wide: a 22px day-header lane along the top, a 220px plot (128 on phones), hour ticks alone in the axis lane, the same strip. The Weather card grows to 1080px only while a plan is on screen, and everything else in it keeps its 476px measure, centered, through one `.sr-weather-narrow` wrapper in App.tsx and one in the panel, driven by an `onPlanVisible` callback and a class on the card (the card's inline `maxWidth: 540` is lifted to a class). Chosen over a separate wide plan card below the form because FR-04 keeps the plan in the same result region, and over widening the card always because a 1080px checklist form is wrong.
- **D4-14 Days in view: a segmented control in the house `SegControl` register, wide tier only, options 1 day · 3 days · 7 days · All {n} days, default All.** The choice sets the pixels per hour so the chosen span fills the box (`max(4, (boxWidth − 42) / hours)`); All fits the whole window with nothing to scroll and is the default because the week at a glance is the feature's purpose. Options whose day count is not below the plan's are omitted. `role="group"` + `aria-pressed` `Button`s, one tab stop each, exactly as the Calendar's controls. Not offered on phones: the phone chart stays at 16px per hour and the list is the phone's reading form.
- **D4-15 The choice persists device-local as the setting `planDaysInView`, validated on read, default `all`.** The `chartTipDismissed` precedent: a small UI preference through the storage seam, never synced. A change re-lays out instantly and keeps the instant at the box's left edge in place.
- **D4-16 Density rules are derived from pixels per hour and change only what would otherwise collide.** Below 16px/h the hourly cells collapse into one cell per 3- or 6-hour clock-aligned block showing the block's first reading, and the tag says so ("a glyph every 6 hours"); at 32px/h and up hourly cells gain the temperature; daily cells shed their description, then keep the emoji; event labels drop to the time alone below 8px/h; ticks fall back to Noon below 34px per six hours; day labels shorten by the day's visible width. The curve, the bands, the markers, the Now line and the gutter never change with density, and the tide curve is always the document's 30-minute samples.
- **D4-17 On wide plan regions (≥760px of region width) the day list flows into two CSS columns, unchanged in content.** A container query on the plan region (the `.sr-cal-minimonth` / `.sr-wx-pair` precedent), `break-inside: avoid` per day, DOM order preserved for assistive technology, and each line keeps the measure it was designed at instead of running 980px wide.
- **D4-18 No token changes.** The day-header band is `--sr-surface-subtle`; the control and buttons use existing surface, border and text tokens; the six `--sr-plan-*` tokens and their measured ratios stand.
