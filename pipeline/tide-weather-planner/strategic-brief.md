# Strategic Brief — Weather/tide Planner

## What We're Building
An expansion of the Weather tab's Predict lookup: for the place the user has already picked, one action produces a timeline from now to the end of the available forecast that shows the predicted tide curve, every sunrise and sunset in that window with the tide height and rising/falling state at each, and the provider's forecast weather along the way (hour by hour for the first two days, a labeled daily summary after). It renders as one chart plus a readable per-event list that carries the same figures.

## Why Now
Predict answers one moment. The question a birder actually has when planning a shore, estuary or mudflat visit is a pattern: which mornings in the coming week put low water at first light, and what will the weather be doing when they do. Today that pattern costs up to sixteen Predict lookups by hand (a sunrise and a sunset for each of eight days), each a fresh pair of network calls, with the linkage reconstructed in the user's head or from a tide table and a weather app held side by side. Every ingredient already ships and is already reviewed: the One Call forecast (48 hourly points, 8 daily entries, and each day's sunrise and sunset), NOAA predictions that run far past the weather horizon, the Predict place picker, the too-far/outside-US tide flow, and the honest offline and no-key states. This build composes them into the view the user has been assembling by hand, and it adds no provider, no key, and no path that touches personal data.

## The User Problem
The user described in the product brief plans outings around tide and daylight, and the app already gives them one forecast moment at a time. What it cannot show is how sunrise, tide and weather move against each other across the days they can actually get out, which is the thing a coastal birder decides on. The result is either repeated lookups or planning outside the app, with the sunrise-to-tide link done by eye and no single picture of the week.

## Success Criteria
- From the Predict place picker, one action produces the plan for that place with no date or time entered: the window is always now through the end of the last day the forecast covers.
- Every sunrise and sunset from now to the end of the window is listed with its local time, the predicted tide height and whether the tide is rising or falling at that moment, the high and low on either side with their times, and the forecast weather in force at that time. Within the first ~48 hours that weather is the nearest hourly reading; after it, that day's summary, labeled as a daily summary exactly as Predict already labels one.
- One chart shows the same window: the tide curve on a single time axis, the night hours visibly distinct from the day hours, each sunrise and sunset marked on the curve, high and low turning points marked, and the weather along the strip. A reader can see at a glance which mornings put low water at first light.
- The chart is never the only representation. The per-event list carries every figure the chart draws; it is what a screen reader receives, and it is what a phone user reads when the chart is too dense. At 320px and at 200% in-app text scale the list is fully readable, the chart stays inside its own scroll container if it needs one, and the page never scrolls sideways.
- Where the nearest NOAA station is too far or the place is outside the US, the plan still renders with sunrise, sunset and weather, says why the tide is missing, and offers the same one-tap "show the nearest station anyway" override Predict has today.
- Where there is no OpenWeather key, no network, or a provider failure, the planner says so in the same words Predict uses. A plan loaded once re-shows offline with a cue that states when it was fetched.
- One plan costs one OpenWeather call and at most two NOAA calls, never a pair of calls per event.
- Both transports (desktop TypeScript and web/Pi FastAPI) produce the same plan from identical fixtures, and every figure in the plan matches what Predict shows for the same place and moment.
- `docs/HELP.md`, `README.md` and the website describe the planner in the same words, held to the code by a published-claims guard.

## Scope
- A second action inside the existing Predict form, beside the single-moment lookup, sharing the place picker as it stands (name search, draggable pin, latitude/longitude fields). The date and time fields keep governing only the single-moment lookup.
- The plan window: from now, in the location's timezone, through the end of the last day the One Call daily forecast covers (about eight days).
- The per-event list: each sunrise and sunset in the window, with local time; tide height in feet MLLW and trend at that moment; the bracketing high and low with times; the weather at that moment using the fields Predict's summary already shows (condition, temperature, wind, humidity), with the hourly-or-daily label.
- One chart across the window: tide height line on a time axis, day/night shading between sunset and sunrise, sunrise and sunset markers, high/low markers, and a weather strip (one cell per hour for the first ~48 hours, one per day after). Built on Recharts, which the app already ships, loaded as a lazy chunk.
- Tide data for the whole window from one continuous-predictions request and one high/low request per station. Subordinate (high/low-only) stations interpolate from the high/low curve the way `lib/tide.ts` already does.
- Sunrise and sunset taken from the One Call daily entries already present in the forecast response. No additional call and no second calculation.
- The tide too-far / outside-US notice and its override, reused from Predict.
- Honest states reusing Predict's classification and copy: no key, offline, provider error, no tide station. Replay of the last plan with a staleness cue.
- Both transports: a range-shaped weather result and a range-shaped tide result (new routes or extended `/at` routes, the Architect's call), with TypeScript/Python parity tests over identical fixtures.
- Documentation in the same change: `docs/HELP.md`, `README.md`, `website/`, `CHANGELOG.md`, the version bump, a published-claims guard, and a read of `PRIVACY_POLICY.md` (no change expected).

## Out of Scope
- Extending the timeline past the weather horizon. The window ends where the forecast ends; the plan notes that tide predictions exist further out and that Predict can look up any specific later moment.
- eBird hotspot search by name as the place input. The picker already accepts a place name and a dropped pin; a hotspot-name picker is a later extension.
- Any judgement about the data: no "good" mornings, no ranking or scoring of days, no recommended windows, no thresholds, no highlighting of one event over another.
- A copy-ready block for the plan. The plan is a planning view, not a checklist annotation.
- Moon phase on the timeline. Predict's night moon glyph stays where it is.
- A tide-only plan when the weather call fails. The sunrise/sunset spine comes from the weather response, so no weather means no plan, stated honestly.
- A local astronomical sunrise/sunset calculation.
- Any One Call field the existing weather summary does not already show (precipitation probability, UV, pressure, alerts).
- Saving, naming or comparing plans; more than one place at a time; reminders or notifications.
- Changes to Current, to the checklist lookup, to the copy block, or to the Predict single-moment result.

## Key Decisions
- **Placement: an expansion inside Predict, not a fourth Weather-tab mode.** The user framed it as expanding Predict, and the reason holds on the merits: the place picker (search, pin, lat/lng, the too-far tide override) is the expensive, already-reviewed part, and a separate "Planner" button would either duplicate it or force a refactor for no user gain, while adding a third top-level button to a row that must fit at 320px. The Predict form gains a second action; the plan result replaces the single-moment result in the same result region. The Designer settles the visible label and the two actions' relative weight.
- **Naming.** The user calls it the "Weather/tide Planner"; published prose (`docs/HELP.md`, `README.md`, `website/`) uses one formulation for it and never a component or file name. The on-screen action label is the Designer's to settle; the help section describing it sits under Current and Predict.
- **The window is the weather horizon, and it starts now.** From the current hour in the location's timezone through the end of the last One Call daily entry, about eight days. Weather resolution changes at ~48 hours, and the plan shows that change rather than hiding it: hourly cells then daily cells, labeled as Predict already labels a daily summary. Tide is drawn to the same end. Past the window nothing is drawn in v1; the plan states that tide predictions exist further out. Only sunrises and sunsets still ahead of now are listed; today's already-passed events are not.
- **"Tide at sunrise and sunset" means, concretely:** for each event, the predicted water height (ft MLLW) at the nearest NOAA station at that time, whether the tide is rising or falling then, and the nearest high and low on either side with their times. Height comes from the station's continuous predictions where it has them and from interpolation on the high/low curve where it does not, the same rule `lib/tide.ts` already applies.
- **Place input is the existing Predict picker.** Nominatim name search plus a draggable pin plus coordinate fields already cover "a particular hotspot" for anyone who knows where it is. eBird hotspot search by name is a real later extension, deferred so this build adds no eBird call and no new gated path.
- **The chart is Recharts, lazy-loaded, and never the only representation.** Recharts already ships (Statistics, Multimedia stats, Projects, Species Detail); reuse it. `WeatherForecastPanel` is a static import in `App.tsx` and `entryChunk.test.ts` asserts no chart library is reachable from that graph, so the chart component ships as its own lazy chunk exactly as `PredictMap` does. The per-event list is the accessible form and the phone form; the chart is decorative-plus, with a `role="img"` name that says what it shows, following `SightingsGraph`. All colors through `var(--sr-*)` tokens in both themes; the day/night shading and the tide line need tokens that clear contrast in both, checked the way the calendar and county ramps are. The Designer decides density and whether the chart scrolls horizontally on phones and whether it mounts the existing `ChartViewTip`.
- **No inference, no ranking, no "good birding weather."** The Statistics section's "nothing is predicted and nothing is ranked" stance is about not extrapolating from the user's own records. This planner presents the provider's forecast and NOAA's astronomical predictions, each labeled as such, and infers nothing from them: it does not choose a best morning, does not score days, and does not color any event as better than another. The pattern is the user's to read.
- **No new outbound destination, no privacy-policy change expected.** `PRIVACY_POLICY.md` already discloses OpenWeather for "the current and forecast weather for a location and time you choose" and NOAA for "the current or predicted tide for a location and time you choose." The plan sends the same coordinates to the same two providers for a window instead of a moment. Sunrise and sunset come from the One Call daily entries already in the response, so no third source and no local calculation. The Planner confirms those two sentences still read true for a window; if the wording needs "time or period," that is a one-clause edit in the same change.
- **One time axis, the location's.** Sunrise, sunset and weather arrive as epoch seconds with the location's timezone name; NOAA's `lst_ldt` clock is the station's. For a nearby station they agree; for a forced far-station override they can differ, and an eight-day window can cross a DST change. Every series is placed on one unambiguous axis before drawing, and the axis is rendered in the location's timezone. Requesting NOAA in `time_zone=gmt` for the range calls is the obvious route; the Architect decides, and the parity fixtures include a DST-crossing window.
- **Request budget: one OpenWeather call and at most two NOAA calls per plan.** The forecast response already holds the whole window; the tide window is fetched once per station as one continuous-predictions request and one high/low request, both well inside NOAA's range limits for an eight-day span. No per-event calls, no per-day calls.
- **Failure and offline posture: stated facts, never errors dressed as failures of the app.** A missing tide (too far, outside the US, station unavailable) does not block the plan; sunrise, sunset and weather still render, with the notice and the override. A missing weather response does block it, because the day spine comes from it, and the plan says which of no key, offline, or provider error applied, in Predict's existing words. A plan fetched once replays offline with a cue naming when it was fetched, since a plan anchored at "now" is stale by construction once replayed.
- **Both transports are twins.** The desktop TypeScript path and the FastAPI path each produce the plan, with parity tests over identical fixtures, the posture `forecastSlice.ts` and `forecast.py` already hold. Any new route is declared before the `{checklist_id}` routes and matched before the `/weather/` and `/tide/` prefixes in `transport.ts`, the trap `/at` already documents. The Vite proxy already forwards `/weather` and `/tide`.
- **Alignment verdict: aligned.** Same user as the product brief. Predict (v0.5.34) already established that a forward-looking, zero-personal-data lookup on the Weather tab is inside the product's purpose, and this is the same category one step further. Nothing in the founding Out of Scope list is touched: no account, no server, no data leaving the device beyond coordinates to two already-disclosed providers. It moves toward the origin (weather and tide for birding) rather than away. The one tension worth naming is that the product brief frames SnowRaven around the user's own eBird and Macaulay data and the planner uses none of it; Current and Predict already sit on that same ground, so it is a known and accepted position rather than new drift.
- **Documentation is part of the build, and it is guarded.** The feature adds sentences to `docs/HELP.md`, `README.md` and `website/`, so it owes a published-claims test of the `weatherStatsPublishedClaims.test.ts` shape in the same change, scoped to each file's own passage, asserting the claim exists before checking it, and comparing the three files against each other.
