Features section: proposed structure

Revised: ten sections, the ten tabs, in the app's own DEFAULT_TAB_ORDER. Search and Settings are dropped as more functional than a unique birding feature, and Offline support is retired. All three articles go with their figures. Every remaining section has a figure and none is orphaned; nothing in website/assets/ becomes unused, because both retired figures were hand-built HTML rather than files. The alternation is now a clean parity: odd rows media right, even rows reverse, nine hairlines across ten rows.

Every paragraph below is a stand-in, set in italics. The words are not the deliverable here; each one names what that section will describe, at roughly the length the real paragraph will run, so the row heights and the alternation read truthfully. The screenshots, the three mocks, the icons, the dividers and the alternation are real.

One piece of markup here must not be copied. Every stand-in is <p class="stand-in"> so it can be set in italics. In the real file every body paragraph in a feature row opens as a bare <p>, with no attributes: three published-claims guards locate their passage with lastIndexOf('<p>', i), and a classed paragraph makes that search skip back to an earlier one and silently widen what it extracts. The only classed paragraph in the shipped section is .feature-tag, last in its row.

What's inside

A heading that states a property, not a count

One line on where everything is read from, replacing the shipped sub. No count, and nothing that names the Planner: the Planner passage is located by the first occurrence of its name in the file.

Weather

Paragraph one, the tab's own actions: the checklist lookup and the summary it hands back, Current for where you are now, Plan for a place and time you choose, and the backlog of checklists that still have no weather block. Free to rewrite from scratch.

Paragraph two, the Weather/tide Planner. This is the one paragraph on the page whose sentences are decided before the copy pass starts: five of them are byte-pinned by a guard, so the split exists to put all of them here and leave paragraph one free. It runs longer than this stand-in.

Statistics

Paragraph one, the dashboard itself: what it is built from, what it counts, and the two count rules in the tab header that follow eBird's own rules for what counts.

Paragraph two, the two sections that reach past the tab: the Geographic Stats map with its county shading, and Projects, the answer the app has to go and fetch.

Paragraph three, the Weather section that reads your own weather blocks back. Guard-anchored on its opening phrase, with a 200-character floor, so it keeps its anchor and its length. The Species Detail card paragraph that sits here today moves to row 4.

Light or dark, your choice. Dark mode shown.

Map Explorer

Paragraph one, the map: your sightings, the filters and overlays over them, the hotspot and county colourings, and how a search is something you ask for rather than something that happens.

Paragraph two, maps that fill the window. Stays in this section deliberately: it is a property of any map in the app, and this is where a reader meets maps.

Paragraph three, planting a pin to share a spot. Same reason, framed as any birding map rather than as a Map Explorer feature.

Species Detail

Paragraph one, your whole history with one bird in one view, and the three sections inside it: subspecies and forms, the county shading, and the recent media it plays inline.

Paragraph two, the relocated Weather card paragraph. It describes a card on this tab and now sits in this tab's section. Guard-anchored on its own phrase, with a 200-character floor; its attribution no longer comes from the paragraph above it, which is a guard comment to re-argue.

Calendar

A year of your birding as month grids, what each day carries, and the ways to page, filter, fold the years together and open a day. One paragraph, and the per-feature reassurance at the end of it goes.

Multimedia

Your life list as a media checklist: what you have photographed, recorded and filmed, what you still have not, and the filters that narrow it.

Breeding Codes

Where your breeding evidence stands, as a matrix across every eBird code, with the filters and the legend that make it readable.

Checklists

Your checklists as whole outings: searching every comment you have written, and the filters that combine has and does-not-have across a whole list.

List Comparer

Two lists, or two checklists, side by side: what they share, what only one of them has, and how much detail comes along for the ride.

Named Birds

Naming an individual bird in your own comment, and everything the app then gathers about it: the dates, the places, the timelines, the media. Heading pinned byte-exact by a guard, and already the right name.
