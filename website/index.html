Birding analytics and tools for your eBird workflow

SnowRaven takes your eBird and Macaulay Library records and lets you explore them in new ways. Weather and tides for your checklists, deep per-species history, life-list analytics, media coverage, breeding records, and an interactive map. It uses your own exported data, on your own device, so you stay in control.

Privacy by design

Your data stays on your device

SnowRaven has no account, no analytics, no telemetry, and no server we operate. Your eBird backup, Macaulay Library export, settings, and API keys never leave your computer. When you ask for data, it talks directly to the services it draws from: eBird and OpenWeather with your own keys, plus keyless sources (OpenStreetMap, map tiles, NOAA tides, and the Cornell Lab sites that bird-link icons and, while enabled, embedded Macaulay media load from). There is no SnowRaven server in the middle. An off-by-default Disable embedded media setting prevents those players and requests; direct media links still work when you choose to open them.

What's inside

Ten tools, one quiet workspace. And they work offline

Everything reads from the eBird and Macaulay Library exports you already have. No data entry, no busywork. Once your data has loaded, the tools keep working without a connection.

Weather & Tide Lookup

Paste an eBird checklist ID and get a clean, ready-to-paste weather summary: temperature, wind, humidity, dew point, sunrise and sunset, and the moon phase on night checklists, plus the historical tide from the nearest NOAA station. Current shows the live weather and tide for where you are, Predict forecasts both for a place and time you choose, and a backlog lists your recent checklists that still have no weather block, so you can work down the ones you missed.

Species Detail

Your complete history with any species: sighting stats, breeding codes, field notes, top locations, sightings-over-time graphs, and a map of every observation, all in one view. A Subspecies Explorer breaks your reports down by subspecies and form, a Counties switch shades every US county where you have recorded the bird (offline, from your own file, no API key), and a Recent Media section plays your latest photo, audio, and video of it inline from the Macaulay Library, showing plain links instead when an embed can't load.

Statistics

A full analytics dashboard built from your own data: life-list totals and growth, firsts and milestones, temporal and geographic patterns, effort, data quality, breeding stats, deep media statistics, and a few playful Frivolous Lists. Two off-by-default count rules in the tab header follow eBird's own rules for what counts: Count all forms decides whether forms that leave the species in doubt (a spuh, a slash, a hybrid) are counted, and Count escapees follows eBird's life-list rule for exotics (Naturalized and Provisional count, Escapee does not), naming every bird it leaves out. Both are per-session, resetting on relaunch.

The Geographic Stats map carries the same Counties overlay, shaded by species or by checklists, with numbers that match the county tables beside it, and the Map Explorer's too while Count all forms is off (the Map Explorer always applies the countable-species rule). A Projects section reports which eBird projects your checklists were submitted to: nothing is sent until you press Check projects, the section states the exact cost first, it can be stopped and resumed, and every tally shows how many checklists it was drawn from.

Light or dark, your choice. Dark mode shown.

Calendar

A year of your birding as twelve month grids, each day carrying the species, checklists, or total count you recorded and shaded by how busy it was, so the shape of your year reads at a glance. Filter to a single species, page across every year or fold them all into one combined grid, switch to a whole-year overview of thumbnails, turn on a colorblind-friendly texture mode, and click any day for its checklists. Entirely offline, from your already-loaded backup.

Map Explorer

An interactive map of your sightings, with a heatmap and filters for species, date range, county, breeding code, and media, plus nearby eBird hotspots, a Nearby Lifers view mapping where species you haven't recorded were reported recently, Media Targets, and a California Breeding Bird Atlas overlay. Hotspot pins can be colored by whether you've visited them, or, opt-in, by your own species or checklist counts there or by the community's recent activity, with an optional tier-ring mode so the reading never depends on color alone. County lines can shade each county by your own species or checklist counts, offline from your backup, or by how complete your county list is against everything ever reported to eBird there (that one metric needs a connection and your own key), with a colorblind-friendly texture mode. Search a place name, use your location, drop a pin, or press Search this area after moving the map; nothing is sent until you press. Street, satellite, and topo basemaps, plus a hiking-trails layer.

Any birding map also shares a spot: right-click (long-press on touch) to plant a share pin at the exact point you pressed, drag it to fine-tune, and copy the coordinates plus Google Maps and Apple Maps links, ready to paste into a message. Settings chooses which of the three lines you send, a corner button drives the whole flow from the keyboard, and it is all assembled locally as plain text, so it works offline and no coordinate leaves your device.

Multimedia

Your life list as a media checklist: photo, audio, and video coverage for every species, so you can see at a glance what you still need to capture. Filter by sex and age (pull up just your juveniles, or the males of a dimorphic species), with counts and links straight to the matching media in your Macaulay Library.

Breeding Codes

Every species you've recorded a breeding code for, laid out as a color-coded matrix across all eBird codes, with tier and per-code filters to see exactly where your breeding evidence stands. A legend below the table spells out what each code means, and on a phone the columns tighten to dot-width, with the species-name column staying fixed on the left in the normal view.

Named Birds

Give an individual bird a name in your eBird species comment with a [name:…] tag (like [name:Winky]), and SnowRaven gathers every sighting of that individual: its species, when you first and last saw it, how long you've followed it, each checklist with its location, a map of everywhere that bird has been seen, and its own top locations. With your Macaulay Library export saved, each individual also gathers its own photos, audio, and video as inline players, matched by the same tag. Prefer no inline players anywhere? Turn on Disable embedded media in Settings; direct Macaulay Library links stay available.

Checklists

Your checklists as whole outings. Search every checklist comment and every species comment you've ever written, and browse a filterable list of all your checklists: combine has and doesn't-have filters for comments, media, breeding codes, completeness, protocol, county, and dates. Pasted weather and tide blocks stay hidden (and out of search) until you ask for them.

List Comparer

Compare two life lists to see shared and unique species, or compare two individual checklists side by side: counts with the higher total emphasized, breeding codes, photo, audio, and video indicators, effort details, checklist and species comments, and side-by-side weather and tide for each checklist.

Offline support

Out in the field with no signal, SnowRaven keeps working from the data already on your device. The maps and every analytical tab open offline once they've loaded online once, and a checklist's weather and tide you looked up earlier re-show, marked as the last loaded result.

It is honest about the edges: live lookups (current weather and tide, place search, the Checklist Comparer, nearby-bird overlays, and app updates) still need a connection, and only the vector street basemap works offline.

Runs where you do

One app, everywhere you bird

Install the native desktop app on a Mac or Windows PC, or self-host it on a Raspberry Pi (or any computer on your network) and open it from any browser. One universal Mac build runs on both Apple Silicon and Intel, and the whole interface is responsive, so it works on a phone or tablet too.

  • macOS: universal .dmg
  • Windows: .exe installer
  • Raspberry Pi / Linux: self-host

Get started

Up and running in a few minutes

Mac

Download the universal .dmg from the latest release, drag SnowRaven to Applications, then right-click the app and choose Open on first launch.

Download for Mac

Windows

Download the .exe installer and run it. SmartScreen may warn about an unknown publisher, so choose More info, then Run anyway. In-app updates are cryptographically verified.

Download for Windows

Raspberry Pi / Linux

Run one command on your Pi (or any Debian or Ubuntu machine). It handles packages, the build, the Python environment, and an optional auto-start service.

$ curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dtgibson/snowraven/main/install.sh | bash

What you'll need

Two free API keys, entered once in Settings:

  • eBird API key, free from ebird.org/api/keygen. Powers weather lookups, hotspot and nearby-sighting data, and taxonomy and region lookups.
  • OpenWeather API key, free from openweathermap.org, subscribed to the One Call by Call plan.

Most analytics also use your own eBird backup (a free export of your data) and an optional Macaulay Library export. They stay on your device. On a Mac, iPhone or iPad, an optional iCloud Sync switch in Settings (off by default) keeps those two files the same across your own Apple devices through your own iCloud account, so an export uploaded once is used everywhere; nothing else is synced.

Already installed? Desktop apps update from Check For Updates in the footer; self-hosted installs run ./update.sh.

A personal project, shared freely

SnowRaven began as a tool for one birder's own data, and it's shared here, open source and free, in case other birders find it useful. It works alongside eBird and the Macaulay Library, building on the exports they let you download. There's nothing to sign up for, and nothing to buy. The app says its own thank-you, too: an Acknowledgments section at the bottom of Settings credits the Cornell Lab of Ornithology and the Macaulay Library, and Deven Simonson.
